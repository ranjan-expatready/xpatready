SA-1B: what it is, who needs it, and when to do it

What SA-1B contains (the Canada rules builder · reviewed 22 Sep · 28 decisions)

The library, written onceEvery question a client can be asked, tagged for lead, prospect or case; groups of questions (address, job, education); reusable sections; the master list of documents with accepted substitutes and expiry rules; the list of government forms (automatic filling parked); eligibility rules and points; reference tables; government steps; the regulator's required wording.
The builderOne row for each exact application type (who the client is › program › application › variant), holding its ordered list of questions, documents, forms and steps, each saying who it applies to and when.
Applications and routesA case can hold several applications: side by side for a family's separate permits, one after another for two- or three-step provincial routes.
People and rolesA family is one case with per-person questionnaire, checklist, forms, fee and decision.
Prospect questions in three tiersA short lead form, a prospect questionnaire and the full case questionnaire; each one pre-fills the next.
Assessment result, readiness checks, firm questionsVerdict, reason and gap with "rules as at" a date; a program can be opened only when its row is complete; firms may add their own questions but never change a required one.

Who depends on it: AG-3 (assessment), AG-7 (cases), AG-8 (questionnaire), AG-9 (checklist), and in small ways AG-2 (lead form) and AG-5/6 (fee wording). Still open: three additions the LMIA module needs (steps before filing, to-do lines, clocks read from tables) are not yet in the file; which government forms ship; the session that decides the Canada content for launch has no date or owner. Not the same thing as the Practice Pack (the firm's own regulator, trust-money, fee and retention rules), which has no module file at all and which the Charter wrongly calls "SA-1-B".

Three ways to time it

Before all AG

  • Nothing is reviewed twice
  • Clean dependency story
  • Delays AG-01, AG-02, AG-18, which do not need it and are in the October build
  • About a week of calendar lost for no gain on 12 modules
RECOMMENDED

Now, in parallel with Wave 1; closed before Wave 3

  • AG-3/7/8/9 are reviewed once, against a settled model
  • Keeps the Charter's October build and 28 Oct dates
  • Serves the 13 Nov "first Canada rules loaded for testing" date and the 11 Dec "first client end to end" gate
  • Removes what the 5 Oct scope review called the "bigger risk than any feature"
  • Two tracks at once: Tanu and Sanjay on SA-1B content, the AG reviews beside it

After AG

  • None that hold: AG work does not make SA-1B easier
  • AG-3/7/8/9 written against a moving target, then rewritten
  • The 13 Nov date for the first Canada rules slips; no firm can get going until its country's rules are published
  • The team paused SA-01 "waiting on 1-B" on 18 Sep; testing is on hold

The remaining SA-1B requirements work is small: one revision (add the LMIA additions, settle "which forms") plus one session on the Canada content for launch. Building the rules-builder screens is the long job, and it starts from that revision.

What sets the pace (it is not the writing)

  1. Decision time. One 60–90 minute session per module with Sanjay (Tanu where it is content). One session a day = 16 modules in about three and a half weeks. Two sessions a day, or Sanjay and Tanu running sessions in parallel, halves it again.
  2. Three founder decisions that eleven modules are waiting on: the scope review, AG-11 in or out, and where the Practice Pack lives. Taken in Wave 0, they stop reopening work later.
  3. Outside inputs. Immigration rule values for AG-03, AG-17 and AG-18 from the immigration expert, and the Canada content session for SA-1B. These run alongside; they do not wait for the AG order.
