# 1. The choice

**We propose that the licensed founder-consultant sells Expat Ready personally to owners of small immigration-consultant practices.** Before launch, they test whether practices will commit as *founding firms* at a discounted price. In March 2027 those firms go live with hands-on onboarding, helped part-time. Selling stays founder-led until real results show what to add.

**Why this route.**

- **It is the cheapest way to learn** whether the product sells, and at what price. It uses the founder-consultant's relationships with consultants (the founder, 7 Oct) and the small-practice focus already ratified in FD-19.
- **Two hypotheses, still to be tested:** owners of small practices decide without long approval chains, and they trust a licensed peer more than a sales team.

**What would change course:** too few founding orders, losing mostly on price, or slow onboarding (§7).

**Numbers:**

- Every number lives in [`model.xlsx`](model.xlsx): change the *Inputs* sheet, read the *Summary* sheet.
- Figures here use the model's default inputs on 8 October 2026. If they differ from the model, the model wins.

# 2. The first buyer, and why they would switch

**First buyer:** the owner of a 1–5 person practice licensed by the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants (CICC). English-first, based in Ontario, BC or Alberta, mostly handling Express Entry and provincial nominee files.

**What we know about this market:**

- **Fact:** about 12,500 licensed consultants and student advisers were in good standing in June 2025. 83% of consultants work in those three provinces.
- **Unknown:** how many *practices* there are. If a practice averages 1.5–3 consultants, that is about 3,900–7,800.
- **Unverified:** the founder mentioned an audience of about 25,000, perhaps 10,000. How many owners the founders can reach is unknown.
- **No customer evidence supplied:** the project files hold no record of customer conversations, pilots, commitments or revenue.

**What should persuade an owner to switch** (the problem side is a hypothesis to test):

- **One client record from first enquiry to filed case.** This should end re-typing between intake, assessment, retainer, forms and invoices.
- **Assessments the consultant can explain and defend.** Licensed practitioners write the rules, every result shows its reasons, and every step is recorded. This helps with the College record-keeping duties added in July 2026. AI does not decide (FD-16).
- **A hands-on start.** We import client records and train staff, aiming for a first live case in about two weeks.

**Why they might not switch:**

| Objection | Honest answer |
|----|--------|
| Officio or CaseEasy is cheaper and fills more forms | True (Officio lists 400+ forms). We fill only tested forms and compete on the connected record and explainable assessments, not on price or form count |
| CAPIC gives me IMMeFile free | Its published features omit government forms, client-account accounting and e-signature. Let the owner compare |
| Moving mid-case is risky | At launch only client records are imported (GAP-17). New matters start with us; old ones finish where they are |
| Is client data safe with a new company? | We claim only what is proven in production. No real client data goes into pilots before the safeguards in §6 |

# 4. Getting the first customers, and what we will learn

| When | Founder-consultant does | We learn | Change course if |
|---|------|----|----|
| From late Oct 2026, after go-ahead | Problem conversations with warm contacts: tools, monthly spend, last switch | Is the problem real and urgent? | Fewer than 8 conversations by mid-Nov |
| Mid-Nov 2026 – Feb 2027 | Founding offer (after counsel review); prototype sessions labelled "simulator — not the product" | Will owners commit at this price? | Fewer than 5 orders by 31 Jan, or most lost on price |
| Jan – Feb 2027 | 3–5 pilot firms on the real product, once safeguards are evidenced | Does onboarding take about two weeks? | Much longer than that |
| Mar 2027 onward | Founding firms go live. Selling continues: network, CAPIC as a member, the NCIC conference in May | Weekly use, close rate, reasons for losing | Low use: fix the product before selling harder |
| About Sep 2027 | Peer-referral test (below) | Will customers recommend us to peers? | Almost no introductions after two quarters |

**The customer numbers are a planning experiment, not a growth promise.**

- **Before launch:** the model assumes 3–4 qualified conversations a week and 1 in 5 signing a founding order. That gives about 12 founding firms at launch.
- **After launch:** about 5 conversations a week and a little over 1 in 5 buying, so 4–5 new firms a month. That is roughly 15 selling hours a week.
- **When reality differs:** note what actually happens in the *observed* column on the Inputs sheet. These are notes, not actuals; the model does not use them. Changing an assumption recalculates the whole forecast from the first month, not just the months ahead. Save a dated copy first if you want to compare.

**Outreach:**

- Contact warm contacts first.
- Use published business addresses only if the message fits the person's role and they have not refused unsolicited messages.
- Do not scrape the CICC register or law-society directories.
- Always identify ourselves and offer an unsubscribe.
- Counsel confirms these rules before outreach starts.

**Peer-referral test:**

- **When:** once about 10 customers are live and happy, and counsel has reviewed the terms.
- **Who refers:** licensed practitioners introduce *another practice*, never a client. Rewarding a recommendation to a client breaches CICC Code s.13.
- **Reward:** account credit once the new firm has paid for two months. The referrer discloses it.
- **Our first message:** it meets the anti-spam (CASL) referral conditions.
- **Not yet:** affiliates and resellers wait.

# 5. People, spending and funding

**Who does what:** the founder-consultant sells and leads onboarding; the product team builds and runs the product; the co-founder and an accountant handle finance; counsel reviews terms, privacy, the offer and outreach; a named licensed owner keeps immigration content current. **Support at launch:** business hours, in-app and email, with response targets (not a contract until measured for three months) of 1 business hour for an outage or a data problem, 4 hours for a broken core function, 1 business day for how-to questions.

**Help is chosen by workload, not by a rule.**

- **Default help:** the model assumes 20 founder hours a week plus about 15 hours a week of part-time onboarding and support help from launch (about CAD 2,600 a month).
- **How long that lasts:** the model's *Workload* rows show it covers the work until about spring 2028, at around 70 live firms.
- **After that, three options:**
    - more founder hours, which is cheapest but depends on the founder-consultant's own practice;
    - more part-time help, which is flexible but has less continuity;
    - a full-time specialist or generalist (around CAD 6,900 a month all-in). A generalist can also run demos and follow-up. It is the biggest fixed cost after the product team.

**Spending:**

- **Kept, because operating safely needs it:**
    - legal set-up before any offer or outreach (estimate pending 2–3 fixed-scope quotes);
    - removing unsupported website claims (cost unknown; mostly deleting text);
    - accountant and bookkeeping;
    - cyber and professional insurance before real client data;
    - a help desk and status page from the pilots;
    - card and hosting costs per firm.
- **NCIC conference attendance in May 2027:** included, as one inexpensive chance to meet many consultants.
- **Off unless chosen:** a content or marketing contractor, monthly events and memberships, a professional demo video, and a fixed-fee fundraising adviser.
- **Untouched:** product-team commitments, which cost far more than everything else.

**Cash.** the founder reported INR 2 crore of capital provided by the co-founder, about CAD 295,000. The model uses it as an unverified opening-cash assumption; it is not proven current cash. **The product-team cost is unknown;** the model uses a placeholder of CAD 25,000 a month.

| Model defaults, 8 Oct 2026, no outside money | |
|--------|---|
| Cash first falls below the CAD 50,000 safety line | July 2027 |
| Outside money, cost cuts or founder money needed by | June 2027 |
| **First milestone:** 12 months of trading (March 2028), plus 6 months to raise the next round | to September 2028 |
| Outside money needed to get there, keeping the safety line | about CAD 0.29 million, plus round costs |
| At the milestone: live firms / revenue as a share of costs | about 68 / about 30% |

- **Why this milestone:** by March 2028 there will be a year of real sales, onboarding times, cancellations and the first annual renewals. That is the evidence for the next decision. It is not a three-year survival figure.
- **The biggest swing is the product-team cost:** each CAD 10,000 a month changes the amount needed by about CAD 240,000.

**Funding choices:**

| Choice | Fits if |
|------|------|
| **A. Founders only:** founder money and any product-cost reduction | The actual product cost is well below the placeholder, or the founders can add money |
| **B. Small outside round:** angels or pre-seed funds, closing before mid-2027 | The founders want to keep the product team at full strength. Conversations can start now with an honest account of what is and is not known; existing customers are not required |
| **C. Founders bridge, then a round:** raise once founding orders and pilots exist | Investors want that evidence first |

- **Grants and tax credits are left out.** SR&ED and IRAP stay out of the model until an accountant confirms eligibility; work outside Canada mostly does not qualify.
- **Annual prepayments are already in the forecast.** They are counted in collections and help only a little.
- **Fundraising help:** for now, the founders raise themselves or use fixed-fee help. Before engaging any introducer, finder or dealer, counsel checks their registration or exemption and how they are paid. A fixed fee does not by itself remove securities-law obligations.
- **Recommendation:**
    - First get the actual product cost and a dated bank balance.
    - If the cost is near the placeholder, start investor conversations in November alongside the customer conversations.
    - Choose between B and C once the founding orders are counted.

**If the founders choose to raise:**

- **Pitch:** turn this plan into 8–10 slides: buyer and problem; product and demo; differentiation and market; evidence so far; sales model; team; numbers; the ask, the use of money and the milestone.
- **To send with it:** this workbook, plus a private folder holding the cap table, incorporation records, IP assignments and key contracts.
- **Investor contacts:** record each contact and the agreed next step.
- **Monthly updates:** send interested investors a brief progress update each month, if they agree to receive one.
- **Not built yet:** no deck or tracker is made until the founders decide to raise.

# 6. Safeguards that stay

- **No real client data in pilots** until production has tenant isolation, permissions, multi-factor sign-in, tested backups, monitoring, a breach register, a support procedure and signed pilot terms (GAP-05/07/08/13/14).
- **Claims match what ships:**
    - forms only where named and tested;
    - migration means "client records";
    - no uptime, recovery or savings figures until measured.
- **Immigration rules** each carry a review date and a named licensed owner.
- **Counsel** reviews the terms, privacy, offer, referral rules and outreach.
- **An accountant** reviews tax, credits and grants.

# 7. The strongest objection, and what would change the plan

*"The market is crowded with free and cheap tools. At a founder-led pace, income covers only about a third of costs after a year of trading. The product cost isn't even known. This is a funding plan without customer evidence."*

It is a fair objection. That is why the plan:

- spends little until the founding-order test reports;
- sizes funding to a learning milestone;
- keeps help and discretionary spending as explicit choices.

**What would change it:**

- **Weak orders or price losses:** change price, segment or positioning before raising, and prefer cost cuts.
- **A much lower product cost:** choice A may be enough.
- **Strong commitments and real referrals:** raise more, sooner, and add help earlier.
- **Slow onboarding:** more help and less selling.
