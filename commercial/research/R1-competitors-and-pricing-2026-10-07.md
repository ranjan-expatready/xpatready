# R1 — Competitors, published prices and Expat Ready's price claims

**Date:** 7 Oct 2026. **Writer:** research agent (Claude). **Status:** research input only. Nothing here is a decision, a price change or a website change. Every fact below was read on the vendor's own page on 7 Oct 2026 (see Sources). Nobody was contacted; no form, trial or account was started; nothing was downloaded.
**Scope:** (1) practice and case-management products a Canadian immigration practice can buy today; (2) a line-by-line check of the prices on expatready.com; (3) which Expat Ready claims are uncommon and which competitors already offer.
**Not redone:** the 2 Oct comparison of how competitors keep government forms up to date.

**In plain words:**
- *Per seat* means the price is charged for each person who logs in. *Per firm* means one price covers a set number of people.
- *IRCC form auto-fill* means the software copies client data into the government PDF forms (IMM forms). *Portal auto-fill* means it also types the data into IRCC's online portals, usually through a browser add-on.
- *Trust accounting* means keeping client money (retainers paid in advance) in a separate ledger and reconciling it, as regulators require.
- *n.p.* = not published on the vendor's pages that we read. *n.f.* = not found on the pages read (it may exist elsewhere).

**Two cautions about how the pages were read.**
1. Our connection was located outside Canada (India; the browser pane exited through France). Some vendors change the currency by visitor location. Google Workspace showed Indian rupees, so its Canadian price could not be read. Calendly and Toggl show "$" without naming the currency.
2. Officio refused plain requests (HTTP 403) and Clio showed a bot check. Both were read in a real browser instead. Officio's prices are filled in by the page's script; they were read from the loaded page.

## 1. Summary

- The Canadian market is **crowded and cheap**. We verified 18 products a Canadian practice can buy; 12 are built specifically for Canadian immigration. Most Canadian-focused tools price **per firm, not per seat**, at **CAD 29–199 a month** for a solo or small firm.
- One of them is **already "free" to many RCICs**. CAPIC's IMMeFile case-management tool is included in CAPIC Registered and Associate membership, at no extra cost. Membership is $240–670 a year. CAPIC says IMMeFile has been in service since 2019.
- **Associations are already a sales channel for rivals.** CILA's sponsor page lists CaseEasy, Mitratech INSZoom and Officio. CosmoLex, a law-practice tool, calls itself "Preferred by the CBA".
- At **CAD 199 per seat per month (annual)**, Expat Ready's Practice tier is at the **top** of this range for one user and **2.5–7 times** most Canadian alternatives for a 3-person firm (§3.1).
- The website's **$482 "7-tool baseline" cannot be supported as published.** The two largest lines (Clio Manage $215 and Clio Grow $175, together 81% of the total) are not published by Clio today. Clio Canada publishes only "Starting at $69/user". Most of the other lines are in US dollars, billed yearly, or priced per firm rather than per seat.
- The website's comparison table names the wrong rivals. "Tracker" cannot be identified as a current Canadian product, and the MigrateCRM site was down. The table leaves out the Canadian-native tools buyers actually compare: Officio, Visto, VisaFlo and CaseEasy.
- **Client portal, IRCC form auto-fill, trust accounting, French, Canadian hosting, unlimited cases and data migration are all already offered.** What looks uncommon in published wording: a deterministic, practitioner-written rules engine with a replayable audit log; a written 4-business-hour support promise; and "no per-form fees" set against competitors that cap forms or applications.

## 6. What this means for Expat Ready

These observations depend on the evidence above. None of them is a decision.

1. **If the website keeps the $482 baseline,** it rests on two Clio prices that Clio does not publish. Clio's own page shows CAD 69 as the entry price. A prospect who checks clio.com/ca/pricing would see a conflict. The same applies to the comparison-table figure "$215+".
2. **If the beginning segment is solo and 1–5 person RCIC practices,** most of their realistic alternatives are Canadian per-firm tools at CAD 29–237 a month for three users. Clio is mainly a law-firm tool. A per-seat price of CAD 199 would then need a value story beyond portal, forms, trust, French and Canadian hosting, because rivals already publish all of these.
3. **If differentiation is to rest on published evidence,** the candidates that survive this check are: deterministic, practitioner-written rules with a replayable audit and submitted-form locking; a written 4-business-hour support commitment; no form or application caps; and a non-AI-first stance. None is yet proven with customers.
4. **If a referral or partner programme is planned,** the visible market reference is a one-off reward of about $250 per firm (Clio and Cerenade), plus a discount or free month for the new customer. Commission rates for channel partners are not published by anyone checked.
5. **If competitors are named on the site,** the evidence suggests buyers compare Officio, Visto, VisaFlo and CaseEasy. Tracker and MigrateCRM could not be verified as current products.
6. **A free tool bundled with association membership affects pricing and switching for solo and small RCICs.** CAPIC members already get IMMeFile at no extra cost inside a $240–670 annual fee they may pay anyway for continuing education and community. For them, Expat Ready's comparison point may be "free and already set up", not CAD 49–199 a month. Leaving IMMeFile costs them nothing in fees, so switching is cheap on paper. But the effort of moving files remains, and the reason to pay must exceed what membership already gives. IMMeFile's published features are narrower: no IRCC form auto-fill, trust accounting or e-signature were stated. That gap is where a paid product would have to win.
7. **If associations are a planned channel,** rivals already hold visible positions: CaseEasy, INSZoom and Officio sponsor CILA; CosmoLex claims CBA preference; CAPIC promotes its own tool. Any CAPIC partnership would sit next to, or compete with, CAPIC's own product.
