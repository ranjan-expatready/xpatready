# R2 — Market size and channel directory (Canadian immigration practices)

**Date:** 7 Oct 2026. **Writer:** research agent (Claude). **Status:** research input only. Nothing here is a decision, a contact or a commitment. Every fact was checked on 7 Oct 2026; the Sources table at the end gives the URL and any date shown on the page.
**Rules followed:** primary sources first; where only a secondary source carries a figure, the row says so. Nobody was contacted, no form was submitted, no group was joined, no account was created.

**In plain words:**
- *Licensee* means a person licensed by the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants (CICC, "the College"). *RCIC* = Regulated Canadian Immigration Consultant; *RCIC-IRB* = an RCIC also allowed to appear before the Immigration and Refugee Board (licence class L3); *RISIA* = Regulated International Student Immigration Advisor (works in schools; cannot represent clients).
- *In good standing* means licensed and allowed to practise.
- *CPD* (continuing professional development) means the 16 hours of training each RCIC must complete every year (1 July–30 June) to keep the licence.
- *Primary source* means the organisation that owns the number published it. *Secondary* means someone else reports it, citing the owner.

**What the trend says.** Licensee numbers roughly doubled from 2019 to 2022 (5,923 → 11,698, +97%). Since 2023 the count has flattened and may have fallen slightly (12,264 in 2024 → 11,994 in 2025, −2.2%). **Caution:** the 2019–2023 rows count all *licensees* (which may include RISIAs); the 2024–2025 rows count *RCICs*. The definitions may differ, so the 2023→2025 change cannot be read as a like-for-like decline until the annual reports are read directly.

**Why the CICC annual reports were not read directly.** The College publishes its 2021–2025 annual reports only as PDF files. Opening one in the browser starts a file download, which this task forbids. ### 1.5 What this means for the ~25,000 audience

Without assuming what it is: 25,000 is about 2.1 times the 11,994 RCICs in good standing (June 2025), and CAPIC, the largest RCIC association, states "more than 4300 members" (S19). So **an audience of 25,000 cannot consist only of currently licensed RCICs or only of CAPIC members.** It must include other people (for example lawyers, staff, students, applicants or people outside Canada). Its make-up should be established before it is counted as market reach.

## 2. Channel directory — ranked shortlist

**How the ranking works.** Each factor scored 1–3, where 3 is best: **Fit** (are they practice owners in our beginning segment?), **Reach** (published size), **Cost** (3 = low or free), **Permission** (3 = a clear published route to market; 1 = no route or explicit restriction). Score = Fit × Reach × Cost × Permission (maximum 81). Scores are my judgement from the evidence below; Ranjan can re-weight.

**Membership never equals permission to market.** Every route below needs a separate permission (sponsor agreement, organiser approval, or the College's approval of a CPD activity). Emails to individual practitioners are governed by Canada's anti-spam law (§3).

## 3. Permission rules that apply to every channel

1. **Anti-spam law (CASL), s. 10(9)(b).** Consent to a business email is *implied* only if the person has "conspicuously published" the address, the publication does not say they refuse unsolicited messages, and the message "is relevant to the person's business, role, functions or duties" (S26). Practitioner emails on public registers may meet this test, but the register's own terms and each listing must be checked. **Confirm with counsel before any email campaign.**
2. **LSO directory:** "should not be used for distribution of unsolicited items" (S11). Do not use it for outreach.
3. **CICC Code of Professional Conduct (SOR/2022-128):** a keyword search found no section on RCICs receiving referral fees from vendors. The conflict-of-interest test (s. 1(2)) concerns risk to a licensee's duties to *clients*. Because Expat Ready plans to pay practitioners commission or credit for referring peers, **confirm with counsel and, if needed, with the College** that this cannot be read as a conflict or improper benefit. Lawyers referring lawyers fall under their law society's rules, which were not checked. Note that the IELTS agent programme already pays practitioners (§2, row 10).
4. **Member forums and listservs** (IMMeForum, CBA Connect) have their own codes; none were verified. Assume no commercial posting without the organiser's written permission.
