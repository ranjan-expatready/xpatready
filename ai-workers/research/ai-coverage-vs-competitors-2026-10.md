# AI coverage against VisaFlo and the closest competitors (October 2026)

**Prepared by:** competitor-analyst, 11 Oct 2026. **For:** the founder, through the Lead. **Status:** research input. Nothing here is a decision.
**The question:** do the AI capabilities we have specified at least cover what VisaFlo offers, and where do we lead or lag?
**Builds on, without repeating:** `research/visaflo.md` (9 Oct; §3.1 agents, §12 AI direction, §16 comparison) and `research/field-scan.md` (9 Oct, v2). Facts from those files keep their 9 Oct access date. Everything fetched for this file was accessed on **11 Oct 2026**.

**How to read the labels**
- **C** = vendor claim (the company's own site, help pages, LinkedIn page or store listing text). It shows what they *say*, not that it works.
- **V** = verified by a source the company does not control (a store's user counter, a newspaper, a third-party comparison).
- **I** = inferred (our reading of the evidence; reason given). **U** = unknown (looked, not found).
- **Status words for competitors:** *live* = presented as available now; *announced* = labelled coming soon, launching, in development or "new" with no availability stated; *not found* = not on any page we read.
- **Our side is a specification, not a product.** AW-01 (file `requirements/AW-01-firm-ai-assistants_v0.1.md`, document version 0.6, 11 Oct 2026) says in its document control: *"Every requirement here is a Proposal until the founder places this module on Drive."* AW-02 (file `requirements/AW-02-platform-ai-assistants_v0.1.md`, document version 0.8) says *"Nothing here is built."* (§4 intro). No pilot runs on real client files until counsel has read the counsel pack (AW1-D31, resting on AIW-D64 and AIW-D68). Every "we cover" below means "our specification covers it".

**Rules followed.** Public pages only; no sign-in, no form, no trial, no contact with any vendor. No personal names (README rule 10): testimonials and founders are referred to by role or not at all. Competitor wording appears only as short quotes, attributed; their feature names are used only to say which feature is meant, never as names for anything of ours. Expat Ready facts are cited by ID, file and version and are not restated (AIW-D22).

## 1. Summary (8 lines, for the founder)

1. On paper, our AI specification covers most of what VisaFlo sells and has stricter controls on every row; but VisaFlo is selling today, and nothing of ours is built.
2. We match or go further on: reading documents, sorting incoming messages, drafting letters and replies, helping intake, watching deadlines, checking a file before review, and AI measures, always with a named person approving and every step recorded.
3. We lead on paper where no rival shows anything: billing help (unbilled work, payment matching), a rule-built readiness report showing each fact's source, a Canada-only AI provider gate, and tests that block a bad AI release.
4. VisaFlo leads today on filling IMM forms and IRCC portals (its browser extension has 205 users, a store-verified count), a website chatbot, meeting notes, intake in 13 languages and a staff question-and-answer assistant.
5. Part of that lead is our own choice: no AI chatbot talking to the public, no eligibility content to leads, no AI typing into government portals until counsel answers, no call recording for now.
6. Several of VisaFlo's "agent" features are still announced, not shipped: its whole-file AI review and its rule-change monitor are both labelled "coming soon", and we found no independent user review anywhere.
7. The newest close rival is Navisa: it claims seven AI agents, including government-letter tracking and policy-change alerts, at USD 16 per case; none of this is independently verified.
8. Worth closing for version 1: form filling (already designed, waiting for the team's route), a counsel answer on portal filling, turning client messages into answers on case files, and a scan-quality check; leave the chatbot, transcription and style-learning.

## 5. Where we lead, stated honestly

**First, the honest frame.** Everything in this section is **a written requirement, not a working product**. Nothing is built. The AI provider is not chosen (the tech lead's provider test, AIW-D69). Counsel has not read the limits (AIW-D68). The Canada pack content the AI would read is not authored (`field-scan.md` §4 row 9). Several of these strengths rest on team modules that the Charter or scope triage may still move (`visaflo.md` §16 frame). A rival with shipped AI could copy most of these ideas in a quarter (`design/direction-review-2026-10-09.md` §5).

With that said, where our specification is ahead of every rival's public pages:

1. **Controls you can test.** A delegation record per named person (AW-1-5), limits enforced on the server (AW-1-37), approval floors no setting can lower (AG-10-44), every model call logged (AG-11-72), one AI provenance view per file (AW-1-23), checks against rubber-stamping (AW-1-33), evaluation sets and hidden-instruction tests that block a release (AW-1-119, AW-1-62), and an emergency stop with recall (AW-1-11, AW-2-76). VisaFlo markets five guardrail layers (C) but names no AI provider, no AI processing region and no AI audit trail (V, absence). No rival in the set publishes anything comparable (I).
2. **Canada-only AI as a hard gate.** No AI runs until a provider record shows a Canadian processing region, no training, no retention and a passed test call (AW-1-52; AG11-D15). VisaFlo, Navisa and Immicase state data hosting but not where AI processing happens (C, absence).
3. **Billing help.** Unbilled-work lists, payment-match candidates and proposed invoices (AW-1-96 to AW-1-101). Not found at any rival.
4. **A readiness report built by rule, with every fact's source.** It becomes the submission for internal review only when the officer accepts it (AW-1-71 to AW-1-74). VisaFlo's equivalent is still "coming soon" and promises a score instead.
5. **Eligibility by rule, repeatable, never sent to leads by AI.** Same input and pack version give the same result (AG-3-39); assistants never run it or send it (AW-1-88, AW-1-90).
6. **Rule changes enter through a reviewed, versioned pack.** The watcher proposes; two people publish (AW-2-53 to AW-2-56). Rivals summarise web pages for the firm to act on.
7. **Several countries as data, not code** (the Country Pack design, FD-22, as cited in `field-scan.md` §4 row 8). **Not a lead today:** Canada is the only pack at launch, and no rival has a second country either.
8. **Firms only.** We sell no applicant-side tool (AIW-D11). VisaFlo appears to run a do-it-yourself applicant product (`visaflo.md` §17) and Navisa offers a free applicant tool that hands leads to firms (C). For a firm, a vendor that also serves its clients directly is a channel conflict (I).
