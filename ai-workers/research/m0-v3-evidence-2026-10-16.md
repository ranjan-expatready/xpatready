## 2. Pricing: definitions and the decision in force

**Founder decision, not a new proposal.** `../decisions.md`, AIW-D111: "a prepared-file allowance and clearly priced overage"; "system retries, failed runs and corrections of our errors must not consume additional units". AIW-D114: "One billable prepared file per application prepared" and "Counted once, when the officer accepts ... readiness report for that application". The product's application record, not an uploaded document or a page, is the billing unit. Work permit then PR: two application records, hence two accepted preparations. A 100-page upload is not 100 units. Q1 is now answered: D199 reaffirms this basis, with exact prices and allowances still open.

**Vendor claim, checked 16 Oct 2026.** [Visto pricing](https://visto.ai/pricing/) lists plan allowances of 10, 25 and 55 applications monthly, CAD 29 for each extra application, and separate co-pilot message limits. This supports the recognisability of allowances plus application overage; it does not establish our price, costs, reliability or unit definition. No account, trial or purchase was created.

**Lead recommendation (Proposal).** Retain D111/D114. Show usage and a warning before overage. Keep AI-use and page-cost measurement internal. Per-field charging rewards longer questionnaires and makes repeated rows ambiguous; token charging exposes model and retry choices to firms. A flat unlimited plan hides the heavy-file tail. Per-skill charging remains only AIW-A12 under D189. No new cap, surcharge or exception to free re-runs is introduced here. Very large workloads need measured cost limits and an agreed plan, not an undisclosed second charge.

**Still Unknown:** the precise application-record treatment of an Express Entry profile versus a post-invitation application, an additional-document response, a procedural-fairness response, and a resubmission after refusal. The founder and team must resolve those examples before pricing is implemented. They must not be inferred from government terminology.

## 3. What consumes AI tokens

**Decided:** D169/D197 use saved approved maps; SA-01B v1.4 §3.5 SA-1B-35 says "AI never sets a value on a form at fill time." Filling already confirmed values through an unchanged map needs no generative-model call. AI document extraction, a form cross-check, message drafts, consistency checks and co-pilot answers do consume tokens. A changed screen stops filling; re-mapping changed fields has setup cost. OCR has its own service cost. This is not a claim that preparing an application is free.

**Assumptions, reused transparently:** `../commercial/provider-and-cost.md` §3.2–3.3 supplies the light / typical / heavy workload. Original pages: 16 / 60 / 240. Reread multipliers: 1 / 1.3 / 2. Page-reading calls: 16 / 78 / 480. The typical 435,000 input and 56,200 output tokens comprise: document reading 195,000 / 31,200; consistency checks 90,000 / 12,000; drafts 20,000 / 3,000; message sorting 30,000 / 2,000; co-pilot 80,000 / 5,000; readiness reports 20,000 / 3,000. These are a planning workload, not a measured normal Express Entry file. The co-pilot allocation is a cost allowance, not another billable application.

**Verified price list, checked 16 Oct 2026:** [OpenAI Standard API pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing) lists GPT-6 Luna at USD 0.10 input / 0.50 output per million tokens, and GPT-6.1 Sol at USD 2 / 10. Comparison only; neither is thereby approved to process product client data. Production stays subject to the provider record, approved region/retention terms and the skill tests. Standard short-context prices only; no cache, batch or fast-mode discount. Extra tool and long-context charges are not included.

**Scenario conversion, not a current exchange quote:** reuse 1.424 CAD/USD and a 10% model premium for comparison with D163. OCR remains the historical, **Unverified** USD 0.0015 per processed page. Formula: `CAD = ((input/1e6 × input_price + output/1e6 × output_price) × 1.10 + page_calls × 0.0015) × 1.424`. A real OCR-plus-extraction implementation must measure its actual token payload; page-image and OCR text inputs must not be assumed interchangeable.

Batching would change the cost. This is an illustration, not an Express Entry count or measured implementation. Our saved-map fill adds zero model tokens for that transfer; later AI cross-checks are costed separately.

## 6. Naming hierarchy and branding (D200; confirmed by D201)

**Founder direction, D200:** use a broad internal design area with clearly identified child components; practical naming is delegated to the Lead. The founder also asked whether Product Configurator should be the broader name and how to consolidate the five areas.

**Lead’s applied working names:** Product Studio is the internal authoring area; Pathway Configurator is its first component. Form reading/matching, question/document/condition drafting and content-change review are skills within it. The existing shared facts, rules, tests, versions and human release remain part of that component. No new agent, service, permission or scope is created by a label.

**Confirmed by the founder, D201:** one overall Expat Ready AI heading; five existing areas (Product Studio, Case Flow, Firm Operations, your assistant, Expat Ready Operations); one common AI Controls layer. The user-facing assistant keeps its decided name. Product Configurator describes setup and is therefore too narrow to name all five areas. This is the Lead’s design judgment, not a name prescribed by an external standard or a tested customer preference. The branding hierarchy is approved; no artifact has been published. Each publication still needs the founder’s yes.

**The complete back-office map:** Product Studio contains Pathway Configurator (Module 1); template authoring is a possible further component but its AI drafting is still only a Proposal. Expat Ready Operations contains support/reply skills and account work. AI Controls is shared governance, not an AI personality. Existing administration retains plans, billing, users and permissions. Case Flow/Firm Operations operate in the firm app; the co-pilot requests their existing skills. These are presentation boundaries, not separate infrastructure or five independent AI engines.

**Primary guidance opened 16 Oct 2026; the naming is our inference:**
- [NN/G: familiar language](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/match-system-real-world/), section “Using Specific and Familiar Language for Users”: use labels that match the user’s tasks and vocabulary. Applied here through Configurator, Form Mapper and Question Builder.
- [NN/G: consistency](https://www.nngroup.com/articles/consistency-and-standards/): keep the same meaning and label across related surfaces. Applied here through one overall product heading and repeated skill names.
- [Microsoft HAX guidelines](https://www.microsoft.com/en-us/haxtoolkit/ai-guidelines/): communicate system capabilities and limits clearly. Applied here by showing AI drafting separately from rules, confirmation and release.
- [Anthropic: Building effective agents](https://www.anthropic.com/engineering/building-effective-agents), “When (and when not) to use agents” and “Combining and customizing these patterns”: start simple and add complexity only for demonstrated value. This older architectural guidance supports skill reuse, not a compulsory agent framework or any specific brand.
- [NIST AI RMF Core](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/5-sec-core/), Govern 2.1 and 3.2, Map 2.1: distinguish responsible roles, human oversight and supported tasks. Applied here through a visible common control layer and explicit AI/rules/person boundaries. This is design guidance, not a claim of legal compliance. Legal: Unconfirmed: no counsel (AIW-D94).

**Q3 confirmed, D201:** one heading and the five areas. D196’s scope/charging and D193’s open organisation comparison remain in force. No customer-preference or market test has been performed on these names.
