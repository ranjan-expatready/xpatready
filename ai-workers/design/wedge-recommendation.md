# The wedge: what to sell first (Lead's recommendation, 9 Oct 2026)

**Status: Proposal.** Ranjan asked for my own best call: simple, on trend, achievable, easy to sell, useful, and not yet done by anyone in this space. Everything below is grounded in W1 evidence; the parts that are my judgement are marked.

## The product in one line

**The File Builder: an AI Worker that takes a signed client and builds the whole file to "ready for your review", for a fixed price per file.**

From the moment a retainer is signed, it chases documents, reads them, fills in the questionnaire as suggestions, checks the facts against each other, builds the document checklist and the submission bundle, and stops at a readiness report. A licensed person reviews, edits and approves. Nothing reaches a client or a form without that approval.

## Why this one

| Criterion | Evidence |
|---|---|
| Biggest manual loop in the firm | Officers verify or reject every upload with a written reason, re-review every re-upload, verify every questionnaire answer against a document, and chase missing items (CP-4 §3.5, AG-8, AG-9: W1 inventory). This is the junior-clerk work every firm pays for. |
| Already 80% specified | Reader, Sorter, Checker, Drafter, the Needs you inbox, floors, Binder checklist, document-received and missing-document recipes are in the October requirements (AG-9, AG-11 §3.5, AG-11-93). The worker is a job-shaped bundle of what exists. |
| Easy to sell | Firms think in cost per file. "A prepared file for CAD X, no salary, no training, same quality every time" is a sentence a consultant understands in a demo. Per-case pricing is what the best-funded players use, and nobody in Canadian immigration software publishes it (field-scan §5). |
| On trend but not done | VisaFlo markets "agents per case" but much is "coming soon" and invitation-only; Clio has agents but no IRCC packs; auto-fill vendors fill forms but don't build the file (research/visaflo.md §12, field-scan §4). **"Not done by anyone" is true as far as we found; it cannot be proven, and VisaFlo is close.** |
| Defensible | Exact answers from versioned packs (same input, same result), every value traced to its source document, approval by a named person, Canada-only processing, and an audit record of "AI prepared, person approved" (the last one is a W2 improvement). These are the five things VisaFlo's pages do not show (visaflo.md §12 "what the evidence does not show"). |
| Fits your rules | FD-16 ("AI assists, humans decide"), AIW-D11 (firms only), AIW-D13 (acts on behalf of a named licensed person). The worker never submits anything to a government authority (AG-11-54). |

## What makes it simple

- **One worker, one job, one price.** Not a platform of twelve agents. The other workers (lead follow-up, deadline guardian, post-filing watcher) come later and are sold as add-ons once the first one proves itself.
- **One screen for the firm:** the Needs you inbox they already have. No new place to learn.
- **One promise:** "Ready for your review in X days, every value traced." Not "no errors": the licensed person remains the decision-maker, which is also what keeps us on the right side of the regulator.

## The hook (free or near-free)

**The Deadline Guardian (Minder)**: watches every government and document deadline and nudges the right person. It is in your requirements, it is cheap to run, and "never miss a deadline" is the fear every consultant has. It gets firms switched on; the File Builder makes the money. (Judgement: a free hook plus a paid per-file worker is the pattern that sells fastest in small-firm software.)

## How it makes money (shape only; numbers come in W3 after validation)

- Firm plan (per seat today) stays. The File Builder is **priced per prepared file**, so revenue grows with the firm's caseload, not its headcount, which is the right direction when AI reduces headcount.
- Cost to serve is mostly AI model calls per file plus review tooling; it is measurable per file, so margin can be managed.
- Validation first: five to ten licensed consultants react to a demo file and a price before we fix the number (W3b).

## The strongest objection, honestly

"If the firm still reviews everything, how much time does it really save?" The answer must come from measurement: time per file with and without the worker, on real files, with the consultants in the validation round. We do not have that number today, and I will not invent it.
