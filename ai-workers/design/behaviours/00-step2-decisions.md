## 1. What Step 2 found, in five lines

1. **Seven behaviour documents passed** (each: maker, hostile review, fix round, re-check or the Lead's verification). Each says what starts the persona, every step it takes, what it never does, where a person approves, what is recorded, what it costs, what counsel must read, and walks one fictional firm through a full example where every message and every act is counted.
2. **Much less of the work is AI than Step 1 implied.** Rigour moved work from models to rules, step by step: the readiness report is built by rule (a model adds one labelled paragraph); the deadline watch is the product's own reminders with no AI; the Practice expert's first version answers four fixed questions by rule (a model adds one optional summary line); money, eligibility scores and the retainer's wording never come from a model. AI genuinely works where it reads documents, sorts messages, cross-checks facts and drafts text a person approves.
3. **That is good for safety, cost and the regulator,** and it changes the story: *AI Workers* is a governed assistant layer where AI reads and drafts, and rules and people decide. The investor and sales wording must say that and no more (README rule 5).
4. **No persona acts on its own in any example.** Across the seven examples the personas make proposals and model calls; every send is the product's template or a draft a named person approved; every money record, verification, form approval and submission is a person's act.
5. **The reviews also found real product risks for the team,** listed in section 6 (for example: a bank's notice address learned as one client's, so later payments file to the wrong client; a persona task that would close a government deadline if ticked).
