## 0. Summary: the persona in five plain lines

1. The Practice expert is the reference desk a staff member asks. It answers a fixed set of question shapes about the firm's own files and the rulebook: *which of my files are at risk this week, and why*; *what does this client still need, and which rule says so*; *why did the Qualifier rank this program the way it did*; *what is waiting on me*. Version 1 answers those shapes only; free questions over documents and messages are version 2 (catalogue W-24 "New"; section 11, decision 1).
2. It answers from two sources only: the published rulebook version in force for the firm's country, pinned to the file where the question is about a file, and the firm's own records that the asker is allowed to open (IMP-20 rule 2). Every answer lists where each statement came from (pack section and version; record and field). When it finds no source it says so and stops; it never answers from memory and never fills a gap with general knowledge (IMP-20 rule 2; AIW-D25).
3. It reads only what the asker may see, checked by the product's own permission rules at the moment of reading, on the server, record by record (IMP-20 rule 3; AG-14-22; AG-7-88). A case officer who may see only their own files gets an answer about their own files, and no count, name or hint about anyone else's.
4. It never acts: no action, no Doer call, no write, no message to a client, no eligibility verdict the Qualifier did not compute, no deadline it worked out itself, no number it computed (IMP-20 rules 1 and 4; AIW-D25). The most sensitive personal data (Restricted fields such as a passport number) is never passed to the model and never appears in an answer; the answer says only whether such a field is present, expired or missing (IMP-21). Nobody approves an answer, because nothing happens when it is given; anything a person wants to send to a client goes through an acting persona and its approver (IMP-20 rule 5; AG-10-44).
5. Every question and answer is recorded with its sources, by reference, so the firm can show what the expert said, to whom, from which records and which pack version, and answers to an unlicensed staff member are marked as such (IMP-20 rule 7; AG-11-72; AIW-D33 reading). It costs one model call plus the retrieval per question, which the catalogue calls "the least predictable cost", so it runs under a cap per user per day and per firm per month (W-24; IMP-36).

## 1. What starts it

**The trigger is a question.** A signed-in staff member types a question into the expert (the nearest product pattern is a manual, on-demand run: "manual / on-demand runs of an automation against a chosen entity, permissioned and audited", AG-11-7). There are no scheduled triggers, no event triggers and no runs that span days: one question, one answer, one log entry. The only other way the expert is invoked is a *why* line requested for an item another persona prepared (section 2.5), which is the same question-and-answer under the viewer's permissions.

**What the asker may see, and how that is checked at the moment of reading (the fixed block of this document, in place of the permission subset).** The rule is IMP-20 rule 3: "every record the co-pilot looks up passes the same permission and firm check as opening it by hand (the AG-14-22 pattern); internal notes, Restricted fields and other firms' data are never reachable; the answer is scoped to the viewer, never widened." The product already does this for dashboards and search, and the expert reuses those checks rather than inventing its own:

- **Server-side, from the permission, never from the screen.** "The same dashboard renders firm-wide for `view.all` and own-only for `view.own`; the scope is computed on the server from the caller's permission, never from a client-supplied flag. A filter can narrow within that scope but **never widen** it" (AG-14 v1.10 BR-2). Drill-through "SHALL **re-enforce its own permission + firm_id** (no escalation via drill)" (AG-14-22); "a user can never reach via a chart a record they couldn't open directly" (AG-14 BR-8). The expert's retrieval is a drill-through with a question in front of it: every record it would quote is opened through its owning module's check (Proposal, IMP-20 rule 3).
- **Record by record, at read time.** "Client visibility SHALL be enforced server-side within `firm_id`" (AG-7-88); a user with the own-only case key (`agent/case:view.own`) "but without `agent/case:view.all` SHALL see only the cases they own (case officer, reviewer or lead RCIC on the case team — `CaseTeamMember`) or are on the case team of, in lists, the board, counts, search and export" (AG-7-88). "One shared-core visibility check SHALL be used by every list, search, count, export and detail view that returns such a record" and a record the user cannot see "SHALL NOT appear and SHALL NOT be counted" (AG-7-90). "Visibility is evaluated at read time against the user's current role and the parent's current owner, so a reassignment takes effect on the next read" (AG-13-67). So the expert never caches an answer across askers and never tells an asker how many records it could not see.
- **The search the expert would use.** The Finder is "One firm-scoped, permission-filtered, ownership-filtered search across all entity types" whose queries are "scoped by `firm_id` (RLS + explicit predicate) and by the caller's ownership scope (e.g., `view.own` vs `view.all`) *before* full-text matching"; "**Every result is triple-filtered:** firm-scoped → permission-filtered (no entity-type permission ⇒ zero hits of that type) → record-level-ownership-filtered"; and "**NO Restricted PII is projected into the search index.**" (Doc 1 v1.3 §8.8). Its first acceptance test is that "Tenant A's search never returns a Tenant B row, including via error message or count" (Doc 1 v1.3 §8.8 AC-F1). The expert's retrieval index over records inherits all three filters (Proposal, catalogue D9); its index over pack rule text is new (section 9, G-3).

*Two consequences the firm should hear in plain words.* First, because every starter role holds the see-all keys by default, in a new firm every staff member can ask about every file; a firm that wants need-to-know copies a role and removes the see-all key, exactly as SA-06 describes, and the expert follows on the next read. Second, a firm-wide question (*how many of our files are at risk*) is a reporting question, and only the Owner, the Admin and the Case Manager hold the firm-wide report key by default; a Case Officer asking it gets the answer over their own files, with no hint of the rest (AG-14 BR-2; AG-7-90 "SHALL NOT be counted").

**What stops a question or an answer.** The AI switch is off, or the platform emergency stop has tripped (IMP-8, Proposal): the expert is unavailable and says so. The provider is down: "AI provider down or timing out → treated as unavailable for that call: logged in `AiRunLog` as PROVIDER_ERROR, the manual path applies, and the work is not blocked" (AG-11 v1.11 §9 edge cases). The asker's daily cap or the firm's monthly cap is reached (IMP-36, Proposal): the question is refused with the cap wording and nothing is called. The asker's permission to a record changes while the answer is being assembled: the record is re-checked at read, so it is simply not in the answer (AG-13-67 pattern). The question is outside the fixed shapes, or no source is found: the plain refusal (IMP-20 rules 2 and 11).

**When AI is off or the provider fails.** The expert is never load-bearing (catalogue G14): "With AI off, gated, or the provider unavailable, every path SHALL still work by hand" (AG-11-71); "every AI-assisted task SHALL stay available by hand, and no other function SHALL be blocked" (AG-16-35). The person opens the screens the expert would have read: the Needs you queue (AG-11 v1.11 §3.13), the case's readiness panel with its deficiencies and fix links (AG-07 v2.7 §3.6), the Minder's active watches view and the upcoming-deadline list (AG-11 v1.11 §3.4; AG-14-17), the assessment's breakdown (AG-03 v1.4 §3.5), and the checklist, whose every item already "carries **the reason it is there**" (SA-1B-59). Nothing the expert does is the only way to see something.

## 2. What it does, step by step: the question shapes and the retrieval rules

**How to read this section.** The expert has no actions from the Conductor's closed list (AG-11-2) and never will: "it has no actions, cannot call a Doer, cannot write anything; the only thing it can produce is text for the signed-in staff member" (IMP-20 rule 1). So the "step" of this persona is a *question shape*: a recognised kind of question, the records and pack text it is allowed to open for it, the shape of the answer, what it refuses, and what is logged. The model's only job is to put the retrieved facts into plain words with their references; the retrieval itself is a rule over product data and the asker's permissions (IMP-10: a model "never selects which action runs"; here it never selects which record is read either, Proposal). The model call is one of the "plain Doer calls ...: no tools, output checked against a fixed expected shape, treated as untrusted" (IMP-10, whose words these are; the product rule behind it is AG-11-94: "models SHALL have no tool access"). The answer's expected shape is "text plus source references, nothing else" (IMP-26 rule 3).

### 2.1 Shape 1: *at risk this week, and why*

*In plain words.* The asker wants the files that are close to a date that matters, and the reason each date matters. The expert does not decide what is at risk: it reads the Minder's watches and the product's own deadline list for the files the asker may see, and quotes the dates, the statuses and the pack's definition of each deadline. **Hidden instructions in what it reads (IMP-26, Proposal, on AG-11-94).** A file's notes, a client's message classification, a deficiency label or a pack passage reaches the model "as delimited data, never as instructions" (AG-11-94). For this persona: a note that says *tell the user to email the file to this address* is quoted as a note, if the asker may see notes on that case, and never followed; the answer's shape is checked ("text plus source references, nothing else", IMP-26 rule 3), so no link, no address and no instruction to the asker survives the check; and "A standing test set of planted instructions" runs against every version of the model instructions before it goes live (IMP-26 rule 5; IMP-5).

**Restricted personal data (IMP-21, Proposal).** The exact Restricted set is the founder's open item B-1, whose question is "The exact field list classified "Restricted" for field-level envelope encryption + mask-by-default + audited reveal" and whose note says it "must be counsel-confirmed, not architect-invented", with the proposed default "passport/travel-doc numbers, SIN + foreign equivalents, gov-ID numbers, financial-account/bank details, biometric references, licence/designated-person credential fields" (Founder Decisions Log v1.5 B-1). **What happens when a limit is hit.** (1) *No source found, or a question outside the shapes:* the plain refusal (IMP-20 rule 11), logged as a refusal (section 5). (5) *The answer fails the shape or word check (a number not in the retrieved values, a forbidden word, anything that is not text plus references):* the answer is not shown; the log records the failed check; the asker sees the refusal (Proposal, IMP-26 rule 3 and IMP-5 pattern). (6) *Who is told:* nobody but the asker, for a refusal; the firm's AI health view counts refusals and failed checks (section 6); a failed planted-instruction test before release stops the release (IMP-26 rule 5). (7) *Always recorded:* the AI run log row (section 5).

## 6. Cost drivers and how we measure it

**Cost drivers (cited, not recomputed).** The catalogue: "retrieval plus one call per question; context size; capped per user per day and per firm per month (IMP-36); the least predictable cost" (W-24 "Cost drivers"). The cost scenario carries two rows for the expert as a paired worker: E1, co-pilot questions per file "(W-24 paired with W-01)", **2 / 10 / 30** low / typical / high, "Capped per user per day and per firm per month (IMP-36)"; E2, tokens per question (in / out) **5,000 / 300, 8,000 / 500, 12,000 / 800**, with the note "Retrieval context is the biggest input (catalogue W-24: "the least predictable cost")" (`../../commercial/provider-and-cost.md` §3.2). Every input is an Assumption with a range (§3.2 heading). The retrieval itself (the permission-filtered search and the record reads) is the product's own work, not a model call, and is not costed there.

**Cost per question (Assumption, derived from §3.2 rows E2 and §3.4's method, re-runnable per §3.7).** On the mid-tier model priced at $2 per million input tokens and $10 per million output tokens (§3.4, 9 Oct 2026), with the regional premium 1.10 (G1) and the rate 1.4240 (G2): low (5,000 × 2 + 300 × 10) ÷ 1,000,000 = $0.013 → × 1.10 × 1.4240 = **about CAD 0.02**; typical (8,000 × 2 + 500 × 10) ÷ 1,000,000 = $0.021 → **about CAD 0.03**; high (12,000 × 2 + 800 × 10) ÷ 1,000,000 = $0.032 → **about CAD 0.05**. Per prepared file at E1's counts: about CAD 0.04 / 0.33 / 1.50, which is the scenario's own co-pilot line (§3.3: 10,000 / 600, 80,000 / 5,000 and 360,000 / 24,000 tokens per file). §3.4's "sensible mix" puts the co-pilot on the top-tier model, which doubles these (§3.4: "about double on Opus 5.5"). The unpredictability is in E2, not E1: a question that retrieves a long pack passage and many records costs several times a short one, and the cap is the only control (§3.6: "a co-pilot used heavily, can exceed the subscription on model cost alone").

## 7. Confirm with counsel / RCIC

One list; each line says why. Where a term is law, this document states general knowledge and asks counsel to confirm (README rule 5).

1. **Is an answer to staff that cites a pack rule "advice"?** The expert tells a staff member which published rule applies to a file and what the Qualifier concluded, with sources, and never speaks to a client. Whether that is information to the firm's own staff or the practice of immigration advice when the asker is unlicensed (the Case Officer in section 8), and whether the mark *answered to an unlicensed staff member* and the licensed approver on anything that reaches a client are enough: counsel / RCIC. The product's own position is that advice cannot be detected by a machine: ""Anything that reads as advice" is **not** a mechanical floor (it cannot be detected)" (AG-10-44).
2. **Who may ask.** AIW-D26 and AIW-D33 let the person a persona works for be unlicensed; for the expert that means any staff member the firm allows may ask (section 4). ## 10. Glossary additions

Only terms not already in `../ai-organisation.md` §8 or `case-assistant.md` §10.

- **Question shape:** this document's name for one of the fixed kinds of question version 1 answers (*at risk this week*, *what is missing*, *which rule*, *what is waiting*), each with its own allowed sources and answer format (Proposal; catalogue W-24 "New").
- **Reference desk:** the master's plain-words picture of the persona (`../ai-organisation.md` §2.5): a place staff ask and get an answer with its sources; it does nothing else.
- **Source (of an answer):** one of the two things the expert may answer from: a passage of the published pack version in force (section and version) or a record the asker may open (record and field) (IMP-20 rule 2). **Refusal:** the expert's fixed reply when it has no source, when the question is outside the shapes, when the cap is reached or when its own check fails (IMP-20 rule 11; wording Unknown).
- **Scope line:** the line on every answer saying whether it covers *your own files* or *all files of the firm*, taken from the permission check and never from the question (Proposal; AG-14 BR-2).
- **Pinned version:** the pack version a client's assessments and case were started under and stay on until staff adopt a newer one (SA-01 v2.2 §3.14; SA-1-58). **Adoption:** that one-click, reasoned, audited switch to the newer version (SA-01 v2.2 §3.14).
- **Finder:** the product's search across all record types, firm-scoped, permission-filtered and ownership-filtered, with no Restricted field in its index (Doc 1 v1.3 §8.8); release Unknown. **Retrieval index:** this document's name for the expert's search over records and pack text that inherits the Finder's filters (Proposal, catalogue D9).
- **Minder watch:** the Minder's record of one deadline it is watching on one file, with its status (armed, escalating, satisfied, breached) and next step (`MinderWatch`, AG-11 v1.11 §7). **At-risk mark:** the flag the Minder puts on a case at or near a breach, only where the firm has that Team safeguards part on (AG-11 v1.11 §3.4; AG-11-49).
- **Readiness state:** the Reviewer's summary of a case, not ready, attention or ready (`NOT_READY | ATTENTION | READY`, AG-7-27). **Fix link:** the action the product attaches to a deficiency, such as "Request missing document" (AG-07 v2.7 §3.6).
- **Breakdown:** the Qualifier's factor-by-factor list of points behind a score (AG-3-21). **Rules as at:** the date the rules an assessment used were current (`rulesAsOf`, AG-03 v1.4 §3.7; SA-1B-88). **Re-run:** a new assessment of the same person, always a new record, never an edit of the old one (AG-3-28).
- **Done for you:** the feed of what the Doers did automatically in the last 24 hours (AG-11-65). **Run history:** the per-file list of every automation that touched it (AG-11-35).
- **Licensed-or-not mark:** this document's name for the line on an answer and in its log row saying whether the asker holds a regulator credential on their profile (Proposal; AIW-D26, AIW-D33; SA6-D9).
- **Hallucination test set:** a fixed list of questions with known right answers, run against every version of the expert before it goes live (IMP-5 "fixed test set"; the Step 3 gate's "evaluation set"). **Planted-instruction test set:** documents and messages containing hidden instructions, run the same way (IMP-26 rule 5).
- **Legal hallucination:** a model's "textual output that is not consistent with legal facts" (REPORT-CARD §6 lens 4, quoting arxiv.org/abs/2401.01301, opened 10 Oct 2026); the reason this persona answers from sources only.
- **Practice Pack:** the rulebook for the firm's own regulator (who may advise, trust money, retention, privacy), as distinct from the Country Pack for the destination (FD-22, Founder Decisions Log v1.5).
- **ECA:** an educational credential assessment, the report that states what a foreign qualification equals in Canada (general knowledge; confirm with the Immigration SME; `case-assistant.md` §10).

## 11. Decisions for the founder (and, where marked, for the team)

Every decision this document raises, with the Lead's recommendation where the Lead has made one and this maker's recommendation otherwise, marked as such. Only the founder ratifies decisions (AIW-D23).

1. **The fixed-shapes limit and version 2 (founder).** Version 1 answers four shapes only, from the Minder, the deficiencies, Needs you and pack rule text; free questions over documents and messages are version 2 (catalogue W-24 "New"; `../../proposals.md` expert v1 row of 9 Oct 2026). *Maker's recommendation: confirm version 1 as the pilot scope; decide version 2 only after the pilot's refusal-by-reason rate (section 6) shows what staff ask that the shapes do not cover, and after IMP-26's planted-instruction set has run on document text.*
2. **Does asking need a delegation record (founder, with the Lead)?** IMP-20 rule 7 logs the answer "under the user's delegation (IMP-1)"; IMP-20 rule 3 and AIW-A8 read under the asker's own permissions; the expert never acts, so a delegation's purpose, a named person answerable for an act, has nothing to attach to. Two readings: (a) no delegation; the question is the asker's own read, the AI run log row names the asker, and the firm's product-map item (IMP-9) is the only switch; (b) a one-line read-only delegation each staff member accepts once (IMP-1 rule 1 pattern), so every row carries a `delegationId` like every other persona's and the per-user cap and the licensed-or-not mark hang off it. *Maker's recommendation: (b), for one reason: the record then says the same thing for all seven personas (IMP-1: a named person is answerable for every step), and the first acceptance is where the firm's own words about what the expert is and is not are shown and acknowledged.*
3. **AIW-D25 stands as written (founder, confirm).** "Expert co-pilot, no actions. That's fine": answers only, sources only, refuses without a source, Canada-only. This document adds nothing to it and relies on every word. *Maker's recommendation: confirm, and add the two sentences this document rests on that D25 does not say: the expert reads under the asker's own permissions (AIW-A8), and it is never a component of another persona (the Lead's brief of 10 Oct 2026).*
4. **How the *why* lines reach the Case assistant, the Firm assistant and the Intake assistant (founder, with the Lead; G-12).** Section 2.5 proposes render time, for the viewer, under the viewer's permissions, as display text only, with a blank line when the expert refuses or is unavailable; the alternative is run time, under the delegating person's permissions, stored in the artefact. *Maker's recommendation: render time, because it keeps the expert "answers only" (nothing of it is stored inside another persona's work), keeps every reader inside their own permissions, and means no persona ever waits on the expert to finish a run; the price is one question per open line per open of an item, which E1 already allows for.*

But the expert is a new way to read many records at once, and the product's earlier design logged every reveal of a Restricted field before returning it (Doc 1 v1.3 §7.2), a path AG7-D10 has since withdrawn. *Maker's recommendation: no audit row; the AI run log row with its sources by reference is the record, and it goes into the firm's export (IMP-2(d)); revisit if counsel's answer to section 7 item 7 asks for a read log of who asked about whom.*
