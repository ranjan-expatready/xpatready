5 · One button colour, app-wide (R-144 point 6)

One design-system token, --color-primary, drives every primary button: card buttons, + Add, Approve, Release. Every check below was worked out on white and on the page grey (#f7f7f7). EXPERIENCE §12 asks for 4.5:1 for text, and a button's edge needs 3:1 against what is around it.

A · Brand blue (recommended)

Start setupResume setupOpen

#0862C5 with white text, 5.9:1 (hover #054E9C, 8.2:1); fill against white 5.9:1. It is the team's own token today (tokens.css, primary-600), so every existing primary button already matches and nothing new enters the design system. Green stays free to mean "Live" and "done", so a button never looks like a status.

B · Green, as the founder suggested

Start setupResume setupOpen

#15803D with white text, 5.0:1, which passes. But green already means Live and done (✓ Live, the green bar, the ok tokens), so a green Open on a Live card reads as a status, not an action. It would also be a new token outside the brand kit.

C · Light green

Start setupResume setupOpen

#DCFCE7 fill with #0F5C2E text, 7.4:1, which passes. The fill against white is only 1.1:1, so it needs the #16A34A border (3.3:1) to be seen as a button. It is the weakest call to action of the three, and it has the same meaning clash as B.

  1. Q1 · The button for a person who does not set up (the immigration expert or the approver). Recommendation: they see Open on every card, and Needs you tells them when they must act. Why: "Resume setup" offers them an act the server refuses (403). The founder's one word per state still holds for the author. Other choice: the same three words for everyone, with a refusal in words when they click.
  2. Q2 · Fewer status words in the filter. Recommendation: All · Not started · In progress · Live, plus the Needs you toggle. Why: they match the card's own words, and "Not ready" is always also Needs you for the author. Other choice: keep Not ready as a fifth chip.
  3. Q3 · The button colour. Recommendation: A, the brand blue the app already uses (5.9:1). Why: green means Live and done on this page. Other choice: C, light green with a green border (7.4:1 text), if the founder prefers it after seeing both.
