How employer and worker cases connect i
The employer's LMIA is set up here; the worker's work permit is set up under Retail. On the worker's case the agent picks “relies on employer case”, and the employer's name, address and LMIA number fill in (later wave).
  • A software company hires one developer → one employer case here, one worker case under Retail.
  • One LMIA for three cooks → one employer case, three worker cases, each relying on it.
  • A household hires a caregiver → the household is the employer.

Why D (concept §2, summarised)

  • Vercel-style cards with a list toggle, laid out in Linear-style collapsible groups: cards make 5–20 combinations look finished and easy to scan, and the toggle keeps 50 compact.
  • The setup progress tells the content publisher why the button says what it says; status uses shape and text, never colour alone.
  • Nothing is cut at 1024 px: the trail wraps inside the card.
  • Team parts only: segmented controls (like the team's tree/pane switch), chips, buttons and tokens; List view = the team's table pattern.
  • Rejected: plain cards (B: no progress, weak at 50); split view (C: repeats Structure); Notion gallery (pretty, no status); Stripe dense tables (built for thousands of rows, not 50).
