Design notes — how this answers the founder's questions (27 Oct)
  • One stage or two? Two stages, one screen. Facts feed outputs continuously (a changed fact marks its finished items stale — it never edits them silently), so they must share the screen; the groups and the strip make the two stages unmistakable.
  • Route vs channel: no route step. Each form's channel is picked on its row, inside what the library says IRCC accepts (refused with the reason). The combination proposes the defaults. A form may carry a second line — "PDF copy, not for filing" — which is how "fill it online AND auto-populate the PDF" works without claiming it is filed twice.
  • Why not a wizard: IRCC's own portals are wizards because the applicant enters data once. The officer's work is not linear — a client signs late, a fact changes after upload, a document arrives mid-week. A wizard that keeps reopening is worse than a checklist that shows everything. The strip gives the order; the list gives the whole picture; the right side is one item at a time.
  • Where this pattern comes from: IRCC's portals (per-section "Not started · In progress · Complete" + a per-document upload checklist with a status per row); DocuSign/CLM envelopes (a packet of deliverables, each with its own state); Linear/Jira (list left, work right). Nobody combines them because their silos force one channel per case — ours doesn't, so the deliverable list is the model that stays clean.
  • Research round 2 (27 Oct, live): Docketwise — the leading immigration-forms product — uses exactly this split: one intake questionnaire per client auto-completes every form ("smart forms"), then "packet assembly" bundles the forms and documents into a single filing package; AI/OCR reads uploaded documents into tagged facts. Our "facts once → deliverables list" is the same shape with channels added. Clio: forms fill from "client data you've approved" — our confirmed-facts-only rule (P-72). Oracle BLAF checklist pattern: a nonlinear grouped checklist is the proven pattern for many discrete items finished over days (the officer is interrupted, the client is slow) — wizards are only for ≤4-step data entry done in one sitting (Visa design system). Master-detail is the documented choice when a list item owns a full work destination and must keep selection/scroll across resizes.
  • What "modern" means here: the board fixes the model (deliverables, two stages, states) — the hard part. The polish — exact components, density, motion, keyboard path, empty/error/conflict states — comes when the concept and the build apply EXPERIENCE.md (Workspace pattern §3, states §4) and the team's tokens. That is deliberately not decided by a static board.
