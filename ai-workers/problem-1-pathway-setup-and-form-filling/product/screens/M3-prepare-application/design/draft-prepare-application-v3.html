Design notes — alternatives weighed, research behind them (QA lane, 27 Oct)
  • Why two stages as structure, not headings: the founder's direction is "information gathering" and "form population" clearly split. In v2 the split is only a list heading; here each stage is a first-class view with its own progress, its own blockers and its own next action — while "Everything" preserves the v2 nonlinear checklist.
  • Alternatives: A Split view (this board's default) — stage cards + segment filter, recommended. B Command view — big stage cards + attention queue; best "what next", hides inventory one click down (shown as the second layout). C Side-by-side zones — facts left, deliverables right; most literal, but the item detail has no home and it crams at 1024 px. D Wizard — rejected: IRCC portals are wizards because the applicant enters data once; officer work is nonlinear (a fact change marks finished items stale), so a wizard would reopen constantly. E Checklist — the v2 baseline, kept for comparison.
  • Research (27 Oct, live): Docketwise Smart Forms v3 runs exactly this model — Assemble (forms + files into a packet), Intake (one questionnaire populates every form), review, file. eimmigration: forms auto-fill from the case profile, "Party on Demand" switches the person a section draws from — our person × form matrix. INSZoom: questionnaires → auto-populated forms; automated data entry reads documents into case data — our AI-read uploads proposing facts. Document-generation pipeline pattern (HotDocs/Nintex/Anvil): Collect → Merge → Review → Approve → Distribute; Anvil's multi-party collection work confirms explicit per-section completion flags over inferred completeness — our "Confirm section" (AV-5).
  • What crosses the stage boundary: only confirmed facts flow Stage 1 → Stage 2 (P-72); a changed fact marks finished Stage-2 items stale, never edits them (BR-7); an upload in Stage 2 can propose facts back into Stage 1 (read-back loop). The stage cards carry those badges so the loop is visible from either side.
  • Open: whether a stale item blocks "ready to file" (challenge Q1 — board assumes yes); whether "Needs attention" belongs as a filter chip (split view) or a first-class queue (command view) — the two layouts let the founder feel both.
