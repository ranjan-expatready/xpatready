## Objections

### Blocker
**B1 — The filing grain contradicts AG-7-45, BR-31 and P-119, and Q1 asks the wrong question.** *Facts:* frame 1 (board :200) puts Client A, B and C in **one** filing, with the note that each is marked "Files own application". BR-11 (concept :49) then gives each person their own application number, and frame 9 (:397) gives each person their own decision. Team AG-7-45 says a filing is "one application to one authority". It also gives each filing its own number (`Filing.applicationNumber`), its own decision (`Decision.filingId`) and its own status (OPEN · SUBMITTED · DECIDED · WITHDRAWN); a filing can be withdrawn on its own (BR-31; AG-07 :1936–1949). SA-1B-74 gives each family member their own number, fee and decision. P-119 says "Files own application" marks a person as an **applicant**. *What breaks:* Client C is refused while A and B are approved. The concept's single filing then has no status to show, cannot withdraw C alone, and cannot hold three decisions. BR-31's "After decision when every filing is decided" stops working. §9.1's "tell the team" is a divergence we create ourselves, not one the team made. Q1 (concept :115) asks "one or several filings per case?", but both sides already say several. *Fix:* adopt the team's grain. There is **one filing for each person marked "Files own application"**. People who do not file their own (for example an accompanying spouse or child on a PR application) are applicants on the principal's filing. The family's filings show together in one Prepare view: the filing chip reads "Visitor visa · 3 filings", with a person filter. They are recorded together in one action (BR-31 already provides this). The application number then sits per filing, as both the team and IRCC have it, and §9.1 goes away. Rewrite Q1 (below). *Needed.* Confidence: high on the facts. Medium on IRCC numbering per program (Sample; for the expert).

## Questions for the founder (one at a time, recommendation first)
1. **What is a "Filing"?** In plain words: a filing is one application that IRCC gives its own application number and its own decision. *Recommend:* each person who files their own application gets their own filing. A visiting family of three is three filings, prepared on one screen and sent together in one action. A PR application with an accompanying spouse and child is one filing with three people on it. One case holds all of the family's filings. This is the team's AG-7-45 and BR-31, and it gives each person their own number and decision (SA-1B-74). *Alternative:* the concept's one filing per family submission, with numbers and decisions per person inside it. That diverges from the team's filing status, its withdraw-one-filing rule and its "After decision" rule.
2. **Does an out-of-date signed, uploaded or entered item, or a missing required signature, block "Ready to file"?** *Recommend yes,* as a narrow amendment to P-71: sending IRCC a signed form that disagrees with the record, or an unsigned one, gets the application returned. AI findings and missing documents still only warn. *Alternative:* keep P-71 as written and show these as warnings. (This replaces the route question. P-122 stands, with the combination's portal shown read-only: M2.)
3. **Must date of birth, passport number and expiry, when read from a document, each get a "Matches page" click before Confirm section?** Names already need one (P-134 D2). *Recommend yes,* together with values read from handwritten or foreign civil documents (report D5): accepting must not be easier than correcting. *Alternative:* the whole section in one action, with those values highlighted.
