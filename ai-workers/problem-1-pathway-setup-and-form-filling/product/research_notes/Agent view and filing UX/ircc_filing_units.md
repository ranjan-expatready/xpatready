# How IRCC groups a family's applications ("filings"), 29 Oct 2026

Research only. **Filing** = one application that the government gives its own application number and its own decision. All programs, forms, fees and routes below are **Sample until the immigration expert confirms**; legal points are **Unconfirmed: no counsel**. Fees are from IRCC's fee list ([fees](https://ircc.canada.ca/english/information/fees/fees.asp), 2026-09-21) and change often.

## 2. Exceptions to "one filing per person who files"

The founder's leaning holds for temporary residence and citizenship. It breaks in six places:

1. **Permanent residence: one filing covers the whole family** (rows 4, 4c, 5, 6). The spouse and children are applicants on the principal's filing: one application number, one decision letter for the file, one payment, but per-person forms (IMM 5669 each adult, IMM 5406), per-person fees, medicals and confirmations of PR. Here SA-1B-74 is wrong and AG-7-45's "applicants with roles" is right.
2. **People on a filing who are not applying.** A non-accompanying spouse or child is declared and must be examined (medical; forms where asked), pays no fee and gets no visa. Leaving one out has a lasting cost: the guide warns a PR who does not declare all family members "could risk losing their permanent resident status" (Guide 5289; also IRPR 117(9)(d), Unconfirmed: no counsel).
3. **Two applications from two people in one indivisible submission** (sponsorship, rows 5, 6, 6b). The sponsor files their own application, which is assessed on its own (sponsor eligibility), but it cannot be sent or paid without the PR application, and the fee is a bundle on the sponsored person. Whether IRCC gives the sponsorship its own number is open (Q4).
4. **Filings that wait for another filing.** The provincial nomination before the eAPR (4b); the in-Canada spousal open work permit after the PR filing's AOR (5b); the PR application after a PGP invitation or an Express Entry invitation. They belong to the same case but are not "sent together".
5. **Filings by someone outside the family.** The employer's LMIA (ESDC) or offer of employment (IRCC Employer Portal) has its own number, fee and decision, but its applicant is the employer (Corporate pathway). It is a separate case, linked, not a filing on the family case (P-106).
6. **One person, several filings at once, or one filing giving several documents.** A spouse in Canada can hold a PR filing and an open work permit filing at once (different portals). Conversely, a visa or eTA issued with a first study or work permit is **not** a filing ("no separate application"); nor are biometrics or a medical. They are **steps** on a filing with their own fees (concept BR-8).

Fees: the visitor visa cap ($500 for 5+) and biometrics cap ($170 for 2+) need "same time and place"; the principal pays the "Family Rate", the others are fee-exempt. Split submissions lose the cap ([2019 fee instruction](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/publications-manuals/operational-bulletins-manuals/fees/immigration/e-apps-issues-regarding-family-member-applicants.html)).

Paper and citizenship add a sending rule: one incomplete application returns the whole envelope or group.

## 3. What our configuration and rule engine must hold

### 3a. Per combination (configuration, extends P-119 "Who can be in this application")
For each person role in the combination, one of three **filing modes**, plus a count:
* **Files own application**: this role gets its own filing, on a combination named for it (see below). Temporary residence and citizenship.
* **On the principal's filing, applying**: forms, documents, checks and fees repeat per person on the one filing. PR spouse and children.
* **On the principal's filing, declared only** (not accompanying): only the forms and checks marked for non-applying people (family information, medical) and no fee. A per-person condition chosen on the case ("Coming to Canada: yes / no"), not a new role.

Plus, per combination:
* **Linked combinations for "files own application" roles**: which combinations each role may open (study permit → spouse: open work permit or visitor; child: visitor or study permit), each released with its own forms, fees and pin (AG-7-45 rule 4); the officer picks per person on the case.
* **Co-filed applications.** The sponsorship combination declares a companion: "the sponsor files the sponsorship application in the same submission". It has its own forms (IMM 1344, IMM 5532, IMM 5476 for the sponsor) and its own applicant (the sponsor role). A second filing that **cannot be sent alone**.
* **Follows** (nomination → eAPR; invitation → PR; PR AOR → in-Canada open work permit) with a trigger: approved, invitation received, AOR received. **Supported by** (LMIA or offer of employment from a Corporate case) stays a case link (P-106).
* **Fee lines per role and mode** (principal, spouse, child, open work permit holder fee, right of PR fee, family cap with its threshold and "same time and place" condition, biometrics per person and cap). Values per release, Sample.
* **Steps that are not filings**: biometrics, medical (who: every family member 18+ or all, accompanying or not), a visa issued with a permit. Each with an owner person and a fee line.
* **Portal and channel** come from the combination (P-122). Two that matter here: citizenship by a representative is paper only; an in-Canada spousal open work permit goes in a different account from the PR filing.

### 3b. Per case (what the rule engine derives)
1. **How many filings, and whose.** One per person in "files own application" mode, each on its linked combination; one filing per PR combination with all its applying and declared people; one co-filed sponsorship filing; refusal of a role not allowed or above the maximum (P-119).
2. **Which forms on each filing, per person**: form lines × role × mode × conditions (age: IMM 5707 only for 18+; marital status: IMM 5409; not accompanying: medical and family information only).
3. **Which fees, on whose filing, and who pays**: per-person lines; the family cap applied only when the filings are in one submission and the people are spouse or dependent children; the cap paid on the principal's filing, others marked fee-exempt (the APR "Family Rate" words); the bundled sponsorship fee on the sponsored person's filing.
4. **Submission groups.** Which filings go together: the family's temporary residence filings in one submission (offered, not forced; the officer may split, with a warning that the family cap is lost); sponsorship + PR always together (cannot split); citizenship as a group with the warning that one incomplete file returns all. One "record the submission" per group, one number per filing (BR-31, concept BR-11).
5. **Sequence and waits**: filings that cannot start ("Waits for the AOR on Client A's PR filing"), and "Start next stage" offered on the trigger (AG-7-45 rule 2).

### 3c. The agent screen
One case, a list of filings, a person filter still holds (concept BR-1, P-122). The chip counts filings, not people ("Express Entry · 1 filing · 4 people"; "Visitor visa · 4 filings"); a sponsorship shows inside its partner's group; a waiting filing says what it waits for.
