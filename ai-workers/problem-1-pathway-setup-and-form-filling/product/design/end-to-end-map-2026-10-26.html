Numbers are the reading order of one form's journey: ① build path → ② set channels → ③/④ get sources in (PDF upload / extension capture) → ⑤ second person approves held items → ⑥ AI proposes field maps (+ ⑦ the rest of the masters) → ⑧ author curates → ⑨ second person approves new masters → ⑩ submit → expert → release → ⑪–⑬ assemble SQR per person → ⑭ case opened, client answers once → ⑮ engine validates, officer confirms → ⑯–⑰ engine fills PDF + extension fills portal → ⑱–⑳ new editions, filing, staying current.

What AI is not creating yet (designed, but still needing a person or unbuilt): sections, document types/checklist lines, conditions and requirement rules, tier-1/2 validations and checks, PQR picks, "describe what you need" drafting, and new-combination proposals — AI drafts for all of these are designed but not built; today AI only maps form fields to shared fields. Until they land, a person supplies those masters by hand. And by rule, AI never creates: sources (a person uploads/captures), immigration values (the expert), approvals and releases (two people), case facts and form values (client + officer), and keys (generated by the system).
The one rule that makes it safe: AI output exists only as a proposal beside its source passage. The engine writes to the draft library only what a person accepted; new masters pass a second person; releases publish them. So the shared library — the canonical master data every combination reuses ("34 reused · 12 new") — stays deduplicated, auditable and reversible, and AI can be replayed at US$0 for the demo.
