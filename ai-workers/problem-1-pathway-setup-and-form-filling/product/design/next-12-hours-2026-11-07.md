## 5. Where the limit really is

From the Theory of Constraints: find the one limit, and don't spend on the others.

| Resource | Capacity | Used | Limit? |
|---|---|---|---|
| GitHub machines | Pro plan 40 jobs at once, free on a public repo ([GitHub](https://docs.github.com/en/actions/reference/limits)). The plan name itself is the founder's word; the token does not show it | About 8 runs at a look | No |
| Merge queue | 3 batches of up to 6, about 32 minutes each: up to ~33 PRs an hour | About 1 to 2 an hour | No |
| Weekly plan | 0% used, 5 d 15 h left | About 2.1%/h at 6 lanes this morning (44% to 49% in 2.4 hours) | Not this block: ~30% |
| Mac | 13 GB free, load 1.4, memory tight | Lead, demo, qa, standbys | Keep builders in the cloud |
| Files many rows touch | One owner per file (plan section 4) | Sets how many lanes can run at once | **Yes, for building** |
| qa on `main` | One lane, about an hour per capability | Up to 3 can land together | **Yes, from about 16:00** |
| Founder-named writes | Only while he is present | Blocks labels and dispatches | **Yes, when he is away** |

DORA's research backs what we already do: small batches and frequent merges go with better results, and change failure rate is the proxy for rework ([2023 report](https://dora.dev/research/2023/dora-report/2023-dora-accelerate-state-of-devops-report.pdf), [metrics history](https://dora.dev/insights/dora-metrics-history/)). It says nothing direct on limits for work in progress, so our seat limit stands on our own measurements. Mergify says frequent batch splits point to a batch that is too large and publishes no optimal formula ([batches](https://docs.mergify.com/merge-queue/batches)), so we change batch settings only on our own numbers. Our failures so far come from rows clashing rather than from batch size.
