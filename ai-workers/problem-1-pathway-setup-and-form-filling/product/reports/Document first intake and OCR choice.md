# Read documents first, confirm every value

The best way to collect data for IRCC family cases is to read the documents first and confirm every value. Each person's documents (passport, birth certificate, marriage certificate, letters, test reports) are read into **proposed values**. A named person confirms each value before it counts. The client questionnaire then asks only what no document supplies, or what a document failed to supply. This matches P-130 (document first), P-62 (AI never sets a value) and P-72 (only confirmed facts fill forms). Three findings shape the design. First, **no Canadian competitor found reads uploaded documents into IMM form fields**. Canadian tools push data they already hold out to IRCC portals and PDFs. The nearest model is a US product in closed beta (Docketwise), which shows the right pattern: an upload request inside a questionnaire section, a field-by-field review, then one "Confirm & Import". Second, the rules fall into three layers. What a document supplies is the same everywhere. Whether it is required depends on the combination, the person's role and age, and sometimes the visa office. Third, **no single AWS product fits**. Textract runs in Canada but reads identity documents only for US-issued IDs, and handwriting only in English. Claude on Bedrock reads any language and layout, but from Canada its processing may run in the US. The recommendation is a **hybrid**: free, rule-based passport checks; Textract in Canada for text and tables; Claude for letters and foreign civil documents once the founder approves cross-border processing; and a person in front of every disagreement. That is a founder decision, not a default.

*Plain words used below.* **OCR** (optical character recognition) is software that turns a picture of text into text. **LLM vision** means a large language model, such as Claude, that looks at the page image and writes out the values it sees. **MRZ** (machine-readable zone) is the two lines of `<<<` text at the bottom of a passport photo page. It carries built-in check digits, small arithmetic codes that reveal a misread. **Provenance** is the record of where a value came from, who confirmed it and when. **PQR / SQR** are the pre-qualification and client questionnaires; the **agent view** is the full working view the licensed representative uses. Every immigration value in this report is **Sample until the immigration expert confirms**. Legal points are marked **Unconfirmed: no counsel**.

## Five facts decide most of each person's document list

Across the six demo combinations, the official checklists turn on the same few facts about each person. Those facts are: age 18 or over, marital status, accompanying children, a minor travelling without both parents, and any "yes" on a background question. Each combination has a published checklist: IMM 5484 for visitor and super visa ([IMM 5484](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/ircc/documents/pdf/english/kits/forms/imm5484/01-05-2026/imm5484e.pdf)), IMM 5483 for study ([IMM 5483](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/ircc/documents/pdf/english/kits/forms/imm5483/01-08-2025/imm5483e.pdf)), the work-permit kit page ([Work permit outside: forms and documents](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/work-canada/permit-outside/forms-documents.html)) and IMM 5533 for spouse sponsorship ([IMM 5533](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/ircc/documents/pdf/english/kits/forms/imm5533/01-09-2022/imm5533e.pdf)). Express Entry has no fixed checklist. The online system builds a personal one from the profile answers and **"won't let you submit" until every item is in** ([Apply for PR through Express Entry](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/apply-permanent-residence.html)). The provincial nominee variant differs only by the nomination certificate ([Express Entry: Documents](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents.html)). Visa offices add a layer on top. New Delhi's IMM 5881 asks for two years of tax returns, six months of bank statements and an employer leave letter giving name, position, salary and date of hire ([IMM 5881](https://ircc.canada.ca/english/pdf/kits/forms/IMM5881E.pdf)).

The table shows, per person in a family case, the documents that supply form fields and the fields they fill. It is a Sample until the immigration expert confirms.

| Person | Document (when) | Fields it supplies |
|---|---|---|
| Every applicant (principal, spouse, each child) | Passport information page (always) | Family and given names, sex, date of birth, city and country of birth, citizenship, passport number, issuing country, issue and expiry dates. Name and date of birth also fill that person's row on IMM 5707/5645. Entry stamps give previous Canadian entries; old passports give travel history |
| Every applicant who has one | National ID; US green card; residence permit (if country of residence differs from citizenship) | ID number and dates; US PR number and expiry; country of residence, status and dates |
| Principal applicant and spouse | Marriage certificate (if married); divorce, annulment or death certificate (if previously married) | Marital status, marriage date, spouse's name; previous spouse's names and dates. For sponsorship, the marriage date also decides how much relationship evidence is needed |
| Each child (and every person, for PR) | Birth certificate | Child's name, sex, date and place of birth; **both parents' names**. The date of birth fixes age, which triggers IMM 5707, police certificates (18+), custodianship (study, under 17) and dependency proof (22+) |
| Principal applicant (and payer, host or sponsor) | Employment letter, pay slips, bank statement or bank letter, tax notice of assessment (NOA) | Current and past jobs, dates, title, employer; funds available; host or sponsor income (NOA line 15000) |

The mapping from IMM 1294 and 1295 fields to the letter of acceptance and job offer is by field name, an inference: no official source spells it out.

The passport carries the most weight. IRCC tells applicants to copy names **"exactly as shown on your passport or travel document (even if the name is misspelled)"** ([Guide 5256](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/application-forms-guides/guide-5256-applying-visitor-visa-temporary-resident-visa.html)). That makes the passport the natural proposed source for identity fields. Three kinds of fact must stay with people, not documents. The background and health questions (tuberculosis, refusals, criminality, military service) are client-only declarations; police certificates and medical exams must never answer them. Contact details, intentions, siblings and "will accompany" are client facts. Some facts are mixed: funds available, purpose of visit, and countries lived in for six months or more. For those the right pattern is **"client answers, document checks"**, not auto-fill. Two things remain open. Four checklists and six forms (IMM 5488, 5556, 5589, 5707, 5406, 0008 and others) are encrypted PDFs that could not be read on this Mac, so their lines come from guides. It is also unconfirmed whether an accompanying Express Entry spouse needs a language test or ECA.

## Offer "fill from your documents" per section, never silently

The current pattern is an **opt-in, per-section upload request for a named person, with nothing written until a person confirms**. In Docketwise's AI Data Capture, the firm adds a document request to a questionnaire section and assigns it to one contact. Processing starts on upload. A "Review & Edit" screen lists each extracted value, which can be edited or ignored, and data reaches the record **only on "Confirm & Import"**. Supported types are passport, green card, work authorisation and I-94, and the feature is in closed beta ([Docketwise Support](https://supportcenter.docketwise.com/en/articles/10247778-docketwise-iq-smart-docs)). TurboTax works the same way for tax slips: photograph the W-2, or type it in if the import fails, and the user remains "responsible for verifying the accuracy" ([TurboTax help](https://ttlc.intuit.com/questions/2942107); [TurboTax community](https://ttlc.intuit.com/community/taxes/discussion/import-w-2/00/2439261)). On the Canadian side, Visto and VisaFlo fill IRCC portals and IMM PDFs from client profiles they already hold. Neither was found to read documents into fields ([Canadian Lawyer](https://www.canadianlawyermag.com/practice-areas/immigration/vistoai-using-chatgpt-ai-as-a-first-step-to-enhance-visa-application-process/374471); [VisaFlo blog](https://inblog.ai/vflo/applications-manually)). **That gap is the opening for the product.**

Applied to P-130, each person's questionnaire starts with that person's checklist rather than with questions: "Upload Client A's passport and we will fill this section for you." For a client, an upload produces proposals that wait for review. For staff, values are approved inline beside the page. This is what the team's own requirement AG-8-41 specifies: values offered "with provenance and confidence in a review view that shows the document region beside the value", and the reader "SHALL NOT itself mark any fact verified" (team requirements AG-08 v1.10, local snapshot). P-72 says a client's own typed answers count as confirmed for the client's fields. A client's review of an extracted value can therefore confirm it as the client's declaration. It becomes **document-verified only when staff approve it against the page**. Questions a document supplies stay hidden in the client questionnaire (SQR). They reappear prefilled with "please confirm" only when the checklist line is missing or the reader could not read that field. A parent uploads for a minor child: the child is the subject, the parent is the uploader, and both are recorded. Under P-78 the parent's confirmation counts for a minor. An adult spouse must confirm their own values, or the agent must (Unconfirmed: no counsel). In PR and sponsorship files, dependants aged 18 or over sign their own forms ([IRCC Help Centre](https://ircc.canada.ca/english/helpcentre/answer.asp?qnum=740); [LawyerInfo](https://lawyerinfo.ca/guides/immigration-visas/family-sponsorship/does-a-handwritten-vs-digital-signature-matter-for-ircc-sponsorship/)).

The review screen design matters more than the reader's accuracy. A 2026 experiment with **2,784 people** checking AI-extracted values found two effects. When correcting a value took more effort than accepting it, people made fewer corrections and **accepted more wrong values**. People who favoured automation also caught fewer errors ([HDSR: Bias in the Loop](https://hdsr.mitpress.mit.edu/pub/nrcn4h7d)). So correcting must take one click and an in-place edit beside a crop of the document region, never a separate form. Identity fields (names, date of birth, passport number, expiry) should always be shown one by one. Low-risk, high-confidence fields can be confirmed a section at a time. Handwritten and foreign civil documents should always be reviewed field by field.

## A mismatch is a check for a person, not an overwrite and not fraud

The order of entry changes what the user sees, but never the rule. **Upload first:** nothing exists yet, so the extracted value is shown as a proposal and written only on confirmation. **Typed first, uploaded later:** the system compares field by field. If the values match, approval upgrades the typed value to document-verified and records the document as evidence. If they differ, both values stay, side by side with the document page, until the agent resolves the difference with a reason. Identity-check vendors work the same way. Onfido marks a name mismatch as **"Caution", not fraud**, because a mismatch does not "necessarily point to a fraudulent document" ([Onfido Document report](https://developers.onfido.com/guide/document-report/)). Persona lets a firm decide whether a comparison is a hard fail or only an alert ([Persona: Configuring Inquiry Comparison](https://help.withpersona.com/articles/2zXa85BILxtncnwdIj8jge/)). The team's requirement AG-8-23 already says a self-declared value "SHALL not silently overwrite a document-verified value" and must raise a conflict for consultant review. Documents can be wrong or out of date too: TurboTax users found imported W-2s that differed from the paper copy because the employer had reissued them ([TurboTax community](https://ttlc.intuit.com/community/taxes/discussion/import-w-2/00/2439261)). So "the document wins" should mean **"the document's value is shown first, and the conflict is still shown"**.

Name comparisons need rules first, or they flood the agent with false alarms. A passport's MRZ allows only the letters A to Z and the filler `<`, may cut long names short, and drops apostrophes (O'CONNOR becomes OCONNOR). A non-Latin name must appear in Latin letters in the printed zone ([ICAO Doc 9303 Part 3](https://icao.int/publications/Documents/9303_p3_cons_en.pdf); [SEVIS name standards](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/sevis-help-hub/sevis-basics/system-data-entry-standards/name-standards)). Arabic names have been written into the MRZ under several spelling schemes, which causes mismatches across databases ([ICAO TAG-MRTD paper](https://www.icao.int:443/Meetings/TAG-MRTD/Documents/Tag-Mrtd-20/TagMrtd-20_WP017_en.pdf)). The rule: take the number, dates and sex from the MRZ after its check digits pass. Take the full name from the printed zone. Flag only differences the ICAO rules cannot explain.

Replacements, conflicts between documents, and corrections all follow one principle: **append, never overwrite, and keep who did what**. Master-data tools pick a "winning" value automatically using trust scores that decay over time ([Informatica](https://docs.informatica.com/master-data-management/multidomain-mdm/10-4/overview-guide/key-concepts/batch-processing/load-process.html)). A filing for a licensed representative needs something different: a fixed order that decides which value is shown first (document-verified, then person-confirmed, then declared, then proposed), a person's override with a reason, and "why this value" beside the field. Informatica also lets stewards override a value and see the reason behind each field ([Informatica: best version of the truth](https://onlinehelp.informatica.com/IICS/prod/mde/en/cg-c360-bvt-records/Establish_the_best_version_of_the_truth.html)). The W3C provenance standard adds the remaining columns: who acted, through which channel, derived from which document page, revising which earlier value, and acting on behalf of whom ([W3C PROV-DM](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-dm/)). The cases play out as follows.

| Situation | What happens |
|---|---|
| New passport replaces old | The old passport is kept as history, because IRCC asks for previous passports and travel. The checklist line moves to the new one. Every value verified from the old passport is marked "evidence replaced" and keeps filling drafts. Values read from the new passport that match re-point their evidence; values that differ open a conflict. Forms built on the old values are listed for review |
| Two documents disagree (passport and birth certificate show different dates of birth) | A document-disagreement conflict. Neither value is chosen automatically. The agent picks one with a reason, and the other stays on record |
| Correction after confirmation | A new value row that supersedes the old one, with the reason. A declared value replacing a document-verified one goes through a conflict (AG-8-23) |
| Correction after a form was signed or filed | The locked form never changes. The case shows "filed with an earlier value", and the agent decides whether to re-sign or tell IRCC. Whether IRCC must be told is **Unconfirmed: no counsel** |
| Translated document | The certified translation is read and linked to its original. It becomes stale when the original changes or expires: IRCC says a translation "remains valid indefinitely unless the original document changes or expires". Translations by the applicant, family members or the representative are not accepted, so the platform should block them ([IRCC: Translation](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/publications-manuals/operational-bulletins-manuals/standard-requirements/translation.html)) |
| One document serves two cases | The document belongs to the person, and each case has its own checklist line pointing to it. One verification serves both, and each filing keeps its own snapshot |

## Rules live in three places: document type, combination, and the person's facts

Configurable case platforms all split document rules the same way. Salesforce's document model has a **global document-type master**, a **decision rule** that decides which types apply ([DocumentDecisionRequirement](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/atlas.en-us.psc_api.meta/psc_api/sforce_api_objects_documentdecisionrequirement.htm)), and a **per-case checklist item** with its own status (New, Pending, Accepted, Rejected) ([DocumentChecklistItem](https://developer.salesforce.com/docs/atlas.en-us.psc_api.meta/psc_api/sforce_psc_api_objects_documentchecklistitem.htm)). Docketwise ties extraction to the document type, not to the case type ([Docketwise Support](https://supportcenter.docketwise.com/en/articles/10247778-docketwise-iq-smart-docs)). IRCC's checklists follow the same logic. The passport page is described identically on IMM 5484, 5483 and 5533. What varies by combination is the requirement: a valid passport for a visitor visa, an expired one acceptable at submission for PR, and "renew" if it expires within six months for Express Entry ([IMM 5881](https://ircc.canada.ca/english/pdf/kits/forms/IMM5881E.pdf); [IMM 5533](https://www.canada.ca/content/dam/ircc/documents/pdf/english/kits/forms/imm5533/01-09-2022/imm5533e.pdf); [Express Entry: Documents](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/immigrate-canada/express-entry/documents.html)).

The answer to "document type master, combination, or person role?" is therefore **all three, each for one job**. The **document type master** (configured once in the library) says what the document is, how it is read, which facts it supplies, and **about whom**. A birth certificate supplies the child's date of birth and also the parents' names. Our prototype's `suppliesFacts` list cannot say that today. It should become a small table with a subject per fact ("holder", "holder's mother", "holder's spouse"), flags for "the reader can extract it" and "must be verified before filing", and which fact is the expiry date. A passport supplies the same facts whoever holds it, so this must **not** vary by combination or role. The **combination requirement** says whether the document is needed, for which roles, under which condition, how many, how old it may be, and which alternatives count, such as "any one of PR card, CoPR or citizenship certificate". The team's code already has most of these fields (`primacy`, `alternativeGroupId`, `expiryRuleJson`, `appliesToRoles` as a list). Our prototype should align to them. **Person role and age** are not a separate store. They are conditions on the requirement, evaluated against that person's facts, which is how one checklist per person grows and shrinks as answers arrive (team decision SA1B-D15). A **visa-office layer** (New Delhi IMM 5881/5905) sits on top for local additions. P-130's per-fact "Comes from: Document · type" stays on the combination. A release check should refuse any fact marked as coming from document X unless X's master says it supplies that fact and X is required, or accepted as an alternative, for that role. What is missing today is the case side, which the team's code calls "explicitly OUT of this wave". That case side is: the document owned by the person, the checklist line on the case, the reader's proposals kept apart from answers, and the team's conflict record.

## Textract stays in Canada but cannot read most immigration documents

Amazon Textract has a Canada (Central) endpoint. Basic text reading costs **US$0.0015 a page**, form reading US$0.05, table reading US$0.015, and its identity-document reader US$0.025 ([Textract pricing](https://aws.amazon.com/textract/pricing/); [Textract endpoints](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/general/latest/gr/textract.html)). Three limits rule it out as the only reader. Its identity reader is documented for **"identity documentation issued by the US Government"** only ([AnalyzeID docs](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/textract/latest/dg/how-it-works-identity.html)). Handwriting, identity documents and question-style extraction work in **English only**, and printed text in six European languages ([Textract FAQ](https://aws.amazon.com/textract/faqs/)). Default throughput in Canada is one request a second ([Textract endpoints](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/general/latest/gr/textract.html)). That excludes Hindi, Punjabi, Chinese, Arabic, Tagalog and Farsi civil documents, which are common in Canadian files. Bedrock Data Automation, AWS's AI-based document product, has the same six-language limit ([BDA limits](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/bda-limits.html)), and its availability in Canada was not confirmed. One more point: by default, Textract may keep inputs to improve the service unless the AWS account opts out ([Textract FAQ](https://aws.amazon.com/textract/faqs/)). The opt-out should be switched on from day one.

Claude on Bedrock reads any layout and language, but **Claude 5.5 models are not hosted inside the Canada region**. They are reachable only through cross-region routing ([Bedrock model regions](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/models-region-compatibility.html)). AWS states that with this routing, stored data, logs and settings stay in Canada, while **"inference processing (the transient computation) may occur in another Region"**, in the US or elsewhere ([AWS ML blog](https://aws.amazon.com/blogs/machine-learning/accelerate-generative-ai-innovation-in-canada-with-amazon-bedrock-cross-region-inference)). Model providers have no access to prompts or outputs ([Bedrock data protection](https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/data-protection.html)). A Canada-only routing profile exists for Bedrock's safety filters ([Bedrock guardrails](https://docs.aws.eu/bedrock/latest/userguide/guardrails-cross-region-support.html)). Whether one exists for Claude is unconfirmed; one command checks it (`aws bedrock list-inference-profiles --region ca-central-1`). On accuracy, an independent 300-document benchmark found classic OCR level with frontier models on clean printed text (**Textract 95%, Azure 96%**). Models led on handwriting, where scores ranged from **46% to 95%** ([AIMultiple](https://aimultiple.com/ocr-accuracy)). No benchmark covers immigration documents, so the team must measure on its own sample set.

The privacy position (**Unconfirmed: no counsel**) is that US processing is probably permitted but carries duties. The Privacy Commissioner treats sending data to a processor as a "use", not a disclosure. The firm stays accountable, must secure comparable protection by contract, and must tell clients in plain language that their data may be processed abroad ([OPC guidelines](https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/airports-and-borders/gl_dab_090127/)). The CICC Code requires licensees to keep client information confidential "indefinitely", and allows disclosure to people assisting the licensee ([Code s.28](https://laws-lois.justice.gc.ca/eng/regulations/SOR-2022-128/section-28.html)). Quebec's Law 25 and the CICC record-keeping by-laws were not checked.

The design that holds up is layered, and it fits P-62. Passports go through the MRZ first: parse it and verify the check digits with rules, at no cost per page and with no AI. A passing MRZ is strong evidence for number, dates, nationality and sex. Fixed-layout documents (NOA, test reports, ECA, bank tables) go through Textract text and tables in Canada, followed by rule checks such as balance arithmetic and score ranges. Letters, invitations and foreign civil records go through Claude, which must cite the page and quote the words behind each value so a reviewer can check it. Any disagreement or low confidence goes to a person. AWS's human-review service shows the routing: per-field thresholds (99 for critical keys and 90 generally in its example) plus random audit samples ([AWS A2I](https://docs.amazonaws.cn/en_us/sagemaker/latest/dg/a2i-get-started-console.md)). One tension needs the founder: P-62 says AI is used "only for unstructured sources, never for identity". Under that rule the passport's printed name (needed for non-Latin and truncated names) would be read by Textract plus rules, or typed by a person, not read by Claude.

## Recommendation and the founder's decisions

**Recommendation.** Build document-first intake as a per-person checklist that opens each client questionnaire section. Each section offers "fill this from your documents". Every read value is a proposal, the client confirms their own declarations, staff verify against the page, and nothing overwrites a verified value without a recorded conflict and reason. Keep the rules in three places: the global document type master (what it supplies and about whom), the combination requirement (when, for which role, how many, how old, which alternatives), and person facts as conditions, with a visa-office layer on top. For reading, start with what stays in Canada and needs no AI decision: MRZ rules for passports, Textract in Canada for printed text and tables, and Textract's AI-services opt-out switched on. Add Claude for letters and non-English civil documents after the decision below, or as soon as a Canada-only Claude profile is confirmed. Before committing, measure Textract plus rules against Claude Haiku and Sonnet, per field, on **50–100 synthetic or consented documents per kind**. Throughout, rule out **trust scores, automatic winners and silent extraction**.

## Conclusion

The hard part of document-first intake is not reading documents. It is deciding, field by field, who may turn a reading into a fact, and making a correction as quick as an acceptance. The team's requirements (AG-8-23, AG-8-41) and the founder's decisions (P-62, P-72, P-78, P-130) already answer most of that. Two pieces are missing: the case side (the person's document, the checklist line, proposals kept apart from answers, and conflicts) and a "supplies about whom" table on the document type. Those can be added without new concepts. The technology choice is narrower than it looks. Textract alone cannot read most of a typical Canadian family file, and Claude alone raises a cross-border question. The cheapest, most trustworthy check, passport MRZ check digits, needs no AI at all. Since no Canadian competitor reads documents into IMM forms, the gap is real. The firm that fills it with visible provenance and honest conflict handling, rather than accuracy claims, should earn representatives' trust first.
