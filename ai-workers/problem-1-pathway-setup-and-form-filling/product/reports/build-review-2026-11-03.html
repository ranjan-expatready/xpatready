M2 Works over time

M2 rows scored Works in each commit of the scorecard. The number of M2 rows itself grew from 24 to 60 as rows were written, so early points are out of a smaller total.

By area, in plain words

  • Setup: an author builds Program › Category › Sub-category in one pop-up (duplicates refused), sees all 41 combinations with their trail, status and one next step, copies or removes form and document lines across combinations, and sets copies, minimum age and signer.
  • Sources: an author uploads an IRCC PDF; its code, edition and checksum are read without AI; a file not on the approved list is held until a second person approves it; a new edition is held for impact review; checklists become document proposals.
  • Library: AI proposes how each form box maps to a shared field and a reviewer accepts, edits or rejects; merge with aliases, retire, delete (blocked while in use), rename (a second person when a released combination uses it) and a validator work; a near-duplicate's reason is shown at release.
  • Questionnaires: per-person questionnaires, section order, Pre-qualification marking, who answers each question, and a Preview per person on a sample family.
  • Release: author submits, the immigration expert ticks, a different person approves and releases; a release is refused for a published number without a source or a missing answer-key test; sample content releases only as a visible "Demo release".
  • Cases and forms (M3, thin): an officer opens a family case, the client answers on a link, fees and filings are derived, and IMM 5257 fills 84 of 161 boxes, all matching the facts. The form's own Adobe validation has not been run (no Acrobat on this Mac). Signed PDFs 13/14.
  • Not built: AI reading of uploaded documents, the independent AI review of a filled form, stale-marking when facts change, the browser extension (parked), the prospect runtime (team-owned).

7. Lessons, by strength of evidence

Rule used here, taken from the research below: change how we work only when a measure moves outside its normal range, stays on one side of its middle for 8 or more points, or two or more independent incidents share a cause. Anything seen once is logged, not acted on.

firm · keepOne fresh cloud builder per row, merged by the queue, multiplied delivery about 15 times without more rework.

0.25 → ~4 product merges an hour; fix-titled share flat at ~23%; no reverts; 0 open Blocker/Critical.

firm · fix firstOur own scorecard overstated what works: 12 of 52 "Works" rows fail their own acceptance pack.

An independent re-run on main confirms 36 of 60 M2 rows, not 52. At least 8 of the 12 are real defects; 3–4 are packs that were never updated after a later decision (P-266, P-279), which is itself a gap: a row should not count as Works while its pack is red or out of date. Score only on a green pack, re-tested by someone who did not score it.

firm · changeThe limit now is review and merge, not builders or machines.

Approval-to-merge median rose 41 → 54 → 71 → 76 min as volume rose, and 40 machines did not shorten it. DORA 2025 and a 10,000-developer telemetry study report the same pattern: more AI changes, longer review.

firm · changeEach product change is fully tested twice, and half of all runs are thrown away.

~27 min on the PR, ~27 min again in the queue batch since 1 Nov 23:16; 50% of runs cancelled on 2 Nov, 104 on records PRs. One full test per change, in the queue, would roughly halve machine time.

firm · changeA large Lead context is the most expensive thing we run locally.

US$869 (29%), 99% cache re-reads of ~300k tokens per message. A short Lead context, or handing over earlier, costs less with no loss of work.

firm · changePaid AI runs are the gate for M2, and only a founder-typed turn can start them.

5 of the 8 M2 rows left wait on paid runs; every timed check overnight could only leave a card.

firm · watchHow we work changed almost daily.

46% of 182 decisions are about the way of working (33 on 2 Nov); records PRs are 31–42% of merges. That they slowed delivery is not proven: records cost queue slots and cancelled runs, but the effect was not isolated.

not supported"Speed is faster when the founder is not talking to the Lead."

Merges per hour were 4.0 in the founder's daytime on 2 Nov and 3.1 overnight without him; 1 Nov (135 founder turns) and 2 Nov (194) were the two fastest days. The data does not show that founder involvement slows delivery.

too little dataqa per batch and Sonnet reviewers (P-277, P-278).

Only 1.5 hours and 5 merges fall in the window before the next change; the one-day pilot cannot be judged yet.

gap · fix firstWe cannot see the cloud builders' tokens, stalls or tool errors.

Since 31 Oct most work ran in the cloud, which leaves no local transcript. Claude Code's OpenTelemetry export (cost, tokens, tool results, active time) would close this.

8. How to measure and run retrospectives from now

From primary sources (DORA 2024 and 2025, the SPACE framework, METR's 2025 and 2026 studies, Anthropic's own research and Claude Code docs, Google's SRE book, Atlassian's retrospective play, GitHub and Mergify docs). There is no published benchmark for 5–27 parallel coding agents, so our own numbers are the baseline.

  • Do not set targets on these numbers (DORA warns that a measure used as a goal gets gamed); use them to see change.
  • Weekly 30-minute look at trends; a 60-minute retrospective every two weeks (Atlassian format: gather, find patterns, actions with an owner and a date). A blameless incident review only on agreed triggers: a revert, a wrong merge, a spend spike.
  • Evidence threshold before any process change as stated in section 7; each change is a labelled experiment checked at the next retrospective.
  • Check what agents and people say against the logs: in METR's study experienced developers believed AI made them 20% faster when it made them 19% slower.
  • Cap work in progress so the review wait stops growing (Little's law: more open work means longer cycle time unless throughput rises).

9. Retiring Leads 1–6 without losing work

Branches on GitHub are never deleted, so no pushed work can disappear. Before each Lead is archived, its nested sessions are checked for anything not pushed, and every branch whose content is not on main is sorted into: already on main another way, records not needed, records worth keeping, or real unmerged work for the fresh backlog.
