## Key terms — cases and delivery

> **For writers and scripts (X-1 rule 4).** Each entry sits between an HTML comment that starts with "term:" and its id, and a closing comment "/term". A script copies these entries word for word into the "Start here" part of each epic. Never retype them. To change a term, change it here and run the script again. Where a term also has a row in the Glossary table above, that row is the long form and this entry is the short form; they must agree. Examples use the Client E family: Client B (principal), Client A (spouse), Client D (child, 8) and Client Q (Client B's brother, a related contact). Firm staff in the examples: Client F (licensed user, lead representative), Client C (case officer), Client I (case reviewer), Client G (a second licensed user) and Client H (a colleague who leaves). The dated examples follow one timeline: Client C creates case C-2026-00012 on Thu 5 Nov 2026, the filings are submitted on Fri 20 Nov 2026, and the decisions come on Wed 3 Mar 2027.
>
> **Order of the entries.** The entries follow the order in which a reader meets them in the Client E walk-through of the AG-07 v3.0 plan (section 4), not the order of the Glossary table. The entry "Delivery stage" is placed after "Stage" because the two are read together. Two terms are never used alone: write "delivery stage" or "government step", never "step" by itself.

<!-- term:case -->
**Case** (`Case`) — A case is the firm's work for one client in one destination country. It holds one or more filings and the people on them. *Example:* Case C-2026-00012 is the Client E family's move to Canada. It holds three filings. They are Client B's employer-specific work permit, Client A's open work permit and Client D's study permit. *Not the same as:* a filing, which is one application inside a case.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:client -->
**Client** (client file record and `Party`) — A client is the person or employer the firm works for. It is a person record, or an employer record, with a client file record added. *Example:* Client B became client CL-00001 when his retainer was signed by him and by the firm (one of the three ways to become a client, AG02-D2). *Not the same as:* an applicant, who is any person named on a filing.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:client-file-record -->
**Client file record** (`ClientFile`) — The client file record holds the firm's own details for one person or employer who is a lead or client: the lifecycle stage, the client number, the file owner and the office. It is AG-02's table; client fields never go on the person record (BUILD-1). It is created when a person becomes a lead, or when a person becomes a client directly through Add client, the client import, a work-permit case or "make this person a client" (BUILD-3). *Example:* Client B becomes a client. His record shows client number CL-00001, file owner Client C and his office. *Not the same as:* the person record, which holds name, email, phone and language.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:person-record -->
**Person record** (`Party`) — Every real person on a case has one person record, kept once and reused on all their cases. Their details, answers and consent are stored with it. *Example:* Client D, age 8, gets her own person record when she is added to the case. Her record gives her no portal login and no emails. *Not the same as:* the client file record, which holds the firm's own fields.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:contact-screen -->
**Contact** (no code name) — Contact is only the name of a screen where staff see a person. It is not a record type and not a second table. *Example:* Staff open Client B on the Contact screen. What they see comes from his person record and his client file record. *Not the same as:* a Contact table. None exists, so older text about a Contact table means the person record.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:client-type -->
**Client type** (`clientType`) — The client type is Individual or Corporate. It comes from the program's pathway. Both types use the same case design, not two separate ones. *Example:* The Client E case is Individual. An employer's LMIA file is Corporate. LMIA means labour market impact assessment. *Not the same as:* a separate corporate case type. None exists; it is a case with client type Corporate.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:filing -->
**Filing** (`Filing`) — A filing is one application to one government authority. It has its own program, forms, fees, deadlines, rules version, people and decision. *Example:* Client B's employer-specific work permit is one filing. Client A's open work permit and Client D's study permit are two more, in the same case. *Not the same as:* a case, which can hold several filings.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:applicant -->
**Applicant** (`CaseApplicant`) — An applicant is a person named on a filing, with a role: principal, spouse, child or other. The role belongs to the filing, so one person can be principal on one filing and spouse on another. *Example:* Client B is the principal applicant on his work permit filing. Client A is the principal applicant on her own filing, and Client D on hers. Spouse or child is used when a family member is included in someone else's filing. *Not the same as:* the client, who is the person or employer that hired the firm.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:related-contact -->
**Related contact** (`PartyRelationship`) — A related contact is a person linked to one case who is not an applicant. Examples are a brother, an HR contact or an agent. Staff who can edit cases add one, with a relationship. It uses the person record, so there is no separate table. *Example:* Client C adds Client Q, Client B's brother, with the relationship "brother". His email address now counts as linked to the case when mail is filed. *Not the same as:* an applicant, who is named on a filing.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:people-on-this-case -->
**People on this case** (no code name) — People on this case is a panel on the case screen that lists everyone on the case, with a **Copy on emails** tick beside each person (AG-7-97 field 3). Everyone on the case means the client, the applicants, the employer contacts of an employer client, the related contacts and the people on the copy list (AG-7-97 rule 1). *Example:* The Client E panel lists Client B, Client A, Client D and Client Q (brother). Client C ticks Copy on emails beside Client A. *Not the same as:* the case team (case officer, case reviewer, lead representative), which is firm staff; firm staff are never labelled "Not on this case" (AG-7-97 rule 1). *(added 25 Oct — CC-5)*
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:copy-on-emails -->
**Copy on emails** (`CaseCopy`) — Copy on emails is the case's list of people who get a copy (Cc) of the emails staff write on the case. System emails (Welcome, links, reminders, invoices, signing, portal invitation) go to their usual recipient only, never copied (AG-10-89). The list is kept on the case (AG-7-97 rule 6, C3.8a). Staff add a person with the tick on People on this case or on the Add to case form, and "How did the client ask?" is offered, never required (AG-7-97 rules 7, 8). *Example:* Client B emails Client C with his brother Client Q in Cc. Client C clicks Add to case for Client Q; Copy on emails is already ticked and the source reads "Client copied them". From then on, every email Client C writes to Client B on C-2026-00012 has Client Q in Cc. Client B's questionnaire link and reminders still go to Client B alone. *Not the same as:* a consent or authorisation record; it is a firm instruction to send copies, kept on the case only, with no list on a lead, prospect or client (AG-7-97 rule 6). Nor is it a related contact: a copy creates no `PartyRelationship` (AG-7-97 rule 9). *(added 25 Oct — CC-1, CC-2, CC-5; changed 26 Oct — CC-8)*
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:stop-copying-someone -->
**Stop copying someone** (no code name) — Stop copying someone is the no-login link at the end of the footer "Copied on this case: <full names>. Stop copying someone", which every email a staff member writes with copy-list people in Cc carries (AG-10-89 rule 6). System emails are never copied, so they never carry it (AG-10-85 rule 7). It opens a small page listing the copied people named in that footer, each with a "Stop copying <name>" button; a click removes that person from the case's copy list at once, and earlier emails are unchanged (AG-10-85 rule 7). Anyone holding the email may use it. *Example:* Client A, copied on an email Client C wrote to Client B, taps "Stop copying someone", then "Stop copying Client A". She gets no further emails on that case. *Not the same as:* **Stop copying** on the People on this case panel, which staff use; both end the same copy-list row (AG-7-97 rule 10). *(added 25 Oct — CC-4; changed 26 Oct — CC-8)*
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:file-owner -->
**File owner** (`fileOwnerUserId`) — The file owner is the staff member who owns a client. It is set on the client file record, not on a case. It is set from the facts: an existing file owner stays; otherwise the person who adds the client, the person who makes someone a client, the employer's file owner for a work-permit case, or the owner the import gives (BUILD-18). A client with no file owner shows in **Unassigned clients**, where anyone who can edit clients can **Take it** or **Assign** it, even with own-only visibility (BUILD-25). *Example:* Client C is file owner of client CL-00001. *Not the same as:* the case officer, who does the day-to-day work on one case.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:case-number -->
**Case number** (`caseCode`) — The case number is the firm's reference for a case, such as C-2026-00001. It is the letter C, the year and a 5-digit counter that restarts each year. *Example:* The Client E case is C-2026-00012, the 12th case the firm opened in 2026. The server sets it; it is never edited or reused. *Not the same as:* the client number or the government's application number.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:client-number -->
**Client number** (`clientCode`) — The client number is the firm's reference for a client, such as CL-00001. It is CL and a 5-digit counter for the firm, with no year. The screens show it as "Client code". *Example:* Client B is client CL-00001, and all his cases show that number. It is never edited or reused. *Not the same as:* the case number, which includes a year.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:lead-representative -->
**Lead representative** (`LEAD_REPRESENTATIVE`) — The lead representative is the licensed person whose licence backs the case. It is filled in from the signed retainer or the firm's default, and a case may have none. *Example:* Client F is lead representative on the Client E case because she signed the retainer. If Client G were to sign instead, a warning shows: "Client F is this case's lead representative. Client G will sign this retainer." It never blocks. *Not the same as:* the case officer, who does the day-to-day work.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:licensed-user -->
**Licensed user** (no code name) — A licensed user is an active user whom the firm has linked to a professional credential. ExpatReady does not check the licence. Only licensed users are offered as lead representative. *Example:* Client F is a licensed user. Client C is not, so he is never offered as lead representative. *Not the same as:* a role such as Owner or Admin, which says what a user may do.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:case-officer -->
**Case officer** (`OFFICER`) — The case officer does the day-to-day work on a case: documents, forms and getting it ready. A paralegal does this job too, so there is no separate paralegal role. *Example:* Client C is case officer on the Client E case and collects the family's documents. *Not the same as:* the lead representative, who is accountable for the file.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:case-reviewer -->
**Case reviewer** (`REVIEWER`) — The case reviewer is the team member who checks a case before it is submitted. A case may have none, and a missing reviewer does not make it Unassigned. *Example:* Client I is the case reviewer on the Client E case and approves it before Client F records the submission. *Not the same as:* the Reviewer Doer, which works out what is missing on a case by fixed rules.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:unassigned -->
**Unassigned** (no code name) — A case is Unassigned when it has no case officer. A missing reviewer or lead representative does not make it Unassigned. *Example:* The Client E case has no case officer yet, so it appears in the Unassigned list. *Not the same as:* "Needs a lead representative", a separate filter for cases with no lead representative.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:take-it -->
**Take it** (`agent/case:edit`) — Take it is one click that makes you the case officer of an unassigned case. Anyone who can edit cases may use it, for themselves only. *Example:* Client C clicks Take it on the Client E case and becomes its case officer. Adding a colleague instead needs the permission to assign cases. *Not the same as:* Take it on a mailbox conversation, which makes you the owner of an email thread.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:needs-a-lead-representative -->
**Needs a lead representative** (no code name) — Needs a lead representative is a filter for cases that have no lead representative. It is separate from Unassigned. A missing lead representative warns at submission and never blocks. *Example:* A walk-in case has no retainer and the firm has no default, so it shows under Needs a lead representative. *Not the same as:* Unassigned, which means no case officer.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:welcome-message -->
**Welcome message** (no code name) — The Welcome is an email offered in a ticked box when a case is created. It is one email, even for a family case, To the case's client only. It is a system email, so it is never copied (AG-10-89). <!-- term:on-hold -->

<!-- term:form-viewer -->
**Form viewer** (no code name) — The form viewer shows the real government form inside ExpatReady. The form is pre-filled by the Filler, staff can edit it, and the form's own rules validate it. <!-- term:form-approval -->
**Form approval** (`agent/case:form-approve`) — Form approval is a person with the form-approve permission accepting a prepared government form. <!-- term:form-edition -->
**Form edition** (`FormEdition`) — A form edition is the version of one government form, with an effective date from the pack. A newer edition makes the form show "form out of date" and offers a re-bind. A retired edition is a CRITICAL warning, never a stop. *Example:* A newer edition appears. Client B's form shows "form out of date", and a re-bind carries his values forward. *Not the same as:* a rules version, which is the version of the whole country pack.

<!-- term:override -->
**Override** (no code name) — An override is a staff correction to one auto-filled value on one form. The person's own answers do not change. Pushing the correction back to the person's answers is a separate, explicit action. *Example:* Client C corrects Client B's employer name on the form only. Client B's answers still show the old name. *Not the same as:* verifying a value, which makes it usable by the Filler.

<!-- term:verified-value -->
**Verified value** (`agent/sqr:verify`) — A value from the Reader or an import is unverified until a person checks it. Only verified values fill forms. An unverified value shows beside the field as "unverified — verify to use". *Example:* An imported passport number shows "unverified — verify to use". <!-- term:must-verify-before-filing -->
**Must verify before filing** (`mustVerifyBeforeFiling`) — Must verify before filing is a yes/no flag on a library canonical field, default no, set only by ExpatReady's content team with the Immigration-SME; firms have no setting (SA-01B SA-1B-1, SA1B-D42). An unverified fact on a flagged field is a CRITICAL deficiency at the filing step, passed only with `agent/case:waive-deficiency` and a reason; nothing is locked (AG-8-27, BR-9, AG17-D25). *Example:* The passport-number field is flagged yes. Until Client C verifies Client B's passport number, the filing step lists it as a CRITICAL deficiency. *Not the same as:* the immigration-significant flag, which only controls whether a value may go into a message (SA1B-D42). *(added 25 Oct — SA1B-D42)*

<!-- term:immigration-significant -->

Which fields carry it is pack content, set with the Immigration-SME (AG-11-97). *Example:* An automatic message to Client B would fill in an unverified immigration-significant date, so it waits in Needs you instead of sending. *Not the same as:* must verify before filing, a separate flag that raises a CRITICAL deficiency at the filing step and has nothing to do with messages (SA1B-D42). *(added 25 Oct — SA1B-D42)*

<!-- term:self-approved -->
**Self-approved** (`selfApproved`) — Self-approved is recorded when the person who approves a form also prepared it. It is never blocked. The approver's name, time and the self-approved flag show in the form's history. *Example:* Client F prepares and approves Client D's form, so it shows "self-approved". *Not the same as:* a rule that needs a second approver. No such rule exists.

<!-- term:needs-you -->
**Needs you** (no code name) — Needs you is each person's list of drafts and proposals waiting for their approval. Nothing in it runs by itself, and a draft is approved inline when the approver is already acting. *Example:* A Filler draft for Client B's form waits in Client F's Needs you until she approves it. *Not the same as:* the whole Home screen, because Needs you is only section 1 of it.

<!-- term:deficiency -->
**Deficiency** (`Deficiency`) — A deficiency is a gap found on a case, such as a missing document or an unsigned retainer. Each has a severity: critical, major, minor or info. Only critical ones must be fixed or passed with a reason. *Example:* Client D's school letter is not accepted yet, so a critical warning shows on her filing. At review approval Client I passes it with a reason, and nothing is blocked. *Not the same as:* an overdue invoice, which shows on the case header instead.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:deficiency-library -->
**Deficiency library** (`DeficiencyLibraryItem`) — The deficiency library is ExpatReady's list of checks for each country and program. Firms cannot edit it, but staff can add a one-off deficiency to a case by hand. The checks are on every plan. *Example:* The library raises "Passport copy missing" for Client A. <!-- term:readiness -->
**Readiness** (`NOT_READY`, `ATTENTION`, `READY`) — Readiness shows how close a filing is to submission: Not ready, Attention or Ready, plus a percentage score. Not ready means a critical deficiency is open and not passed. Attention means an open major one; otherwise Ready. The case shows the lowest. *Example:* Client B's filing is Ready, Client A's is Attention and Client D's is Not ready, so the Client E case shows Not ready. A gap passed with a reason counts as finished in the score. *Not the same as:* a lock, because readiness never blocks any action.

<!-- term:severity -->
**Severity** (`Deficiency` severity) — Every deficiency has a severity: CRITICAL, MAJOR, MINOR or INFO. The pack sets the severity of each check. Only an open CRITICAL deficiency needs to be fixed or passed. *Example:* The Checker finds a name mismatch on Client A's certificate. <!-- term:critical-warning -->
**CRITICAL warning** (`agent/case:waive-deficiency`) — A CRITICAL warning is a deficiency of severity CRITICAL that is open or acknowledged. Acknowledging it only records that someone has seen it; it still counts until it is resolved or passed (BUILD-9). It is listed when the case goes into review and at submission. An unapproved or unsigned form is one. It is fixed or passed with a reason, and it never locks the case. *Example:* Client I passes Client D's warning with the reason "Original sent by courier". <!-- term:checker -->
**Checker** (Doer) — The Checker is an AI Doer. It compares names, dates and numbers across documents, answers and forms. Each mismatch becomes a warning on the case, with the values compared. A Checker warning never locks the case. *Example:* "Client E" on the passport and "Client U" on the certificate give a warning. *Not the same as:* the deficiency library, which holds fixed checks written by ExpatReady.

<!-- term:internal-review -->
**Internal review** (`CaseReview`) — Internal review is a colleague's check of one or more filings before they are submitted. Each filing has its own review status. One review may cover several filings. Anyone who can edit cases may send filings for review. The reviewer approves them or sends them back with reasons. *Example:* On Wed 18 Nov 2026 Client C sends the three Client E filings for review in one request. <!-- term:changed-since-review -->
**Changed since review** (no code name) — Changed since review is a note on a filing that changed after its review was approved. It is worked out when the case is read, and no field stores it. It does not undo the approval. At submission it asks for one confirmation. *Example:* After Client I's approval, Client C replaces Client D's school letter on Thu 19 Nov 2026. <!-- term:submission -->
**Submission** (`Submission`) — A submission is the record that a filing was sent to the government authority. <!-- term:government-step -->
**Government step** (`CaseMilestone`) — A government step is an event from the authority, such as acknowledgement of receipt or biometrics. **Step done** (`CaseMilestone.doneAt`) — Step done marks a government step as finished, with a date. Anyone who can edit cases may use it. <!-- term:deadline -->
**Deadline** (`dueDate`, `dueStatus`) — A government deadline is a calendar date by which something must be done. It sits on the government step. <!-- term:government-date -->
**Government date** (no code name) — A government date is a calendar date that never moves for the firm's closed days. A reminder counted back from it lands on the last open day on or before the computed day. The case shows the date with its weekday. *Example:* The firm has closed 1 Jan. Client A's deadline stays Fri 1 Jan 2027. The "2 working days before" reminder lands on Wed 30 Dec 2026. *Not the same as:* the target date, which follows the firm's closed days.

<!-- term:business-hours -->
**Business hours and closed days** (no code name) — Business hours are the days and hours the firm is open. The default is Monday to Friday, 9:00 to 17:00. ExpatReady offers suggested holidays but saves nothing until the firm clicks Add. *Not the same as:* a government date, which closed days never move.

<!-- term:application-number -->
**Application number** (`Filing.applicationNumber`) — An application number is the number a government authority gives to a filing. Each filing keeps its own. <!-- term:uci -->
**UCI** (`ClientCanonicalAnswer`) — The UCI is the 10-digit number Canada's immigration authority gives to one person. Every family member has their own, kept as an answer on that person's record. *Example:* Client B, Client A and Client D each have a different UCI. The submission keeps a copy of each as sent. *Not the same as:* the application number, which belongs to a filing.

<!-- term:provincial-file-number -->
**Provincial file number** (`Case.provincialFileNumbers`) — A provincial file number is a number that a province gives to a filing. A case keeps a free-text list of them. <!-- term:letter-library -->
**Letter library** (no code name) — The letter library is ExpatReady's list of known government letter types. <!-- /term -->

<!-- term:not-started -->
**Not started** (`automationState` QUIET) — Not started means the client's automatic messages have not been started. An imported client, lead or case begins as Not started. Internal work runs, but no client message goes out until the firm starts it. *Example:* The firm imports an old case. It shows "Not started", and its imported values stay unverified until a person verifies them. *Not the same as:* client quiet hours, which are the 08:00 to 20:00 sending window.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:office -->
**Office** (`Office`) — An office is a branch of the firm with its own time zone. It stays hidden until the firm adds a second office. *Example:* The firm adds a Calgary office, and the Client E case belongs to it because a case takes its client's office. Changing a case's office recalculates unsent reminders; government dates never move. *Not the same as:* the person time zone, which is the person's own time zone.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:office-time -->
**Office time** (`Office.timezone`) — A time tied to an office is shown in that office's time zone. It is labelled, such as "10:00 (Calgary)", when it differs from yours; nothing is converted. *Example:* Client C in Toronto sees the Client E booking as "10:00 (Calgary)". *Not the same as:* the person time zone, which decides when messages reach the client.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:person-time-zone -->
**Person time zone** (`PartyProfile.timezone`) — The person time zone is the person's own IANA time zone, held on their details. ExpatReady fills it from the address, else a phone code that covers one zone, else the case's office. It shows "(auto)", and staff can edit it. *Example:* Client B's address is in Dubai, so his details show "Time zone: Dubai (auto)". *Not the same as:* office time, which is shown in the office's time zone.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:timeline -->
**Timeline** (no code name) — The timeline is a case's history in one paged list. It shows stage changes, documents, forms, deficiencies, submissions, government steps, decisions, notes, tasks, messages, reminders and appointments (bookings on the case, AG-7-110). It is built from the other modules and is not a log of its own. *Example:* Client C opens the Client E timeline and sees "Biometrics requested" for Client A on 2 Dec 2026. *Not the same as:* the audit log, which is the compliance record.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:audit-log -->
**Audit log** (`AuditLog`) — The audit log is the record of sensitive actions, and it cannot be changed. Each entry shows who did what, to what, and the values before and after. *Example:* Client I passes Client D's warning. The audit log records Client I, the reason and the time. *Not the same as:* the timeline, which is a readable history for staff.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:notification -->
**Notification** (`Notification`) — A notification alerts a staff member only when someone hands them work. AG-07 raises four: assigned to me, review requested from me, my review sent back and approval waiting. The notice itself is sent by AG-11's Notification Doer once it is built; until then the work shows in the queues and Needs you (BUILD-27). Everything else shows on the case, the timeline and the queue, with no alert. *Example:* Client I is notified "review requested from me" when Client C sends the Client E case for review. A document arriving sends no alert. *Not the same as:* a class-2 message box, which writes to the client.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:starter-roles -->
**Starter roles** (`firm-owner` to `firm-read-only`) — A firm starts with six roles: Owner, Admin, Case Manager, Case Officer, Billing / Finance and Read-only. They say what a user may do. *Example:* Client C holds the Case Officer role, so he can edit cases. On the Client E case he is also its case officer. *Not the same as:* the case officer team slot, which is one person on one case.
<!-- /term -->

## Key terms — leads and sales (AG-02) *(new v1.10)*

> **For writers and scripts (X-1 rule 4).** Same format and rules as "Key terms — cases and delivery" above. These 40 entries are the NEW and REDEFINE blocks of AG-02 v2.0 §A2 ("Terms new in AG-02 v2.0"), copied word for word on 21 Oct (AG02-P110). From now on this section is their home: change a term here and run the script again. Two entries add lead and prospect meanings beside case terms of the same name: **Unassigned leads** (beside the case term Unassigned) and **Take it** on a lead or prospect (beside the case term Take it); the case terms are unchanged (AG02-P109). The Glossary table row **Prospect** is the long form of the prospect entry. Examples follow AG-02's walk-through (Client B's lead L-00031, prospect P-00017 and client CL-00001; Pine Bakery Ltd as the employer).

**People and leads**

<!-- term:lead -->
**Lead** (`Lead`) — A lead is a person who showed interest. It records how and when they arrived, who owns it (`Lead.ownerUserId`) and when the firm first answered. It has no type, stage or value. *Example:* Client B's Meta ad form of Mon 12 Oct 2026 is lead L-00031. *Not the same as:* a prospect, which is a sale being worked, or a lead representative, who is a licensed person on a case.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:company-record -->
**Company record** (`Party`, kind ORGANISATION) — A company record holds an employer as one record. Engage as Corporate creates it, or links an existing one. It gets a client file record only when it becomes a client (AG07-D20(k), AG02-D10(2)). The one exception: after Undo convert the company keeps that record, with no number (AG-2-54). *Example:* Pine Bakery Ltd is a company record. Lena Park and Client T Reyes are linked to it. *Not the same as:* a person record, or a client. A company is a client only after its prospect is won.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:source -->
**Source** (`leadSourceId`) — The source says where a lead came from, such as Meta lead ad, Website form or Referral. Every lead must have one. *Example:* Lead L-00031 has the source Meta lead ad. *Not the same as:* the door, which is the technical path the lead arrived on.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:door -->
**Door** (`door`) — The door is the technical path a lead arrived on, such as web form, lead API, booking, email rule or CSV import. *Example:* Lena's lead L-00032 came through the web form door. *Not the same as:* the source. One source, such as Website form, can arrive through more than one door.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:arrival-path -->
**Arrival path** (no code name) — The arrival path is the one set of steps every door runs. It cleans the contact details, writes one arrival receipt, creates the person record and the lead, records consent evidence and flags possible matches. *Example:* A web form and a Meta ad both run the same steps. *Not the same as:* Engage, which is a later click by staff.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:attribution -->
**Attribution** (`LeadAttribution`) — Attribution holds the campaign, ad and page facts that came with a lead. It is written once at arrival and never edited. *Example:* Client B's lead keeps the ad and the form it came from. *Not the same as:* the credited sales user, who gets credit for a sale.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:interested-in -->
**Interested in** (`interestedIn`) — Interested in lists what the person asked about, on the lead and on the prospect. The list holds the pack's programmes and the firm's own services, such as Canadian passport, OCI card or translation. Staff add an item like a label; the Owner or Admin renames or removes one from the same list (`agent/prospect:configure-pipeline`, AG02-D13(6)). A firm service works on Individual and Corporate prospects. Engage copies the lead's list to the prospect, where staff can edit it (AG02-D13(7)). The web form question uses this list. It never sets the type of a prospect (AG02-D9). *Example:* Client B's lead shows "Interested in: Work permit". *Not the same as:* the prospect type, which staff choose at Engage. Forms ask no "asking as: myself / a business" question (AG02-D12, D8.27).
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:consent -->
**Consent** (`ConsentRecord`) — Consent is a recorded yes or withdrawal for one channel and one purpose. Each row keeps where it came from, the wording shown, the language and the time. Rows are only added, never edited. *Example:* Suppose Client B ticks "email me updates" on a form. The row keeps the form and the wording version. *Not the same as:* permission to answer an inquiry. Only express consent on one channel counts for that channel. WhatsApp marketing needs its own purpose, `WHATSAPP_MARKETING`; the WhatsApp service opt-in (`WHATSAPP_OPT_IN`) never counts as marketing (AG02-D15(4)). An inbound WhatsApp message is not marketing consent, and a referral brings none. Imported consent counts when a consent column is mapped and the importer ticks the declaration, for the channel that column names (MAP:368) (AG02-D8, AG02-D12, D8.8).
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:consent-gate -->
**Consent gate** (BR-9) — The consent gate is the check made at send time. It skips every person who has no consent for that channel, and it counts the people skipped. *Example:* Suppose a list has 50 people and 20 have no consent. The 20 are skipped and shown as a count before sending. *Not the same as:* quiet hours, which are a time window.
<!-- /term -->

**Duplicates and repeat inquiries**

<!-- term:possible-match -->
**Possible match** (`DuplicateFlag`) — A possible match is a banner that says a new lead may be someone the firm already knows. Staff choose Link, Merge or Keep separate. Nothing joins by itself. *Example:* Client B's June 2027 inquiry shows "Possible match: Client B (client CL-00001, file owner Client C)". *Not the same as:* a certain duplicate, because it may be a different person.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:asked-again -->
**Asked again** (no code name) — Asked again is the banner and alert shown when a person the firm already knows sends a new inquiry. A new lead is made with the banner, and the matched person's owner is told once (AG02-D6). *Example:* In June 2027 Client C is alerted that Client B has inquired again. *Not the same as:* a new lead from a stranger.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:company-match -->
**Company match** (no code name) — Company match is the list of companies Engage as Corporate offers when staff type a company name. It looks at business number, name, website domain and main phone. It only suggests. Free-mail domains such as gmail.com never match. *Example:* A second Pine Bakery inquiry shows "Same company? Pine Bakery Ltd". *Not the same as:* a possible match, which compares people.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:family-add-match -->
**Family add match** (AG-2-14 rule 12) — When staff, the questionnaire or an import add a family member or applicant, ExpatReady also offers firm-wide people with the same first name, last name and date of birth; it is an offer only and never links, merges or hides (AG-2-14 rule 12). The date of birth must be on both records, and a swapped given name and surname counts. It runs only on the family-add paths (AG-2-72, AG-7-107, AG-8-28, the import AG-18-94, and when an expected child becomes a real person), never on lead arrival, Add client, the case wizard's "+ New client" or the copy list (AG-2-14 field 3, rule 12 f). *Example:* Client C adds Client D, with her name and date of birth, to Client B's case. Client D is on file only as Client A's daughter from Client A's own earlier case, so ExpatReady offers her with [Use her] and [Add as a new person]. *Not the same as:* a possible match, which compares email and phone and writes a `DuplicateFlag` on a lead; a family add match writes none (AG-2-14 rule 12 c). *(added 25 Oct — DUP-8)*
<!-- /term -->

**Owner and first answer**

<!-- term:unassigned-leads -->
**Unassigned leads** (no code name) — Unassigned leads is the list of open leads that have no owner (`Lead.ownerUserId` empty). *Example:* Lead L-00031 sits in Unassigned leads from 08:20 until Client C takes it. *Not the same as:* Unassigned cases, tasks or conversations. Those are different lists in other epics. A one-user firm never sees it: its user owns every lead (X-4).
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:how-new-leads-are-shared -->
**How new leads are shared** (firm setting) — One setting in Settings → Advanced, for a firm with 2 or more users. **Anyone takes it** (default: the lead waits in Unassigned leads), **Round robin** between the people the admin ticks, or **Always one person**. It has no conditions, no weighting and no workload limits. Facts come first: the person who typed or created a lead, a one-to-one booking's host, or a one-user firm's user owns it. Only the Owner or an Admin changes it (`agent/firm-capability:manage`). *Example:* Suppose Maple picks Round robin for Client C and Farah; a web-form lead goes to whoever was given one least recently. *Not the same as:* a routing rule. ExpatReady has none (AG02-D1).
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:take-it-lead -->
**Take it** on a lead or prospect (`agent/prospect:edit`) — Take it is one click that makes you the owner of a lead or prospect that has none. Anyone who can edit prospects and can see the item may use it, for themselves only. It works whatever the sharing setting is (AG02-D1). *Example:* Client C clicks Take it on L-00031 and becomes its owner. *Not the same as:* Take it on a case, which makes you the case officer.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:lead-owner -->
**Lead owner** (`Lead.ownerUserId`) — The lead owner is the staff member who works one lead. Taking or assigning a lead changes only the lead owner, never the file owner of a person already on file. A brand-new person's file owner is set to the owner of their first lead (AG02-D7). *Example:* Suppose Client B, client CL-00001 with file owner Client C, asks again and Farah is assigned the new lead; Client C stays file owner. *Not the same as:* the file owner, who is set on the person.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:prospect-owner -->
**Prospect owner** (`ownerUserId`) — The prospect owner is the staff member who works one prospect. The file owner is the staff member responsible for the person. They are often the same. *Example:* Client C owns prospect P-00017. *Not the same as:* the file owner, who is set on the person.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:unattended-door -->
**Unattended door** (no code name) — An unattended door is a way in where no staff member is present when the lead arrives. The unattended doors are the web form, the Lead API (including the ExpatReady Zapier app and its Meta Zap) and form-notification email. Only leads from these doors get the thanks email (AG02-D9(4)). *Example:* Client B's Meta ad lead arrives through Zapier at 08:20 with nobody watching. *Not the same as:* a lead typed in by staff, a booking-born lead or an import. Those get no thanks email.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:reminder-doors -->
**Reminder doors** (no code name) — The reminder doors are the doors whose leads the reply reminder covers. They are the unattended doors only: the web form, the Lead API (including the ExpatReady Zapier app) and form-notification email (AG02-D7(2)). *Example:* Client B's Meta ad lead arrives through Zapier at 08:20; when the reminder is on, the lead is covered. Client C clicks "Create lead" on an unknown WhatsApp number; that lead is not covered, because a person made it. *Not the same as:* the doors that get a "Call now" task (AG-2-56 rule 2), which also include "Create lead" from a conversation and a booking. A lead typed by hand, a lead from "Create lead", a booking-born lead and an import are not reminder doors.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:reply-reminder -->
**Reply reminder** (firm timing) — The reply reminder is one optional setting on the Timings screen: "Remind us if a new lead has no reply after: Off / 1 hour / 4 hours / 1 working day / custom". It is Off by default. When a firm switches it on, 1 hour is pre-selected (AG02-D13(1)). When on, it covers leads from the reminder doors only. The reply is the first response: a logged call, inbound or outbound; a message a person writes and sends; or a booking a staff member makes with the person (AG02-D13(3), AG02-D14(2)). Unassigned leads always shows how long each lead has waited, in the firm's open hours, and `firstResponseAt` records the first response (AG02-D7). *Example:* L-00031 arrives at 08:20, Maple opens at 09:00 and Client C calls at 09:20, so the lead waited 20 open minutes. *Not the same as:* a target or a breach. There is no response clock.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:timings-screen -->
**Timings screen** (AG-16 Settings → Advanced → Timings) — The one screen where a firm changes the timings that shape its work: the reply reminder, going cold, the Message many frequency limit and follow-up timing. Each is pre-filled with its default. Other timings are fixed and not shown (X-5). *Example:* Maple changes going cold from 7 to 10 open days. *Not the same as:* business hours (AG16-6).
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:speed-to-lead -->
**Speed-to-lead** (recipe `speed_to_lead`) — Speed-to-lead is a ready-made recipe that runs when a lead arrives. It creates a "Call now" task and, for the unattended doors, sends the automatic thanks email (AG02-D9(4)). The thanks email is the "Lead acknowledgement" row on the Automatic messages page (AG-12, SA-05). A lead made already engaged gets neither. AG-11 owns the recipe. *Example:* On L-00031 it creates a "Call now" task and sends Client B the thanks email. *Not the same as:* the reply reminder, which only reminds staff.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:going-cold -->
**Going cold** (no code name) — Going cold is a quiet mark on an open lead or an active prospect with no activity for a set number of days. The default is 7 days the firm is open, changeable on the Timings screen. Nobody is notified (AG02-D7). Staff calls, messages and meetings and the person's own messages reset it; automatic messages and Message many sends do not (AG02-D12, D8.42). The mark is worked out when shown, not stored. A closed lead, a lost prospect or a nurture prospect never goes cold. *Example:* A lead gets no call or message for 7 open days. It shows "Going cold" in its row. *Not the same as:* Nurture, which is a choice by staff.
<!-- /term -->

**Engage and the sale**

<!-- term:prospect -->
**Prospect** (`Prospect`) — A prospect is a sale being worked. It has a type (Individual or Corporate), a pipeline, a stage, an owner and an expected value. A click on Engage creates it. *Example:* P-00017 is Client B's sale, Individual, now in stage Engaged. *Not the same as:* a lead, who is only a person who showed interest.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:engage -->
**Engage** (`engage`) — Engage is one click by staff that turns a lead into a prospect. Staff choose Individual or Corporate in that click. Nothing is sent and no assessment starts. *Example:* On Thu 15 Oct 2026 Client C clicks Engage on L-00031 and picks Individual. *Not the same as:* assessing a person, or a client signing. Engage is always a person's click; nothing engages by itself. Where a retainer or a booking needs a prospect, the Engage box opens inline (AG02-D9).
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:prospect-type -->
**Prospect type** (`subjectPartyKind`) — The prospect type is Individual or Corporate. Staff choose it at Engage and can change it until the prospect is signed. Corporate means the sale is to an employer. *Example:* Client C engages Lena's inquiry as Corporate, so the sale is to Pine Bakery Ltd. *Not the same as:* client type on a case, which is worked out from the program. Changing the type after a retainer is sent asks staff to void the retainer first (AG02-D10).
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:pipeline -->
**Pipeline** (`PipelineDefinition`) — A pipeline is the set of sales stages for one prospect type. A firm has an Individual pipeline and a Corporate pipeline. *Example:* P-00018 sits in the Corporate pipeline. *Not the same as:* the case board, which shows cases after the sale.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:deal-stage -->
**Stage and kind** (`PipelineStage`, `StageKind`) — A sales stage is one position on a pipeline, such as Engaged, Consult or Retainer Sent. Each stage has a kind that fixes how it behaves: ACTIVE, WON, LOST or NURTURE. *Example:* On Wed 21 Oct 2026 P-00017 moves to Retainer Sent, which is an ACTIVE stage. *Not the same as:* a case stage. Each pipeline is seeded with eight stages starting at Engaged, with one WON, one LOST and one NURTURE stage (AG02-D10).
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:won -->
**WON** (stage kind WON) — WON means the prospect became a client. It is set once, by whichever comes first: the retainer fully signed in ExpatReady Signer, Signed offline, or a person's "Convert to client". How it was won is stored (`wonVia`), apart from the signed date. A person cannot drag a card to it (AG02-D2). The seeded WON stage is labelled "Won – client". *Example:* Client B signs on Sun 1 Nov 2026 and Client F countersigns the same evening, so P-00017 is WON. *Not the same as:* a case decision of Approved.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:signed-offline -->
**Signed offline** (`markSignedOffline`) — Signed offline is an action for a retainer signed on paper. It needs `agent/retainer:offline-sign` (AG-5-39), a reason and the uploaded original. It never invents a document. *Example:* A client signs a paper retainer in the office. Client F uploads the scan, gives a reason and marks it. *Not the same as:* an e-signature, which sets WON by itself once the firm has countersigned.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:becomes-a-client -->
**Becomes a client** (`prospect.won`, `client.created`) — A prospect's subject becomes a client once, by whichever comes first. (1) The retainer is EXECUTED in ExpatReady Signer: all required signatures are in, the client's and, where the firm requires it, the firm's countersignature. While waiting, the deal shows "Client signed, waiting for countersign". (2) Signed offline, with a reason. (3) A person clicks **Convert to client** and confirms "Retainer not signed. Work can start, invoices can't, the case will show a warning." A later signature only clears that warning and lets invoicing start; there is no second client and no second WON. The client number, from the firm's one counter (AG02-D14(6)), and the file owner are set in the same step. Nothing client-facing is sent (AG02-D2). *Example:* Client B signs on Sun 1 Nov 2026, Client F countersigns the same evening, and he is client CL-00001. The prompt "Open a case?" follows. *Not the same as:* opening a case, which a person always does.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:undo-convert -->
**Undo convert** (`agent/prospect:convert`) — Undo convert takes back a client made by mistake, only while the client is empty: no case, no invoice and no signed retainer. An invoice from before the person became a client, such as a paid consult, does not count (AG02-D15(1)). A reason is required. The deal returns to the stage it was in before WON, and WON and the credit are cleared (AG02-D2). If this sale made the person a client, they are a prospect again. A person who was already a client stays a client (AG02-D14(1)). The client number is never given to anyone else; if the same person becomes a client again, they get it back (AG02-D13(8)). *Example:* Client C converts the wrong prospect by mistake and undoes it a minute later. *Not the same as:* Withdraw on a case (AG-7-50), which is used once a case exists; the deal then stays WON.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:credited-sales-user -->
**Credited sales user** (`creditedSalesUserId`) — The credited sales user is the staff member who gets the credit for a won sale. It is set to the prospect owner at WON and is then read-only. *Example:* Client C is the credited sales user of P-00017. *Not the same as:* the file owner or the lead representative.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:nurture -->
**Nurture** (stage kind NURTURE) — Nurture parks a prospect until a chosen date, with a reason. Nothing moves by itself. When the date comes, the prospect shows in the owner's My Tasks. *Example:* Suppose a person is saving for a language test. Client C parks the prospect until 15 Jan 2027. *Not the same as:* Lost, which ends the sale.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:lost -->
**Lost** (stage kind LOST) — Lost ends a prospect that did not sign. A reason is required. A lost prospect is never reopened; staff start a new one. *Example:* A person chooses another firm. Client C marks the prospect Lost with the reason "Went with competitor". *Not the same as:* Close a lead, which ends a lead that never became a prospect.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:close-a-lead -->
**Close a lead** (`Lead.state` CLOSED) — Close a lead ends a lead without a prospect. A reason is required, such as Not interested or Junk. An optional "Remind me on [date]" sets a reminder. A closed lead can be reopened (AG02-D10). *Example:* Client C closes a spam lead with the reason "Junk or spam". *Not the same as:* Lost, which belongs to a prospect.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:contact-role -->
**Contact role** (`PartyRelationship`, role) — A contact role is the part a person plays at a company: PRIMARY, SIGNATORY, HR, BILLING or OTHER (AG02-D14(14)). It is stored once as a `PartyRelationship` of type `CLIENT_CONTACT` and linked, never copied (AG02-D10(1)). *Example:* Lena Park is PRIMARY and Client T Reyes is SIGNATORY at Pine Bakery Ltd. *Not the same as:* a family link, or a lead representative. Applicant and Spouse are family links, not company roles.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:label -->
**Label** (`Tag`, `PartyTag`) — A label is a short word staff put on a person, such as "VIP" or "Spanish speaker". It follows the person as lead, prospect and client. Staff who can edit add it with "+ Label" on the person header. A person carries at most 10 labels, and a row shows at most 2, then "+n". Only the Owner or an Admin renames or deletes a label (`agent/prospect:configure-pipeline`, AG02-D13(6)). Lists filter by label, and Message many can use it. Labels are staff-only and have no colours, groups or automation (AG02-D9(2)). *Example:* Client C labels Client B "Referral from Client I". *Not the same as:* Interested in, or the person's lifecycle label (LEAD, PROSPECT, CLIENT, PAST CLIENT).
<!-- /term -->

**Channels and Message many**

<!-- term:meta-lead-ad -->
**Meta lead ad** (source "Meta lead ad") — A Meta lead ad is a Facebook or Instagram instant form. In Release 1 it reaches ExpatReady through the ExpatReady app on Zapier, using its "Create lead" action and a ready-made Zap "Facebook Lead Ads → ExpatReady lead". The Zap uses a lead API key of kind "Meta lead ads" (`ZAPIER_META`), and that key kind is what makes the door `META_LEAD_AD` (AG-2-6 rule 7). The lead keeps the Meta lead id, form id and ad id. Form-notification email stays as a fallback (AG02-D3). *Example:* Client B's ad form "Canada work permit — spouse and daughter" creates lead L-00031. *Not the same as:* the firm's web form. A native Meta connection is Release 2.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:message-many -->
**Message many** (`agent/communication:bulk-send`) — Message many sends one email or WhatsApp template to a filtered list of leads or clients. A preview says who will get it and who is skipped, such as "112 will get it, 8 skipped (no consent)". Only people with express consent on that channel receive it. A frequency limit, 1 marketing message per person per week by default, skips anyone already messaged. It is a paid extra (AG02-D8). *Example:* Maple sends news to past leads who agreed to hear from it; the others are skipped and counted. *Not the same as:* a follow-up, which runs for one person. There are no campaign objects, schedules or drips.
<!-- /term -->

## Key terms — PDF Studio (AG-19) *(new v1.12)*

> **For writers and scripts (X-1 rule 4).** Same format and rules as "Key terms — cases and delivery" above. Each meaning and example below is copied word for word from Part A2 of `AG-19-pdf-studio_v1.10_2026-10-26.md` (26 Oct); only the "Not the same as" lines are this glossary's own. When AG-19's A2 changes, copy it again. Examples follow the Client E family (Client C is the case officer, Client B the client).

<!-- term:pdf-studio -->
**PDF Studio** (module AG-19; no code name) — ExpatReady's own module for working on PDF pages. Not a Doer, not part of AG-09 (AG19-D1). *Example:* Client C opens Studio from the menu to combine three files from his computer. *Not the same as:* the form viewer, which shows a government form filled; PDF Studio refuses XFA government forms, which stay on their line as their own file; its message has no button and says forms are filled on the case's Forms tab (AG19-D14, AG19-D16, AG19-D39).
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:document-part -->
**Part** (of a document) (`DocumentPart`) — One file of a Document. A Document can hold several parts while it is being worked. Adding a part replaces nothing (AG19-D6). A client upload always adds a part (AG19-D18). Home: AG-09 card AG-9-65. *Example:* Client B's four pay stubs are parts 1 to 4 of one Document. *Not the same as:* a version, which is one whole state of the document's files. A PDF Studio save makes a new version (AG-9-65 rule 6). Replacing or removing an Accepted or submitted file also makes a new version, so that file stays in the history (AG19-D18).
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:replace-file -->
**Replace (a file)** (no code name) — A staff action on one part: pick a new file, and only that part changes. The old file goes to Recently deleted, unless it was Accepted or submitted; then it stays in the history as evidence (AG19-D18). Until AG-07 records which files went into a submission, only Accepted files are kept this way (AG19-D29). Home: AG-09 card AG-9-67. *Example:* Client C replaces part 2, a blurred pay stub, with Client B's clear copy. Parts 1, 3 and 4 do not change. *Not the same as:* a client upload, which always adds a part and never replaces.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:remove-file -->
**Remove (a file)** (no code name) — A staff action that takes one or more parts off a Document, chosen with multi-select. Removed files follow the same rule as Replace (AG19-D18). Replace and Remove need `agent/document:edit` (AG19-D34). Home: AG-09 card AG-9-67. Not the same as Studio's toolbar button **Remove pages**, which takes pages out of a result (AG-19-8). "Remove" alone always means taking a file off a Document (AG19-D34). *Example:* Client C removes a duplicate pay stub that Client B sent twice. *Not the same as:* deleting the whole document (AG-09 §3.15).
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:to-file-list -->
**To file list** (no code name) — The list on a record (case, client, prospect or lead) of files a client sent that answer no specific request, and of the attachments of a reply to a request email that asked for several documents. A file the client attaches to a portal message also waits here (AG19-D61). Staff select files and click **File to line**, or click one of the requested lines shown on a file (AG19-D21, AG19-D32, AG19-D33, AG19-D40). Each item also offers **Open in PDF Studio**; after an unlock or any edit, **Save and file to line** files the result to a line in one step (AG19-D63). A junk file is taken off with the normal **Remove** (AG19-D47). Home: AG-09 card AG-9-68. *Example:* Client B uploads 3 files on the portal without picking a document; they wait on the To file list. *Not the same as:* the import review list for imported files that match no record (AG-9-47).
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:prepare-for-upload -->
**Prepare for upload** (no code name) — Studio's top tool. One click combines, converts, compresses to the size limit and names the file. Staff preview, then click Save (AG19-D13). *Example:* One click turns 4 pay stubs into one named PDF under 4 MB. *Not the same as:* Compress, a single tool on its own. A file name is built from the document type, the person's name, the case number and a date, for example `Pay-stubs_Ravi-Sharma_2026-11-12.pdf`; with no pattern in the pack, the name is `<document type>_<person>.pdf`. 1 MB = 1,000,000 bytes (AG19-D38). The size limits and naming rules per document type are pack content: SA-01B, SA-1B-106 (Not built). Home: AG-19 card AG-19-17.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:password-protected -->
**Password protected** (label) (`DocumentPart.passwordProtected`, `ToFileItem.passwordProtected`, proposed) — The label staff see on a file that reached a record still locked with a password: the client chose **Upload as is**, or the file came as an email attachment. Staff can unlock it in Studio (AG-19-13), except an interactive government form: Studio refuses it, and it is filled on the case's Forms tab (AG-19-5; AG19-D66). When staff save the unlocked result, the result part has no label; an unused locked part keeps its label (AG-9-69 rule 7; AG19-D63). Home: AG-09 card AG-9-69 (AG19-D49, AG19-D50). *Example:* Client B chose **Upload as is** for a locked bank statement; the file shows "Password protected". *Not the same as:* a file that only forbids changes, which is unlocked without asking (AG19-D48(a)); on a client upload, an interactive government form or a digitally signed PDF is never unlocked automatically (AG19-D64), and a locked government form is not unlocked even when the client types its password (AG19-D68).
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:digitally-signed -->
**Digitally signed** (no code name) — A PDF with a filled signature field: someone signed the document itself with a digital ID. A usage-rights signature alone (UR3 or DocMDP) does not count; a form maker adds one so that a free reader can fill and save the form (AG19-D73). Used by AG-19-7 rule 7 and AG-09 card AG-9-69 rules 5 and 5a. *Example:* Client B's employment contract, signed by his employer with a digital ID → digitally signed. A blank form that carries only usage rights → not digitally signed. *Not the same as:* the "Signed by client" tick on a government form (AG-7-21), which records that the client signed; it does not look inside the file.
<!-- /term -->

## Key terms — money (AG-05, AG-06) *(new 25 Oct — MS-D1 – MS-D23, AG05-D1 – AG05-D4)*

> **For writers and scripts (X-1 rule 4).** Same format and rules as "Key terms — cases and delivery" above. Written on 25 Oct from the money-slice decisions while AG-05 and AG-06 v2.0 were being written; if their "Start here" key terms differ, align these entries to those epics word for word. Examples use Maple Immigration, Client B (client), Client C and Client F (staff). Product text names no regulator.

<!-- term:trust-balance -->
**Trust balance** (no code name) — A trust balance is the client money the firm holds for one case. Each case has its own trust balance, in the case's one currency. Money that arrives before a case exists is held on the person or company, and moves to the case when the case opens. The person's or company's screen shows the total across their cases. ExpatReady never holds money; it records what the firm holds. A firm answers once, "Do you hold client money in a trust account? Yes · No", the first time it matters; a firm that answers No bills after the work. *Example:* Client B paid CAD 600 for his PR case, so Maple's case screen shows "Trust balance CAD 600". *Not the same as:* the amount an invoice asks for.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:trust-request -->
**Trust request** (no code name) — A trust request asks a client to pay money into trust before any work is billed. It is not an invoice. It carries a no-login pay link and the firm's trust payment details, and its amount includes estimated tax. *Example:* Client F sends Client B a trust request for CAD 1,130 (incl. estimated HST) from "Request payment". *Not the same as:* an invoice, which bills work that is done.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:deposit-request -->
**Deposit request** (no code name) — A deposit request is the trust request that is prepared when a retainer is signed. It is filled in from the retainer's deposit row and has its own reference, DR-… . It is a row on the Automatic messages page with the choices Send automatically, Ask me first and Off. The default is Ask me first: it waits in Needs you and one click sends it. Nothing is prepared when the retainer has no deposit row or the firm holds no client money in trust. *Example:* Client B signs, and "DR-0007, CAD 1,130" waits in Needs you; Client C clicks Send. *Not the same as:* an invoice. The deposit row is still billed with Bill this.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:trust-receipt -->
**Trust receipt** (no code name) — A trust receipt is the numbered receipt for money paid into trust. It is emailed automatically and records who paid. It shares one number series per firm with the payment receipt. *Example:* Client B pays his deposit and gets trust receipt R-0031. *Not the same as:* a payment receipt, which names an invoice.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:payment-receipt -->
**Payment receipt** (no code name) — A payment receipt is the numbered receipt for a payment against an invoice. It names the invoice and is emailed automatically. It shares one number series per firm with the trust receipt. *Example:* Client B pays invoice INV-0042 and gets payment receipt R-0032. *Not the same as:* a trust receipt, which is for money paid in advance.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:pay-from-trust -->
**Pay from trust** (no code name) — Pay from trust applies money from a case's trust balance to an invoice. It never applies more than the balance. It is ticked in advance on a draft made with Bill this when the case has a trust balance, and staff may lower or untick it. It also works on any issued unpaid invoice. Using money held for a different case is a deliberate pick that is recorded. It is switched off for an invoice marked "Client disputes this invoice" until that is cleared. *Example:* Client C sends Client B's invoice for CAD 1,000 with CAD 600 paid from trust; CAD 400 is due. *Not the same as:* a refund, which returns money to the client.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:to-move-list -->
**To move list** (no code name) — The To move list is the one list of what the firm still has to do with its own bank accounts. It shows trust money already applied to invoices but not yet moved to the operating account, and card fees to cover from the firm's own funds. It has a total and one click, "I moved these". The home screen shows one item only when something has waited more than 25 days. *Example:* Maple applied CAD 600 of Client B's trust to an invoice; it waits on the To move list until Client F moves it and clicks "I moved these". *Not the same as:* the monthly check.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:monthly-check -->
**Monthly check** (no code name) — The monthly check compares the bank's trust balance with what the firm's records say it should be. Staff type the bank's balance. ExpatReady compares it with the case balances, plus what waits to move, minus fees not yet covered, per currency, and shows card payouts still in transit. It saves the result with the date and the person. *Example:* Client F types CAD 12,400; the records say CAD 12,400 and the check is saved with her name and the date. *Not the same as:* a bank statement import, which is not in Release 1.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:ready-to-bill -->
**Ready to bill** (no code name) — Ready to bill is one list, for all clients, of schedule rows and costs not yet billed. A row appears on its "When" moment: On signing, On a date, When submitted, When decided or When done. The list shows; it never sends. Nothing is billed without a person. *Example:* Client B's PR was submitted, so the row "Submission, CAD 1,500" appears in Ready to bill. *Not the same as:* an invoice, which exists only after a person clicks Bill this.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:bill-this -->
**Bill this** (no code name) — Bill this is the button on a schedule row. It makes a draft invoice filled in with the row's service line, amount and tax. Staff may add lines, such as a government fee the firm paid, and then send it. One row makes one invoice. A Case Officer may make the draft; issuing needs the billing keys. *Example:* Client C clicks Bill this on "Deposit on signing" and adds the government fee the firm paid. *Not the same as:* Request payment, which asks for money before billing.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:add-cost -->
**Add cost** (no code name) — Add cost records a cost the firm paid for a client or case. It holds an amount, a date, a receipt and a label that the service suggests. The cost shows in Ready to bill and on close-out. Government fees are never taxed; other costs are taxable by default and the tax can change per line. *Example:* Client C adds CAD 85 for a courier with the receipt photo. *Not the same as:* a government fee, which the case's Government fees section holds.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:request-payment -->
**Request payment** (no code name) — Request payment is an action on any client or case. When the firm holds client money in trust, it sends a trust request. When it does not, nothing is requested and the firm bills after the work. It shows the "retainer isn't signed" warning when the case needs a signed agreement and has none. *Example:* Client F clicks Request payment on Client B's case and sends a trust request for CAD 600. *Not the same as:* Bill this, which bills a schedule row.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:services -->
**Services** (Settings → Services) — Services is the firm's one list of what it sells. It holds the pack programmes the firm uses and the firm's own services. The same list is "Interested in" and the web form. Each service may carry defaults, all blank at first: fee type and price, schedule rows, a disbursement estimate, a task template, a retainer template, documents to ask for, and "Needs a signed agreement" (on by default). Defaults are copied when a retainer, an amendment or a case is made. A later change never alters a sent or signed retainer, an open case or an invoice. A fee quoted on the consult record beats the service price. *Example:* Maple's service "Study permit" has a fixed price of CAD 2,500 and a deposit row of CAD 500. *Not the same as:* an appointment type, which holds the consult price.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:fees-panel -->
**Fees panel** (no code name) — The Fees panel holds money terms. It is the same panel on a service, a retainer, an amendment and a case. It lists the services with their fees, the fee type (Fixed or Hourly), the schedule rows, a disbursement estimate and the tax from the firm's settings. It warns, and never blocks, when the rows do not add up to the fee. *Example:* On Client B's retainer the panel shows a fixed fee of CAD 4,500 in three rows. *Not the same as:* the refund policy and payment terms, which stay the firm's own clause text.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:schedule-row -->
**Schedule row** (no code name) — A schedule row is one line of the fee schedule: a label, an amount and a "When". The first row is "Deposit on signing". Each row is billed by Bill this, one invoice per row. A row does not bill itself. *Example:* "Submission, CAD 1,500, When submitted" is a schedule row on Client B's retainer. *Not the same as:* a payment plan, which is withdrawn.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:firm-service-case -->
**Firm-service case** (`Case.kind = FIRM_SERVICE`) — A firm-service case is a simple case for something the firm sells that is not a pack programme. It has no pack programme, filing, government form, government fee table, government deadline or readiness. Its states are Open and Done. It has tasks, documents, notes, messages, a Fees panel, invoices, trust and close-out. It needs no retainer unless the service says so. **Mark done** closes it as Closed with the outcome Completed (BUILD-7). It is reopened with the same key as Mark done, a reason and an audit entry (BUILD-26). *Example:* Maple opens "Firm service: Resume review" for Client B's wife and marks it done. *Not the same as:* a case on a pack programme.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:close-out -->
**Close-out** (no code name) — Close-out is the check when a case ends. It starts when the closed case has unbilled work, or a trust balance, a credit or an open balance. One home-screen item, dated 30 days after closing, goes to the case lead. The Close out screen shows a final statement, every unbilled item, a final invoice, Pay from trust, refunds, and "Mark settled" with a reason. It never blocks closing the case. *Example:* Client B's case closes with CAD 600 in trust; the item reads "Settle Client B's money: CAD 600 in trust, by 24 Nov". *Not the same as:* closing the case itself.
<!-- /term -->

<!-- term:getting-paid -->
**Getting paid** (Settings → Getting paid) — Getting paid is the one settings page for money. The main part holds how clients pay (the card account, the operating payment details with the Interac e-Transfer email, the trust payment details), the tax and the tax number, and trust. Advanced holds where invoice payments go, numbering and due days. *Example:* Maple's Owner adds the Interac email and the trust bank details here. *Not the same as:* the Services list, which holds prices.
<!-- /term -->
