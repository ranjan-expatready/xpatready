# Counsel pack: what we need counsel to read and answer before the pilot

*Prepared by the Lead, 11 Oct 2026, for counsel engaged by 31 Oct 2026 (AIW-D68). Expat Ready is building AI assistants for licensed Canadian immigration firms (consultants licensed by the College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants, and lawyers). The founder has decided that the product does not launch without them (AIW-D66), and that no pilot runs on real client files until counsel has read this pack (AIW-D64). Several safety decisions are the founder's and are provisional until counsel answers (AIW-D55 to D58). The seven behaviour documents (`../design/behaviours/`) each end with a section 7 for counsel; this pack merges their 76 items into 14 questions and points to the detail. General-knowledge statements of law below are ours, not advice, and are for counsel to correct.*

## How the assistants work, in five lines (so the questions make sense)

1. Each assistant works for one named staff member of a firm, under a record of which of that person's permissions it may use; it has no login and no permissions of its own (founder decisions FD-16, AIW-D33, D36).
2. It reads documents, sorts messages, cross-checks facts and drafts text; it proposes, and a named person approves anything that reaches a client, a form or a record (AIW-D26).
3. It never: deletes, makes a money entry, exports, changes users or settings, signs, holds a professional approval, moves a case, files anything with the government, or sends unapproved free text to a client (the hard limits, AIW-D55, provisional).
4. AI processing of client data happens only in Canada, on a provider with no training on and no retention of client data (AIW-D52); the AI is switched off for every firm until that provider is recorded.
5. Every step is recorded: who the assistant worked for, what it proposed, from which document, who approved, and what changed.
