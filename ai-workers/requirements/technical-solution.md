## 1. The AI provider: which one to plan on, and what a change would cost

### 1.1 In one minute

- **The rule (changed 14 Oct 2026).** Client data is **stored in Canada**. AI and OCR processing of client data may happen **in Canada or the United States**, only under written no-training and zero-data-retention terms, with client notice ([AIW-D147](ref:AIW-D147), replacing AIW-D52's "only in Canada"). No other region and no "global" routing. The same rule covers every AI call Expat Ready makes, pack drafting and support tickets included (AIW-D74 correction 2).
- **Why the founder changed it.** Under "only in Canada", one model qualified (Gemini 3.5 Flash), and it retires "May 19, 2027 or later", about ten weeks after the planned launch (Program Charter V1 – G1 expanded, line 13: "Market launch: 12 March 2027"; AIW-D4: "the March 2027 launch"). No law we found requires Canada-only processing (Unconfirmed: no counsel). Version 0.2 of this section, with that comparison, is kept in its Document control row.
- **Recommendation (Proposal).**
  - **Primary: Claude Sonnet 5.5 on Amazon Bedrock**, called from Expat Ready's Canadian region (`ca-central-1`) through Bedrock's US profile, so each request is processed in Canada or the US and nowhere else. Claude Haiku 5.5 for simple steps where it passes the skill's tests. Reader OCR through Amazon Textract in Canada, on its own condition (below).
  - **Condition on the model (load-bearing, §1.5.1).** Bedrock qualifies under AIW-D147 **only if** the account runs in AWS's zero-retention setting (`data_retention_mode: none`) in Canada and the three US regions, and AWS confirms in writing that this setting covers Sonnet and Haiku 5.5, prompt caching included. The no-training term is already written (Anthropic's terms for Claude on Bedrock). **If AWS will not confirm zero retention in writing, Claude on Bedrock does not qualify**, and the primary becomes the same model through the Anthropic API with a zero-retention agreement. That route has its own written exception, wider than Bedrock's: content Anthropic's automated safety systems flag may be kept "for up to 2 years", and Batch processing and file uploads sit outside zero retention (§1.5).
  - **Condition on the OCR (load-bearing, §1.5.2).** By AWS's own contract, Textract may keep scanned pages and use them to improve the service, and may store them outside Canada for that purpose, **unless** an AWS Organizations AI-services opt-out policy is set. The opt-out must be in force before the first client page is sent, and AWS must confirm in writing that Textract keeps no page beyond the request (no zero-retention switch is documented for Textract). Without that confirmation the Textract route does not qualify and the Reader's OCR step waits for a route that does.
  - **Fallback: Microsoft's current GPT models (the GPT-6 family) in Microsoft's US Data Zone**, once Microsoft approves switching off its abuse-monitoring storage.
  - The provider stays an Assumption until a contract and a passed test call exist (AIW-D139; SA-8-66).
- **Why this primary.** It fits the new rule's regions exactly. The product already stores files on AWS in Canada (SA-08 v2.7 SA-8-6, "Amazon S3 in Canada (Montréal)"; as-built note: "S3 in `ca-central-1`"), so it is the same cloud and account; the model itself comes under AWS Marketplace third-party terms (Anthropic's terms for Claude on Bedrock), and access to Sonnet 5.5 may have criteria (Unknown). - **Does the founder's Assumption still hold?** Yes, more firmly: on paper, several routes appear to offer all three terms (AIW-A11 as widened by AIW-D147), though none is yet confirmed in writing for our account. **In plain words.** "Processing" means the computer that runs the model and reads the client's words. Even when the provider keeps nothing, sending the documents to a US computer is a transfer of personal information, which is why clients are told. "Zero data retention" means the provider keeps no copy of the prompt or the answer once it has replied. "Abuse monitoring" means a provider keeping flagged prompts for a while to check for misuse; it is a form of retention, so it must be off.

### 1.7 What a switch of provider costs (AIW-D143)

A provider change needs no redesign and no code change, but is a **material change** for every AI skill ([AIW-D143](ref:AIW-D143); AW-1-149(d), AW-1-152; AW-2-80 for the platform skills). A change of model within the same provider (for example Sonnet 5.5 to its successor) is the same kind of change (AW-1-149(d)).

**What each AI skill goes through, all 20 of them (the same on every route):**

1. A new skill version with the new provider and model route.
2. Its acceptance sets pass on the new route (AW-1-119, AW-1-155).
3. A second person, not its author, releases it (AW-1-150(a)).
4. It runs **2 to 4 weeks of shadow on real work** beside the older version, which stays live (AW-1-150(b), AW-1-152(b)). Shadow lasts longer where the evidence is still short after four weeks, and it needs a sample graded by the licensed expert (AW-1-150(b); AIW-D83).
5. **Each other firm** then gets its own short check, its length set from evidence (AW-1-150(c)); earned *Auto*, where any exists, is re-earned from the start (AW-1-152(c)).

The product's own AI Doers (the Reader with its OCR, the Sorter, the Writer and the Checker; AG-11) call through the same provider record, so their evaluation sets re-run too (AW-1-119; AW-2-26 for the platform assistants). The older version keeps running only while its own model is still available, and on Bedrock only while it keeps traffic: in the Legacy period, "existing customers may lose access after 15 days of inactivity".

### 2.1 In one minute

- **One path for every AI step.** The product says who is acting and for which firm; the assistant prepares; the product shows the exact preview; a person approves; the product performs the effect once and writes its audit entry and its event in the same database transaction.
- **Most AI steps change nothing on their own.** Reading, sorting, drafting, cross-checking and explaining only produce proposals or answers. Running one again is harmless; the double-run key (AG-11-25; register TC-88) stops the same job twice.
- **Effects are where a duplicate hurts.** In the code today, a duplicate-protection key exists only on pack operations (form instances, assessments, pack authoring and publishing), firm-lifecycle operations and Expat Ready's own subscription payments. The firm-side effects an assistant prepares outside packs (client messages, notes, tasks, stage moves, approved values, firm-to-client invoices, trust entries, sending a retainer for signature) have no key, or no product operation yet. **New register row TC-175 (P1)** asks the team for one. Until it exists, AW-1-164 and its test TC-AW1-393 cannot pass: AG-11-25 is Specified, not built.
- **Five services the AI relies on are not in the code yet:** document reading (OCR), firm-to-client invoices, e-signature, support tickets and the AI record types. Each service's home is the team's own requirement, assumed built (AIW-D21); the register rows beside it are related AI-specific asks, not requests to build the service (§2.6).

### 2.7 Words in this section

| Word | Meaning here |
|---|---|
| Effect | A change the product makes that someone outside the AI would notice: a message sent, an invoice issued, a stage moved |

| Natural protection | The operation cannot happen twice by its nature (a status can move from "submitted" to "approved" only once) |

| Lost response | The request reached the product, but the answer never came back, so the caller cannot tell whether the effect happened |
