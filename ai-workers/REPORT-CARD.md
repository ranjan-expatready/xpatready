### 6.2 Lens by lens: evidence and what to change

**1. Workflows, not free agents.** The article's test: "Workflows are systems where LLMs and tools are orchestrated through predefined code paths"; agents "dynamically direct their own processes and tool usage". It advises "finding the simplest solution possible, and only increasing complexity when needed", notes that "workflows offer predictability and consistency for well-defined tasks", and says "The key to success, as with any LLM features, is measuring performance and iterating on implementations" (anthropic.com/research/building-effective-agents, published 19 Dec 2024, opened 10 Oct 2026). Our work: every persona "is a recipe, not a model with hands" (`design/ai-organisation.md` §1.5; IMP-10), the engine runs an ordered list of product actions and "a model never selects which action runs" (resting on AG-11 v1.11 AG-11-94); the expert co-pilot answers only (AIW-D25). That is the workflow shape with human checkpoints ("Agents can then pause for human feedback at checkpoints", same article). **Gap:** we have measures named (IMP-29, IMP-30) but no plan for *what* to measure the Doers against before a firm relies on them. **Change (plan):** Step 3's gate now requires an evaluation set per AI Doer: a fixed set of sample documents and questions with known right answers, run before every release and before any review level is lowered (`PROGRESS.md` Step 3 gate, updated today).

**2. NIST AI RMF 1.0.** The framework's four functions are Govern (roles, policies, accountability), Map (context and risks per use), Measure (test, benchmark, monitor), Manage (respond, stop, improve) (NIST AI 100-1, Jan 2023; read through secondary summaries 10 Oct 2026; confirm against the NIST text). **Govern 8:** rules 1 to 10, the delegation record (IMP-1, AIW-D33), hard limits (IMP-27), who approves by role (AIW-D26), the instruction-change control (IMP-5). **Map 6:** each persona card lists regulatory flags, but there is no risk register per persona (what can go wrong, how likely, how bad, who owns it). **Measure 4:** IMP-29 names measures; nothing says the thresholds or the evaluation method. **Manage 7:** emergency stops (IMP-8), spend caps (IMP-36), run stops (IMP-35), shadow mode (IMP-30); but no incident procedure: what happens after a wrong AI-prepared value reaches a client (IMP-33 asks for review of such output; nobody has written who is told, what is withdrawn, what is recorded). **Change (plan):** Step 3 adds, inside the requirements documents (not a side file), a risk register per persona and one incident procedure, laid out in the four functions so an investor's or insurer's AI-governance reviewer recognises it (`PROGRESS.md` Step 3 artefact, updated today). ISO/IEC 42001 (2023, the AI management-system standard; general knowledge) would ask for the same, plus a written AI policy; that is counsel and compliance work after Step 3.

**3. Human-AI interaction guidelines.** Microsoft's HAX guidelines (18 guidelines, 2019) and Google's PAIR guidebook (general knowledge) ask a product to make clear what the AI can do and how well, show why it did something, support efficient correction, and scope down when uncertain. Our approvals queue shows "what will happen … why … the provenance of every value and the confidence of any AI output" (AG-11-56), allows approve, edit-then-approve or reject with a reason (AG-11-57), flags stale items (AG-11-61), and shadow mode shows proposals beside the person's own entry until the agreement rate earns trust (IMP-30). **Risk the guidelines name and we have not addressed:** automation bias, the documented tendency of people to accept a machine's suggestion without checking (Parasuraman and Manzey, 2010, "Complacency and bias in human use of automation"; general knowledge). Our own one-item-per-file design (IMP-34) makes approval faster and therefore makes rubber-stamping easier. **Change (proposal, for Step 2 integration and Step 3):** three safeguards: (a) "approve all" on a readiness report opens every value below a confidence threshold before it can be pressed; (b) a small random share of values is shown blank for the approver to enter, and the match rate is a measure (a "holdout check"); (c) an alarm when a person's agreement rate with AI proposals climbs above a firm-set level in a short time, which is the signature of rubber-stamping. Logged in `proposals.md` today.

**4. Legal hallucination evidence.** The study found general-purpose models produce "textual output that is not consistent with legal facts" at rates "between 58% of the time with ChatGPT 4 and 88% with Llama 2" on questions about US federal court cases, and that "Even experienced lawyers must remain wary of legal hallucinations" (arxiv.org/abs/2401.01301, submitted 2 Jan 2024, revised 21 Jun 2024; opened 10 Oct 2026). The same group's later study of retrieval-based legal research tools found lower but still material error rates (reported in trade coverage as 17 to 33 percent; secondary, label before use). **Our design answers this correctly:** no AI answers immigration law from memory; the expert co-pilot answers from pack rule text and case records with sources only and refuses without a source (IMP-20; AIW-D25); the rules engine, not a model, does scoring and checklists (AG-03; IMP-37); every AI value is a proposal with its source document and confidence (AG-11-70). **What is missing:** proof. Nothing has been measured on our own content. **Change:** the evaluation set in lens 1 covers this; the investor story quotes the study as the reason for the design, never as a claim about our error rate.

**5. Decision hygiene.** "Noise" (2021; general knowledge) prescribes structured judgement, independent assessments before discussion, and rules where rules beat judgement. Our scoring and eligibility run on the pack's rules with the same result for the same input and pack version (AG-03 v1.4; catalogue G10): rules where rules are better. Four-eyes gives a second, independent human check on government forms (AG-7-65; AG-16 v1.12 §3.10). **Weakness:** an approver who sees the AI's proposed value first is anchored by it; that is the bias in lens 3. The holdout check (lens 3, safeguard b) is the decision-hygiene fix.

**6. Product discovery.** Cagan's test (general knowledge): did real users touch a prototype before the team built anything; are we judged on outcomes, not output. Torres: weekly customer touchpoints. **Our standing:** zero. The Step 1 page exists; no customer, no licensed consultant, no investor has seen it. The plan puts conversations in Step 5 and the prototype test in Step 3, which is discovery after design. **This is the weakest lens, and the cheapest to fix.** **Change (plan, no new artefact):** the founder shows the Step 1 page to three licensed consultants from the warm network this week and asks the §2 questions of `commercial/validation-script.md` (how a file is built today) and the two story questions. Three conversations are not the ten; they are the first signal, and they start the Step 5 count. Also: one licensed consultant reads the Step 1 page as a reviewer (lens 18).

**7. Hypothesis testing.** Lean practice (general knowledge): every assumption states what result would prove it wrong and how it will be tested. `assumptions.md` listed ten assumptions, why we need each and the effect if wrong, with no test and no kill criterion. **Fixed today:** two columns added, "What would prove it wrong" and "Test", filled for every row. The ones that can kill the wedge: A3 (no Canada-processing provider by Step 3), A4 (no specialist authoring the Canada pack before the pilot), A6 (pilot saves under one hour per file or review time exceeds time saved), A7 (fewer than four of ten conversations accept a per-file price).

**8. Beachhead and whole product.** Moore (general knowledge): pick one segment and give it the *whole product* it needs to get the result, not the core alone. Our beachhead is FD-19's "Solo and small RCIC practices" (Founder Decisions Log v1.5); the page leads with the solo firm (AIW-D34). The whole product for a solo consultant to get value on day one is: the case records (catalogue D7, "Specified, not built"), the Minder (D5, "Specified, not built"), the Canada pack content (D2, not authored), and a Canada-region provider (A3). None exists. Dunford's positioning test (general knowledge): name the competitive alternative, the unique attributes, the value and the segment; the price card and the plan do this honestly and the "they cannot copy it" line is labelled an assumption (REPORT-CARD §2, Direction). **Change (investor story, Step 6):** one plain "whole product" line next to the vision: what must exist before a solo firm gets value, and its status.

**9. Durable advantage.** Helmer's seven powers (scale economies, network effects, counter-positioning, switching costs, branding, cornered resource, process power; general knowledge). None is demonstrated. Candidates: switching costs once a firm's audit record, delegations and approved values live in the product; a cornered resource if the Canada packs are authored with recognised specialists and kept current faster than rivals; counter-positioning if rivals' "AI review" products cannot adopt a named-person-approves, every-value-traced model without hurting their speed story (unknown; `research/visaflo.md` §16.2). **Verdict:** the moat is an assumption, already scored as such (Direction 6). **Change:** none now; the pilot and the pack-authoring decision decide which power is real.

**10. Price before build.** "Monetizing Innovation" (general knowledge): have the willingness-to-pay conversation before the product exists; test the charge unit, not just the number. Van Westendorp's method (general knowledge) asks four price questions; our script asks two ("too cheap to trust", "too expensive to consider") plus an unprompted expected price and a price ladder (CAD 15 and 60 probes) (`commercial/validation-script.md` §5). **Verdict:** right method for ten conversations; a quantitative survey would come after the pilot. **Change:** none to the method; run it.

**11. Customer conversations.** "The Mom Test" (general knowledge): ask about their life and past behaviour, not your idea; opinions and compliments are not data; a real signal is a commitment of time, reputation or money. The script opens with "Walk me through the last file you finished" and "What software do you pay for today" (§2), tells the stories only after the facts (§3), tells the interviewer "Do not defend the claim" (§4), and closes with a pilot ask and a referral ask (§7): all Mom-Test-correct. **Weakness:** §4's 1–5 ratings of our claims are opinions and will run high out of politeness. **Fixed today:** §9 "Comparing ten sheets" now weights commitments (pilot yes, referral given, a number at which they say no, hours and prices stated) above ratings, and says a claim rated 5 with no commitment counts as unproven.

**12. Professional services transformation.** Susskind (general knowledge) predicts legal work decomposed into tasks, routinised and priced for outcomes, with professionals resisting until clients demand "more for less". The catalogue's 28 workers are that decomposition of a consultant's file work; the per-prepared-file unit is outcome pricing; the Deadline watch as a free hook is a "more for less" entry. **Risk he names:** the profession's resistance. Our "assistant to the role" wording (AIW-D27, D31) and shadow mode are the right posture; the regulator questions in the script (§6) measure the resistance rather than argue with it.

**13. The regulator's rulebook.** The Code of Professional Conduct for College of Immigration and Citizenship Consultants Licensees, SOR/2022-128, "current to 2026-09-21" (laws-lois.justice.gc.ca, opened 10 Oct 2026). Each design choice maps to a section; the mapping is ours and counsel has not read it:
- s. 19(2)(e): a licensee must "be able to deliver the services to the client using technology that is appropriate and effective". Supports the whole idea; also a duty to pick technology that works.
- s. 22(2): the licensee "must ensure that all the necessary documents and information are, as applicable, properly prepared, signed and submitted". This is why a named person approves every readiness report and form (AIW-D26; AG-07 v2.7 §3.6) and why no persona submits anything (AG-11-54).
- s. 22(3): "The licensee must provide timely information to the client in writing concerning the status of their case, including by … notifying the client when a document or information has been submitted or received". The Letter Reader's client notice (W-03) and the template chases help a licensee meet this duty; their approval point stays with the firm's setting (AG11-D11(a)).
- s. 28(1): keep client information confidential and "take the measures that are necessary to maintain the confidentiality of that information indefinitely". This is the no-training, no-retention and residency rule (AG-16 v1.12 §3.9; AG11-D15; IMP-7) and the restricted-field rule (IMP-21).
- s. 38(1): a licensee must ensure a person who assists "is provided with a copy of this Code and is familiar with its contents" and "does not carry out any acts that, if carried out by the licensee, would contravene this Code". Whether s. 38 reaches software is a counsel question; if it does, the hard limits (IMP-27), the blocked list (IMP-23) and the controlled instructions (IMP-5) are the AI's "copy of the Code".
- s. 38(2): the licensee "must supervise and assume professional responsibility for any work done by a person who assists" with supervision "adequate for the type of work in question". This is the autonomy level per action and the floors (AG-11-51, AG-11-53): more supervision for client-facing and government-facing work, less for internal filing.
- s. 38(3): a licensee "may delegate certain aspects of their work to a person who is not a licensee" but must ensure that person "does not provide representation or advice in contravention of section 91" of the Immigration and Refugee Protection Act. This is the advice boundary (IMP-28; readiness reports and response plans are internal until a person decides what reaches the client).
**Change:** Step 2's "confirm with counsel" list (section 7 of every behaviour document) cites these sections exactly; the Lead adds them at integration of the Case assistant document. Counsel reads the mapping before the Step 5 conversations ask the regulator questions.

**14. The courts.** The Federal Court's page "Artificial Intelligence" (fct-cf.ca, last modified 29 Sep 2025, opened 10 Oct 2026) links its "Notice on the Use of Artificial Intelligence in Court Proceedings", covering "the use of AI by parties, self-represented litigants and interveners", and dates its own interim principles 20 Dec 2023 and 29 Sep 2025. Law-firm commentary (read 10 Oct 2026, secondary) says the notice requires a declaration in the first paragraph of any document prepared for litigation that contains AI-generated content, and that the May 2024 update lets the declaration name specific paragraphs. The field scan already records this (`research/field-scan.md` §6 item 9). **Change (proposal):** W-06 letters carry a "litigation document" flag that inserts the declaration and routes the draft to the representative; the exact notice text is read by counsel before the flag's wording is fixed.

**15. Privacy.** Quebec's Law 25 (general knowledge) requires a privacy impact assessment for a project that acquires or redesigns an information system handling personal information, and conditions for transfers outside Quebec; PIPEDA requires accountability and meaningful consent (general knowledge; SA-11 v2.7 names both). Our files carry the points (`commercial/provider-and-cost.md` §4; IMP-7; IMP-11 client notice) and the residency decision is the recorded blocking risk. **Missing:** no step in the plan produces a privacy impact assessment before a pilot touches real client files. **Change (plan):** Step 4's readiness checklist includes a privacy impact assessment signed off by counsel before any pilot on real files (`PROGRESS.md` Step 4 row, updated today).

**16. Augmentation over automation.** "The Turing Trap" (2022; general knowledge) argues that AI built to replace people concentrates gains and meets resistance, while AI that augments spreads gains; "Co-Intelligence" (2024; general knowledge) adds "be the human in the loop" and "assume this is the worst AI you will ever use". Our personas are assistants to a role, never a user (FD-16; AIW-D27, D31), with review lowered only as trust is measured (IMP-30). **Caution from the second book:** do not hard-code today's limits (XFA forms the engine refuses, SA-01B v1.2 §12; today's hallucination rates) into the product's permanent rules. Step 3 already designs the provider as replaceable (PROGRESS plan review, Technical lens); the review levels are settings, not code (AG-11-51). Good.

**17. The investor's checklist.** Sequoia's outline and Y Combinator's guidance (general knowledge) ask: why now, why you, what is built, who pays, what is the moat, how much and for what. Why now: adoption surveys report sharp rises in legal professionals' AI use (one trade-press summary of the 2024 Clio Legal Trends Report puts it at 79 percent, up from 19 percent; secondary, verify against the report before quoting) and the regulator has no AI guidance yet (`research/field-scan.md` §6 item 9). Why you: a licensed founder-consultant selling to peers (`../commercial/plan.md` §1). What is built: nothing of Workstream 2; the product's own case records and Minder are specified, not built. Who pays: untested. Moat: lens 9. How much: undecided (AIW-D12). **Verdict 3, honestly early.** Step 6 is correctly last in the plan.

**18. Our own operating model.** Maker, hostile checker, Lead, founder; every claim sourced or labelled; a report card that lowers its own scores. Red-teaming practice (general knowledge) endorses an adversary who assumes the work is wrong, which review S1 did (fail, fixable; 91 of 95 citations held). **Two risks:** the checker is the same model family as the maker, so a shared blind spot passes both; and no human with domain expertise has read a line. **Change (plan):** one licensed consultant reads the Step 1 page before Step 2 ends (lens 6); from Step 2, one adversarial review per step runs on a different model (Opus 5.5) when the usage budget allows, and the deviation from AIW-D29 is logged.
