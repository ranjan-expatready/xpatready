What we considered

Four ways to organise the AI, compared fairly

The team's current design

Four AI helpers already specified, with an approval queue

The team's automation module specifies four AI helpers it calls "Doers" (Reader, Sorter, Drafter, Checker), started by a small automation engine, with an autonomy level on every action (automatic, approve first, or off), a "Needs you" approval queue and a "Done for you" feed. One firm switch turns them on. Option C below builds on it; the review inbox on this page is that queue.

The assistant names are proposed business references for each area's AI responsibility, not five workers, faces or engines (terminology register §2). Staff meet one face: your assistant. How the skills are coordinated stays open until M1.

The owner's co-pilot

The same assistant every role uses, but for the firm owner it covers every case, user and figure in the firm: "how is the firm doing?", "which files are stuck?". Figures come from the reports, never from AI arithmetic. Version 1 answers a fixed menu of questions (B·7).

No client-side AI

The client never chats with AI and never sees AI-written words before the firm approves them. Behind the scenes, AI may sort a website chat question so a rule shows the firm's approved answer, and read uploads for staff to confirm. No client co-pilot, no AI eligibility advice to a client.

Skill types: what each does

11 in the catalogue

Reader

Reads documents, letters, guidance and replies into proposed facts, each beside its source.

e.g. Document Reader, Government Letter Reader, Official Guidance Reader

8 in the catalogue

Sorter

Labels and routes what arrives, and proposes matches.

e.g. "This upload is a police certificate" · Form Field Matcher · Import Column Matcher

15 in the catalogue

Writer

Drafts messages, letters, notes, explanations and setup content for a person to approve.

e.g. Client Message Writer, Case Letter Writer, PQR/SQR Builder

7 in the catalogue

Checker

Cross-checks a form, a draft or a file and flags findings. Never changes a value.

e.g. Fact Consistency Checker, Form Consistency Checker, Advice Risk Checker

2 in the catalogue

Filler

Rules, not AI. Transfers confirmed facts into PDF forms and switched-on portals through released connections, then reads them back.

e.g. PDF Form Filler (key 7), Portal Form Filler (key 27)

22 in the catalogue

Rules

Calculates, selects, schedules and prepares. Same answer every time, versioned and dated.

e.g. Eligibility Calculator, Deadline Calculator, Case Setup

1 in the catalogue

Co-pilot

One entry point on request. Uses the same skills inside the person's permissions; never a second copy of them.

e.g. your assistant: "what's missing on this file?"

  • Owner: the same assistant, covering every case, user and figure in the firm. Figures come from the reports, never from AI arithmetic
  • How-to: "how do I add a family member?" PROPOSAL
  • It sees and does only what that person is allowed to

One assistant on request for every role; version 1 answers a fixed menu of questions (B·7).

The firm's controls

  • AI is off until the provider's terms are recorded
  • The Owner switches AI on (the team's design has one firm switch; per area is our proposal, decided at M1), chooses a preset ("review each step" or a lighter one) and can fine-tune
  • Internal notes, tasks and filing labels can earn Auto, only after the skill's tests pass, checked examples from real use and the Owner's approval (a clean approval rate alone is not enough). They drop back when quality falls. None at the first pilot firm
  • Client messages never earn Auto
  • Spend caps; new client-facing preparation pauses if no licensed user is active (the Owner may override)
