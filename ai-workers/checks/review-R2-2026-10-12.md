- **Major:** a wrong or overstated claim the founder would act on, a number with no source, or a breach of the citation rules (cite team IDs; never restate).

## Verdict: **FAIL**

There is 1 blocking finding (B-1), 7 major findings (M-1 to M-7) and 22 minor findings (m-1 to m-22).

**What is good.** Read these before the findings:
- The main answer is right and well supported. A hosted model call cannot be made exactly repeatable. Repeatability has to come from storing and replaying answers. Rules stay in code. I confirmed the key sources word for word: Google's "mostly deterministic" and "best effort" wording, Cohere's "cannot be totally guaranteed", Thinking Machines' 80 unique answers out of 1,000 and the batch-invariant fix, the vLLM and SGLang modes, the ExtractBench figures for Gemini 3.5 Flash, and the EU AI Act Article 15(3) wording.
- The "rule of three" maths is correct. With zero errors in n cases, the 95% upper bound on the error rate is about 3/n, so about 300 cases for 1% and 600 for 0.5%.
- The cost arithmetic is correct: USD 1.27 × 3 = USD 3.81, and the 1.27 figure is A §6.3's.
- The section on what not to do, and the honest "what we can say / cannot say" line (line 232), are exactly what the founder needs.
- Vendor benchmarks are flagged as vendor-run (ExtractBench, IDP Leaderboard, the "Confidently Wrong" study, AWS).
- No personal names and no email addresses appear. Authors are described by organisation (AIW-D35).
- No recommendation sends AI processing outside Canada (AIW-D74). OpenAI appears only as context (line 55).

**What must change before it can pass:** see §5 at the end.
