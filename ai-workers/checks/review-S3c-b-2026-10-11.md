**N-1. The lead block is enforced only for assistant recipes. Seeds, firm automations and hand sends have no enforcement point.**
- **Where.** `CN-AG-11.md` L175 (AG-11-114 (g)); `CN-AG-12.md` L22 (a), L28 (knock-on); `CN-AG-10.md` (no change).
- **Evidence.** AG-12-38 (a) forbids sending a marked template to a lead "not by an automation, not by an AI assistant …, and not by a person sending it by hand". CN-AG-12's knock-on (L28) says the engine applies this through "AG-11 item 9 (g) of CN-AG-11 (the engine applies the default and refuses a lead send)". But AG-11-114 (g) is limited to "When an **assistant recipe** would send a template marked …". The seeds that write to leads are the riskiest path, and nothing in AG-11 refuses them: speed-to-lead (AG-11 L369, "acknowledgement to the lead"), going-cold and post-consult follow-up (AG-11-106). For a person's own send from the AG-10 composer, no note adds a check, and CN-AG-12's knock-ons do not list AG-10. A developer building the engine from AG-11, or the composer from AG-10, would not build the refusal.
- **Fix.** Put the check in one place that every send passes through. For example: "AG-12-38 (a): the template engine (Drafter) SHALL refuse to render a marked template for any recipient who is a lead, whatever the send path (seed, firm automation, AI assistant, a person's manual send)". Then widen AG-11-114 (g) to "every action" or replace it with a pointer to AG-12-38, and add a one-line knock-on to CN-AG-10 for the composer.

**N-2. The block on a person's hand-send rests on an instruction that is not written down, and it adds a fifth fixed safeguard against AG-11 BR-34 without recording the conflict.**
- **Where.** `CN-AG-12.md` L22 (a), L24, L27; `CN-AG-03.md` L33, L34. Source: AG-11 v1.12 L25 and L764 (BR-34).
