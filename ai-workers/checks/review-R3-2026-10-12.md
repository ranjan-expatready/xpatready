## Verdict: **FAIL**

There is 1 blocking finding (B-1), 8 major findings (M-1 to M-8) and 20 minor findings (m-1 to m-20).

**The blocking finding in one line.** The file's central "honest version" has the AI teammate *run the eligibility assessment* when a person asks it to. The founder decided the opposite on 11 Oct (AIW-D40), the specification says it (AW-1-88), and the sibling maker file says it too (A18: "May not run it"). The file presents this as current design and rates it "safe". It never says it reopens a founder decision.

**What is good.** Read these before the findings.
- The main recommendation is sound and honest. Do not call rule results "AI". Name the AI for language work. Make "same facts, same answer" the trust argument. The evidence supports it.
- Most legal quotes are word-perfect at the primary source: Competition Act s. 74.01(1)(a) and (b), s. 74.03(5), the s. 74.1 penalty amounts, the Bureau's 2023 statement, CICC Code ss. 19(2)(e), 38(2), 38(3) and 44(2)(a) and (b), the FTC sweep and DoNotPay, and the SEC's Delphia and Global Predictions order. The IRB notice is also exact.
- The market quotes I opened are exact: TurboTax's guarantee and "AI-powered guidance from Intuit Assist", Pega's "100% accuracy" sentence, Salesforce's "deterministic control", Intercom's "$0.99 per outcome", Salesforce's USD 125, the EvenUp per-case wording, the WSU study and the Thomson Reuters 96/94/90/47%.
- The plan figures 12 founding firms, 4 to 5 a month, 3,900 to 7,800 practices and 12,500 consultants match `plan.md` v1.1. The arithmetic is right, including the Lead's corrected nine years.
- The file is honest about how hard the multi-million path is. It says Canada alone needs 6 to 26% of practices, and that the AI label is not what makes the money.
- It challenges the Lead's own "rules-verified" wording with evidence (SA1B-D18).
- No personal names or email addresses. Officials are described by role.
