**How severity is used here (the brief's scale).**
- **Major:** a wrong or overstated claim, or a passage a firm owner could not understand. I count a claim as Major when it would change what a firm owner believes about safety, supervision, money, privacy, or how much of the work is really AI.
- **Minor:** everything else, including wording that is imprecise but does not change any of those beliefs.

---

## Verdict: **Fail**

There are 10 major findings (M-1 to M-10) and 42 minor ones (m-1 to m-42). The minor IDs are grouped by topic, so they are not in strict number order.

Most fixes are one-line edits. Three need the Lead to make a call:
- **M-1:** what counts as a teammate's own AI skill.
- **M-8:** how wide one skill may be.
- **m-16:** how Practice answers is presented.

**What is good.** Read these before the findings:
- All seven profiles carry every heading AIW-D88 asks for. Each also has a "what can go wrong" section.
- The structure reads like an introduction to a colleague.
- The "Never does" lists are almost all supported, line by line.
- The catalogue's "Built on" IDs match Register B on every one of the 20 rows (one stray ID, m-23).
- No Workstream 2-only number is passed off as a team ID.
- The arithmetic holds: 28 jobs, 18 skills, 13 plus 5.
- The honesty markers are right: no time saving is claimed, nothing is said to be built (AW-01 §12), and the health light is labelled NEW in §4.
- No personal names. All examples use made-up data. The retired words appear only as cross-references in the footnotes.
