# Price card: three ways to charge for the File Builder, to test with 5–10 licensed consultants

*Workstream 2, wave W3b. Written by the commercial-strategist agent on 9 Oct 2026. Draft for the Lead to merge; nothing here changes `../../commercial/plan.md` or `model.xlsx` on its own (README §2 D4: the AI section is added to the existing plan, not a competing one). Every number is either sourced (file and line, or URL and access date) or labelled **Assumption**. Every option is a **Proposal**. Used in [`validation-script.md`](validation-script.md) §5. Page 2 (the three half-pages) is what the consultant sees; page 1 and page 3 are for the founder only.*

---

## Page 1 (founder only): what this sits on

**The unit.** A prepared file = "one File Builder run on one case whose readiness report a person approved (an abandoned or rejected run is not billed; a re-run on the same case within N days is not a new unit; N: the founder)" (`../design/ws2-improvements.md` IMP-18, Proposal). The Deadline Guardian is the hook and is proposed free (`../design/direction-review-2026-10-09.md` §4 item 6: "Sell the Deadline Guardian free, measure the switch-on"); it is not priced on this card.

**The base plan the options sit on is itself undecided.** The website publishes "Practice at CAD 199 per seat per month billed annually (249 monthly), Firm at 359 / 449, and Scale as custom" (`../../commercial/research/R1-competitors-and-pricing-2026-10-07.md` L130). The business plan's model defaults are per firm: "Solo (1 practitioner + 1 assistant) CAD 89 · Practice (up to 4 users) CAD 229 · Firm (up to 10 users) CAD 549 a month; unlimited cases and portal users", with the note "Ratified FD-18 says per seat: the founder decides" (`../../commercial/plan.md` v1.1 §3). FD-18's shape is "Per-seat tiers by firm size (Solo / Small 2–5 / Firm 6+) … plus metered add-ons for consumption rails" (Founder Decisions Log v1.5 FD-18, quoted in `provider-and-cost.md` §1). This card uses **Practice at CAD 229 per firm** (plan default) as the base in its arithmetic and shows the per-seat figure once; whichever base the founder picks, options (a) and (c) sit on top unchanged, and option (b) needs the tier list to exist.

**Two rules that bind.** "Nothing is sold outside a plan: there are no add-on prices in Release 1" (SA-04 v2.14 SA-4-44, quoted in `provider-and-cost.md` §1), and the plan's own "Not at launch: Add-ons" line with "FD-18 includes add-ons; SA-04 says none. The founder decides" (`plan.md` §3). IMP-18 (d) gives the sequence: "sell the File Builder as an allowance inside plan tiers and meter silently during the pilot" and amend SA-4-44 for launch. So option (b) is the only one that works without a rule change today; (a) and (c) need SA-4-44 amended or the founder's reading of FD-18 confirmed (IMP-19).

**The cost floor (our side).** Model cost per prepared file on a mid-tier model, list price plus 10% regional premium, CAD 1.4240 per USD: **CAD 0.37 low / 2.24 typical / 14.38 high**; on the top-tier Opus 5.5 CAD 0.75 / 4.49 / 28.76 (`provider-and-cost.md` §3.4, L173–174; every input a ranged Assumption in §3.2). Headline for planning: "CAD 0.40 low / CAD 2.20 typical / CAD 14 high" (§3.4 L180). Our other costs to serve (evaluation-set upkeep, pack upkeep, log storage, support) are "all Unknown until the pilot" (§3.5). Proposed margin rule: "model cost is under about 15% of the file price at typical volume" (`../proposals.md`, commercial-strategist decision row; `provider-and-cost.md` §4 item 5).

**The cost floor (the firm's side).** The reviewer's minutes are "the real cost, not yet measured" (`../design/workers/catalogue.md` §1.5). Twenty minutes of review costs the firm about CAD 9 at the Job Bank median for an immigration legal assistant (CAD 27.47 an hour, NOC 13111) or about CAD 14 at the licensed-consultant median (CAD 42.56, NOC 41403) (`provider-and-cost.md` §3.5, both Job Bank pages accessed 9 Oct 2026). The firm's true cost of a prepared file is our price plus those minutes.

**The value side (Assumption until W3b).** If a prepared file saves 1 / 3 / 6 hours of assistant work at CAD 27.47 plus 25% employer loading, the value is about CAD 34 / 103 / 206 per file; at the consultant median CAD 53 / 160 / 319 (`provider-and-cost.md` §3.6 L201, every input Assumption). The W3b script captures the hours (sheet rows "staff hours per file" and "licensed hours per file"); until then the corridor between cost (about CAD 2) and value (about CAD 100) is where the price sits, and "the price per prepared file is a positioning choice, not a cost-plus sum" (same line).

**Why the range is CAD 25–40 (Proposal, `provider-and-cost.md` §3.6).** CAD 25 is below the value of one hour saved even at assistant rates (CAD 34), so the firm wins if the file saves an hour; CAD 40 is about 39% of a three-hour saving (CAD 103). At CAD 25 the typical model cost is 9%, at CAD 40 it is 5.6%, both inside the 15% rule; the high case (CAD 14.38) is 58% of CAD 25, which is why spend caps (IMP-36) go with any per-file price. CAD 25 also matches the lowest published per-case price in the niche (CaseEasy's portal fill, below). Assumption: a licensed consultant reads CAD 25–40 as "a fraction of the fee I charge the client", not as "another software fee"; the script tests exactly that.

## Page 2 (show to the consultant): three options, none decided

### (a) Per prepared file

**What the firm pays.** Its plan as today, plus **CAD 25 to 40 for each file whose readiness report its named person approves** (Proposal, `provider-and-cost.md` §3.6). Nothing for a run that is abandoned or rejected (IMP-18). Deadline Guardian included at no charge (Proposal, direction review §4 item 6).

**What it gets.** For each file: documents chased and read, questionnaire values proposed with the page they came from and a confidence level, facts cross-checked, a readiness report for the named person to approve, and a kept record of who approved and when (catalogue W-01 "Job" and "New (a)"). The firm's own settings decide who may approve (AIW-D26).

**Illustration at Practice CAD 229 per firm (plan default), midpoint CAD 30 per file.** 5 files a month: 229 + 150 = CAD 379. 10 files: CAD 529. 20 files: CAD 829. (On the website's per-seat base, a 3-seat firm starts at 3 × 199 = CAD 597 before any file.) Our typical model cost at those volumes: CAD 11.20 / 22.40 / 44.80 (`provider-and-cost.md` §3.6 table).

**Strongest objection.** *"If my person still has to check everything, what am I paying CAD 30 a file for? And ten files a month doubles my software bill."* (The first half is the wedge's own stated objection: "If the firm still reviews everything, how much time does it really save?", `../design/wedge-recommendation.md`.) Second objection from the market: VisaFlo sells "Unlimited users", "Flat firm pricing", "no per-seat fee" (Vendor claim, https://visaflo.ca/crm and https://visaflo.ca/rcic-software, 9 Oct 2026, via `../research/visaflo.md` §4), so a per-unit charge can read as rationing.

**Nearest thing a competitor charges (all Vendor claim).**
- CaseEasy 360: IRCC portal auto-fill "pay-as-you-go 'from $25 per case'" (`../research/field-scan.md` §3.2; R1 L81; https://caseeasy.ca/Home/Pricing, read 7 Oct 2026).
- Visto: "Extra application CAD 29" above the plan's monthly application count (`field-scan.md` §3.3; R1 L98; https://visto.ai/pricing/, read 7 Oct 2026).
- Navisa: "US$36 a month per firm plus US$16 per case opened" (`field-scan.md` §3.9; https://www.navisa.io/, read 7 Oct 2026).
- VisaFlo: "Per-autofill charges exist" in its refund policy; amount not published (https://visaflo.ca/legal/refund-policy, effective 28 Jul 2026, read 9 Oct 2026; `visaflo.md` §4).
- None of these is a prepared file; they are a form fill, an application slot or a case opened. "In the immigration niche, nobody publishes a price per AI task" (`field-scan.md` §5, Inferred).

**The question the conversation must answer.** Will a licensed consultant pay per approved file at all, and at what number do they say no (script §5 step 3)?

---

### (b) An allowance of prepared files inside the Practice plan, with overage

**What the firm pays.** The Practice plan as today, with **10 prepared files a month included** (Proposal, `provider-and-cost.md` §3.6: "an allowance of 10 files inside Practice"). Above ten: **CAD 25 to 40 per file** (same unit and range as (a)). Solo: 3 files included; Firm: 25 files included (both **Assumption**, scaled to the user counts in `plan.md` §3; the founder sets). Whether the plan price rises when the allowance is added: **Unknown; the founder decides** (for the pilot, IMP-18 (d) proposes no change and silent metering).

**What it gets.** The same prepared file as (a), without a separate line on the invoice until the allowance is used up; the same Deadline Guardian at no charge.

**Illustration at Practice CAD 229, midpoint CAD 30 over the allowance.** 5 files: CAD 229. 10 files: CAD 229. 20 files: 229 + 10 × 30 = CAD 529. Our typical model cost inside the allowance: 10 × 2.24 = CAD 22.40, which is 9.8% of CAD 229 (`provider-and-cost.md` §3.6 table); in the high case 10 × 14.38 = CAD 144, 63% of the plan, which is why IMP-36's caps ("a warning at 80 %, a hard stop at 100 %, then the manual path") go with this option too.

**Strongest objection.** *"In a slow month I am paying for files I did not use, and the allowance tells me you plan to ration me."* And ours, not theirs: inside a flat plan the per-file revenue line disappears from the model and from the investor story (IMP-18: "Without a meter there is no per-file revenue line in the W3 model").

**Nearest thing a competitor charges (all Vendor claim).**
- Visto: plans "CAD 199 / 449 / 999 a month, sized by applications per month (10 / 25 / 55). Extra application CAD 29" (`field-scan.md` §3.3; R1 L98; https://visto.ai/pricing/, read 7 Oct 2026). This is the closest shape in the market.
- CaseEasy: form fills capped per day, "25 per day Starter, 100 per day Advanced" at CAD 99 / 199 a month plus CAD 69 per extra user (R1 L81, L100; https://caseeasy.ca/Home/Pricing, read 7 Oct 2026).
- Immiforms: "60 / 300 / 1,200 IRCC forms per month" at CAD 79 / 199 / 449 (R1 L108; currency not stated).
- VisaFlo: "case-volume pricing" per firm, prices unpublished (`visaflo.md` §4).

**The question the conversation must answer.** Is ten the right number for a firm like theirs, and do they accept paying above it, or does any cap read as rationing (script §5 step 3, "does it matter that the price is per file rather than per person")?

### (c) Per AI Worker per month

**What the firm pays.** The plan as today, plus **a flat CAD 199 a month per firm for the File Builder** (Assumption: set equal to the published per-seat price so it is one number to remember, and equal to about 8 files at CAD 25, so a firm preparing more than 8 files a month is better off here than on (a)), with a **fair-use cap of 20 prepared files a month** (Assumption, from the volume tail in `provider-and-cost.md` §4 item 6), then CAD 25 per file. Deadline Guardian: CAD 0 (Proposal). Later workers (Practice Expert, Letter Reader; `catalogue.md` §6 top five) would each carry their own monthly price: not on this card.

**What it gets.** Unlimited prepared files up to the cap, the same readiness report and approval record, one predictable line on the invoice.

**Illustration at Practice CAD 229.** 5 files: 229 + 199 = CAD 428. 10 files: CAD 428. 20 files: CAD 428. Our typical model cost: CAD 11.20 / 22.40 / 44.80; in the high case at 20 files, 20 × 14.38 = CAD 288, more than the fee, which is the margin risk of any flat price (`provider-and-cost.md` §3.6: "a firm that runs the File Builder on many long, scanned files … can exceed the subscription on model cost alone").

**Strongest objection.** *"The AI add-ons I see cost CAD 29 to 59 a month. Why is yours CAD 199? And with two files this month I paid CAD 100 a file."* And ours: a flat add-on frames the worker as a feature, not finished work; the field scan notes that "the highest valuations sit with companies that sell finished work … not seat-priced practice software" (`field-scan.md` §5, Inferred).

**Nearest thing a competitor charges (all Vendor claim unless marked).**
- Immicase: "CAD 29 a month add-on on top of CAD 49–99 per firm" (`field-scan.md` §3.9; https://www.immicase.ca/pricing/, read 7 Oct 2026).
- Clio: "AI add-on reported at US$39–59 (third party, unverified)" (`field-scan.md` §5 row B).
- DocketWise: "AI from Pro, US$100 per user" (`field-scan.md` §5 row A).
- Officio: "AI Writer" drafts letters; "No separate AI price seen"; plans CAD 69 / 99 / 129 per firm (`field-scan.md` §3.1; R1 L97; https://officio.ca/pricing.html, read 7 Oct 2026 in a browser because the site returned 403).
- VisaFlo: three agents presented as live inside "Flat firm pricing" with per-autofill charges on top; no public prices (`visaflo.md` §3.1, §4; https://visaflo.ca/, 9 Oct 2026).
- Migrawise: AI "Included; CAD 29 a month for the whole firm" (`field-scan.md` §3.9; https://migrawise.ca/, read 7 Oct 2026).

**The question the conversation must answer.** Do they want predictability more than a per-file price, and what monthly number feels right for "a worker, not a feature" (script §5 step 1: "too cheap to trust / too expensive to consider")?

## Page 3 (founder only): side by side, and what would make us wrong

|---|---|---|---|
| Unit | Approved readiness report | Plan tier, then approved report | Worker per firm per month |
| Firm pays at 5 / 10 / 20 files (Practice CAD 229 base, CAD 30 midpoint) | 379 / 529 / 829 | 229 / 229 / 529 | 428 / 428 / 428 |
| Our typical model cost at 5 / 10 / 20 files (CAD 2.24) | 11.20 / 22.40 / 44.80 | same | same |
| Model cost as share of the AI price, typical | 5.6–9% | 9.8% of the plan | 5.6% at 5 files, 22.5% at 20 |

| Strongest objection | "I still check everything"; rationing | Paying for unused files; rationing | Flat fee vs CAD 29–59 add-ons |
| Nearest competitor shape | CaseEasy $25 per case; Visto $29 extra | Visto 10/25/55 applications | Immicase CAD 29; DocketWise US$100 per user |

**Reconciling with the published price and the open decision.** Option (a) and option (c) are indifferent to whether the base is CAD 199 per seat or CAD 89 / 229 / 549 per firm; that is a point in their favour while the decision is open (`plan.md` §8 action 3, due 30 Oct). Option (b) cannot be priced until the tiers are fixed. Whatever the base, the catalogue's position stands: "revenue tracks caseload, not seats" is "the right direction when AI reduces headcount" (`wedge-recommendation.md`, "How it makes money"). The AI line in `model.xlsx` does not exist yet; W3c adds it as an input block (`provider-and-cost.md` §3.7).

**The strongest objection to the whole card.** *"If my named person still reviews every file, the saving is the clerk's hours, not mine, and I do not know what those hours are. Show me the number before you show me a price."* That is why the script asks for hours per file before it shows the card, and why the pilot measures review minutes (U8 in `provider-and-cost.md` §5) before W3c fixes anything.

**What would make us wrong.** We are wrong about the range if the ten expected prices, given unprompted, cluster below CAD 15 or above CAD 60 per file, or if most consultants expect AI to be bundled at no extra charge, as most rivals do (`field-scan.md` §5 rows A and B: the most common patterns are "bundled into a higher seat tier" and "flat monthly add-on"). We are wrong about the unit if the staff hours per file they report are under one hour, because then even CAD 25 exceeds the assistant-rate value (CAD 34 at one hour, Assumption) once their review minutes are added. We are wrong about the whole wedge if review minutes in the pilot come close to the minutes saved (the wedge's own objection), or if counsel or the College say that delegating file preparation needs more than a named person's approval (no CICC guidance on AI was found, `field-scan.md` §6 item 9; `validation-script.md` §6). And we are wrong about timing if no provider that "process[es] in Canada" (AG11-D15) is recorded, because then the AI switch stays locked, there is no prepared file to sell, and this card is moot (`provider-and-cost.md` §4 items 1 and 2).
