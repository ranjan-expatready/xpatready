## 1. In one minute

- **What this is.** The one home for money in the AI layer. Chapter 7 of the product book (`handbook/07-money-decisions-and-research.md` 0.4.1, §3) gives the short version; it will point here after its scrum 7 refresh (§9). This document replaces three working papers as the place to start (§10); they stay behind it as background.
- **How a firm pays.** The founder chose **workflow pricing**: every plan includes the AI, each plan has a monthly allowance of prepared files, and a busy month pays a clearly listed price for each extra prepared file, with warnings first ([the founder's pricing decision](ref:AIW-D111); [AI on every plan](ref:AIW-D115)). A **prepared file** is one application the assistant prepared, counted once, at the moment the case officer accepts its readiness report ([what counts](ref:AIW-D114)).
- **Per firm, not per seat.** On 14 Oct 2026 the founder chose **per firm, with users included in bands**: Solo, up to 4, up to 10, and a priced band above 10 ([the founder's per-firm decision](ref:AIW-D142)). The user band is a hard limit: a firm moves up a band before adding a user ([the founder's band-limit decision](ref:AIW-D144)). That decides the shape only; every amount stays open (§3).
- **What it costs us.** Unknown. A capable provider that processes in Canada or the US ([AIW-D147](ref:AIW-D147)) is an **Assumption** ([the founder's provider decision](ref:AIW-D139); AIW-A11 in `assumptions.md`). Only scenario arithmetic exists for the model bill; most other costs have no figure.
- **What is open with the founder.** The price of each band, the allowance and the extra-file price for each plan, set from cost to serve and pilot results ("CAD 25–40 remains illustrative", AIW-D111; AIW-D142).
- **What never happens.** No price, allowance or extra-file price is set without the founder. No time-saving figure goes to a buyer or an investor until it is measured (chapter 0 §5); no saving, accuracy or conversion figure is attached to the investor line until measured (workflow pricing §7). The AI is never sold on its own, and the charge is never called an AI price (AIW-D92, AIW-D111).

### 3.2 What never counts, and what does not exempt a file

The full list is in [AW-1-75](ref:AW-1-75) and chapter 7 §3.2. In short, nothing counts that does not end in a first accepted report on an application the assistant prepared, and a file is not billable when any AI step fell back to the manual path for our reason: a provider failure, our safety check, our platform or per-call cost cap, our emergency stop or withdrawn provider record, or a skill still in shadow (AW-1-75 C4; AW-1-154, a **Proposal** in AW-01's own tag). The firm's own stricter cap, the firm switching the AI off, and an unreadable client scan do **not** exempt a file. The split between our reasons and the firm's is the Lead's ruling M1 (and rulings (d) and (f)), written into AW-1-75; the founder may overrule it. Every file not counted keeps its reason on the run record (AW-01 §7, §10), so the firm can see a line such as "re-runs: 2, not charged" (screen wording a **Proposal**).

Two edge cases are **Unknown**: whether another holder of the review-submit key may accept the report, and whether an acceptance can be undone (`commercial/workflow-pricing-2026-10-12.md` §1.4). A file wrongly counted is refunded as a billing error with a credit note under the team's existing rules (SA-04 SA-4-49, SA-4-62; a **Proposal**, workflow pricing §1.3). This is Expat Ready's own billing of the firm; FD-11's "no credit notes" covers only firms' billing of their clients (AIW-D76).

## 5. What the AI costs Expat Ready

### 5.1 The provider

A capable provider that processes in Canada or the United States is an **Assumption** ([AIW-D139](ref:AIW-D139); AIW-A11; widened by [AIW-D147](ref:AIW-D147)): the founder chose to assume one is available, as competitors appear to, and to proceed with no test call before development. The AI layer stays provider-agnostic in design: a change of provider needs no redesign and no code change, but every AI skill gets a new version that is re-tested, released by a second person and shadowed while the older version keeps running ([AIW-D143](ref:AIW-D143), correcting AIW-D139; AW-1-149(d); AW-1-152). The one gate is the product's provider record (SA-8-66, a **Proposal** in `CN-SA-08` change 2; the AIW-D139 correction row). A switch therefore costs a full round of evaluation and shadow calls, which Expat Ready bears (§5.4). Until a provider record is published with a processing region in Canada or the US, no-training and zero-retention terms and a passed test call, every firm's AI switch stays locked (AW-1-52); that is a safety gate at run time, not a project blocker. Client data is stored in Canada; AI processing may happen in Canada or the United States only, under no-training and zero-retention terms, with client notice, for every AI call Expat Ready makes ([AIW-D147](ref:AIW-D147), replacing AIW-D52's "only in Canada"; [AIW-D74](ref:AIW-D74)). If no capable option exists, the choice returns to the founder (AIW-D52 as corrected). The current demands on a provider are AW-1-52 and the provider record SA-8-66; `commercial/provider-and-cost.md` §1 (9 Oct snapshot) is background.

**The risk now** *(re-based 14 Oct 2026, AIW-D163)*: the proposed route is Claude Sonnet 5.5 on Amazon Bedrock through the US profile from the Canadian region, with Microsoft's GPT-6 family in the US Data Zone as fallback (`requirements/technical-solution.md` §1; Sonnet 5.5 retires no sooner than 28 Sep 2027). It qualifies only on the four conditions of technical solution §1.5.1, and its text-recognition step only with the AI-services opt-out and AWS's written confirmation that it keeps and stores nothing outside Canada or the US (§1.5.2); both are **Assumptions** until AWS confirms. ~~Appendix H calls the Gemini change the highest residual risk~~: that risk (Gemini 2.5 Flash in Google's Montréal region retiring around 16 to 20 Oct 2026, from `research/ai-approach-founders-brief-2026-10-13.md` Appendix H, **Unverified**) belonged to the Canada-only rule that AIW-D147 replaced.

### 5.3 What has no figure (Unknown)

Evaluation-set upkeep, instruction versions, AI incident handling, storage of run logs and approval records, support per firm, payment processing on extra-file charges, and the long tail of pages per file (`commercial/workflow-pricing-2026-10-12.md` §4.1; value map §(e) U3). ### 5.4 Who pays for proof

Shadow, firm-check and evaluation calls are Expat Ready's cost. They never count against a firm's caps, never appear as the firm's spend and are never billed; a retry keeps the treatment of the call it retries ([who bears which cost](ref:AW-1-154) (c), a **Proposal** in AW-01's tag). A step done by hand because its skill is still in shadow is our reason, so the file is not billable (AW-1-154 (c); AW-1-75 C4). This includes the re-test and shadow of every AI skill after a change of provider ([AIW-D143](ref:AIW-D143)).

## 6. Value, what the pilot measures, and break-even

### 6.1 The value hypothesis

The value of the assistant is a hypothesis: less re-typing, fewer missed disagreements between documents, a clearer route to a review-ready file (`concept.md` §3). It is **not** a measured saving (chapter 0 §5). No customer has yet told us the problem, the saving or the price: the business plan says "No customer evidence supplied" (`../commercial/plan.md` §2, quoted in value map summary item 1).

### 6.2 What the pilot measures

The founder-consultant's own practice is pilot firm one. An AI skill's work is shown to anyone or used on real files there only after it has passed its driving test and its shadow period. Shadow itself runs on real files only after the provider test has passed, and is the first time real client data reaches the provider; clients are told in plain words before it starts (Unconfirmed: no counsel, AIW-D94), and every output keeps licensed review for the whole pilot, with no earned Auto there ([the pilot decision](ref:AIW-D112); [its reading](ref:AIW-D133); [no Auto in the pilot](ref:AIW-D145)). So the pilot's review effort is measured with a person reviewing everything; the saving from earned Auto is measured only after the pilot. The pilot measures preparation time, review effort, corrections and cost from the product's own records (AIW-D112). The measurement plan is `commercial/ai-value-map-2026-10-12.md` §(c), summarised in chapter 7 §3.5. Two gaps matter for money:

- **G1, cost per call:** tokens, pages, route and cost on every AI run log row. Specified (AW-1-55; `CN-AG-11` change 14), not built.
- **G2, review minutes:** not recorded today. The **Proposal** is to record the first and last time an approval item is opened, firm-wide only, never per person; that still gives an elapsed window, not active review time.

The comparison needs no experiment: files with no assistant, files where a skill is in shadow and files with a live skill sit side by side in the same firm and month (value map §(c)). Shadow gives quality and comparison evidence, not directly observed time savings (value map §c.6). The first numbers come from the driving tests on made-up files, Expat Ready's own use of its internal assistant, and pilot firm one; none is outside validation, which the founder dropped ([AIW-D94](ref:AIW-D94)).

### 6.3 Break-even, as thresholds

For an extra-file price to pay for itself, the net time saved per file (preparation saved minus extra review) must reach a threshold. These are thresholds to measure against, **not savings**, and are never quoted as such (value map §c.7).

| Price per extra file (**illustration**, inside the illustrative CAD 25–40 of AIW-D111) | Net assistant minutes needed | Net consultant minutes needed |
|---|---|---|
| CAD 25 | about 44 | about 28 |
| CAD 30 | about 52 | about 34 |
| CAD 40 | about 70 | about 45 |

*Basis: Job Bank medians (a published wage, accessed 9 Oct 2026; pages re-read 14 Oct 2026 by the reviewer): immigration legal assistant CAD 27.47 an hour, licensed consultant CAD 42.56 an hour, plus a 25 % employer loading (**Assumption**), giving about CAD 34.34 and 53.20 an hour (`commercial/provider-and-cost.md` §3.5, §3.6; value map §c.7).*

### 6.4 How the numbers will be set (Proposal)

The working paper proposes a rule (workflow pricing §4.3), all of it a **Proposal** under AIW-D111 and AIW-D142:

1. **Floor (cost).** The extra-file price sits above measured total cost to serve, at a stated workload percentile (the cost that most files fall under, for example nine in ten) and a margin the founder chooses. The paper's check of about CAD 16 (the typical CAD 2.41 divided by an unratified 15 % model-cost share; CAD 11.50 on the old CAD 1.72, AIW-D163) is an **illustration**, neither a measured percentile nor a total-cost floor.
2. **Ceiling (benefit).** Below the median measured net benefit per file, so a firm paying for an extra file is better off than doing it by hand. Where the price sits in that corridor is the founder's positioning choice.
3. **Allowance.** Near the measured typical month for firms on that plan. The typical month is **Unknown**: "about 10 prepared files per firm per month" is an "Assumption, no source" (`research/rules-and-ai-positioning-2026-10.md` §(c)4).
4. **Ladder.** Higher plans get a larger allowance (AIW-D115) and an extra-file price no higher than the plan below (team change T9); the band prices themselves rise with the plan (SA-4-75).
5. **Timing.** An upgrade applies at once (SA-04 SA4-D24); a raised allowance does too, once T1/T2 make the allowance a plan quantity (SA-3-43; workflow pricing §4.5). Every other change waits for each firm's first renewal at least 30 days after approval: a lowered allowance (SA-3-43), and any change to an existing firm's price, up or down, since "Price cuts follow the same path" (SA-4-48, assuming team change T9 brings the extra-file price under it). For a yearly firm that can be up to a year. So the paper proposes a launch allowance near the assumed typical month (not generous: a raise applies at once, a cut waits up to a year for a yearly firm), an extra-file price in the lower half of the corridor of items 1 and 2, a raise as soon as records show a higher typical month, and a review once each plan has three full usage months of records (workflow pricing §4.5).

### 6.5 The strongest objection

"An allowance is rationing, and you cannot tell me how long my licensed person's check takes." It stands until review minutes are measured (G2); until then no saving is claimed (workflow pricing §6). Rivals' prices are not like-for-like: one Canadian rival listed CAD 29 a month for the whole firm on 7 Oct 2026 (`../commercial/research/R1-competitors-and-pricing-2026-10-07.md` §2.3; a published price, vendor claim), and a 12 Oct note records an optional CAD 15 per AI-enabled case on top (`research/field-scan.md` notice; its primary source was deleted, so **Unverified** until re-read with a URL and date).
