**The short version.** The product rule is strict: AI processing of client data "happens only in Canada" and the Super Admin "may record only a provider and region that process in Canada" (AG11-D15). On 9 Oct 2026, by their own pages, **Anthropic's direct API, Anthropic on Amazon Bedrock, OpenAI's direct API and Azure OpenAI do not offer in-Canada model processing**; Canada appears only for storage (OpenAI), fine-tuning project regions (Azure) or cross-region routing to the US (Bedrock). The two candidates that might meet the rule are **Google's Montréal region** (Gemini models are listed there; whether Claude is served there is contradicted between Google's own pages and Anthropic's note, so *Unknown* until a test call) and **Cohere's Model Vault** (region on request to Cohere). The model cost of a prepared file is small: about **CAD 0.40 low / CAD 2.20 typical / CAD 14 high** on a mid-tier model, roughly one to seven per cent of a CAD 199 seat-month at typical volumes. The reviewer's minutes, not the model, are the real cost, and they are not yet measured (catalogue §1.5). The pricing risk is therefore not the model bill; it is that **the AI switch stays locked by the product's own rule** until a Canada-processing provider is recorded.

**What this adds up to (reading, not a new rule).** A provider qualifies only if all four hold at once: (1) model processing itself happens in a Canadian region, not just storage; (2) a written no-training term; (3) a written no-retention term (zero data retention or equivalent); (4) Expat Ready can name the provider and region in a Super Admin record that the firm switch checks. Point (1) is the strict one: AG11-D15 says "only in Canada", while SA-11 §3.6 would accept a non-Canadian sub-processor "with documented safeguards". That contradiction is already logged (`A-ai-switch-provider-residency.md` gaps; `ws2-improvements.md` IMP-7). Which wording wins is the founder's call and a counsel question (section 4).

**Code fact (not a requirement).** The reference index records that the founders' July code copy calls `https://api.anthropic.com/v1/messages` with an environment key (`A-ai-switch-provider-residency.md` §4, code rows, mail.service.ts L1721, L1753). That endpoint, by Anthropic's own data-residency page (section 2.1), runs in "global" or "us" geographies only. As it stands it would not pass AG-11-93.

## 2. Which providers can meet that today (checked 9 Oct 2026)

All prices are US dollars per million tokens, standard (not batch), as published on the page named. "In-Canada processing" means the model runs in a Canadian region; storage-only residency does not count under AG11-D15. *Vendor claim* marks a provider's statement about itself. Prices change often; the access date is the only date these numbers are good for.

### 2.1 Anthropic, direct API (api.anthropic.com)

- **Canada region for processing:** **No.** The data-residency page lists only two values for `inference_geo`: `"global"` ("Inference may run in any available geography") and `"us"` ("Inference runs only in US-based infrastructure"). Workspace geo: "Currently, `"us"` is the only available workspace geo." Under "Current limitations": "Inference geo: Only `"us"` and `"global"` are available." Canada does not appear on the page. https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/data-residency (9 Oct 2026).
- **Training:** *Vendor claim.* "By default, we will not use your inputs or outputs from our commercial products" to train models; training happens only if the customer opts in or reports feedback. https://privacy.claude.com/en/articles/7996868-is-my-data-used-for-model-training (9 Oct 2026). Retention page: "Retained data is never used for model training without your express permission."
- **Retention:** *Vendor claim.* "Conversation content (your prompts and Claude's outputs) is not retained by default; the exception is Covered Models, which require 30-day retention." Zero data retention: "Under a ZDR arrangement, Anthropic does not store customer prompts or responses at rest after the API response is returned. To request ZDR for your organization, contact the Anthropic sales team." Covered Models (Claude Fable 5.1, Mythos 5.1, Fable 5, Mythos 5) "require 30-day data retention; ZDR is therefore not available for any of them unless expressly authorized by Anthropic." https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/manage-claude/api-and-data-retention (9 Oct 2026).
- **Prices** (https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/about-claude/pricing, 9 Oct 2026): mid-tier **Claude Sonnet 5.5: $2 input / $10 output**; top-tier **Claude Opus 5.5: $4 / $20** (Claude Fable 5.1, the top of the range, is $10 / $50 but is a Covered Model with mandatory 30-day retention, so it fails the no-retention demand). Cheapest: Claude Haiku 5.5 $0.10 / $0.50 for prompts up to 100,000 tokens. US-only inference costs "1.1x the standard rate across all token pricing categories". Cache reads are 0.05x input on Sonnet 5.5 and Opus 5.5; batch is 50% off. "As a rough estimate, 1 token is approximately 4 characters or 0.75 words in English."
- **Verdict against section 1:** fails (1); meets (2) and (3) with a ZDR arrangement on non-Covered models; (4) is ours to build.

### 2.2 Anthropic via Amazon Bedrock

- **Canada region for processing:** **No in-region processing.** Anthropic's Bedrock page lists `ca-central-1` Canada (Central) with endpoint types "Global, US" and `ca-west-1` Canada West with "Global" only; the "In-region only" marker is on Tokyo, Melbourne, Stockholm, Ireland and four US regions, not Canada. "Regional: the endpoint resolves to the single AWS region you specify, for data-residency requirements. Regional endpoints carry a 10% pricing premium over global endpoints." https://platform.claude.com/docs/en/build-with-claude/claude-in-amazon-bedrock (9 Oct 2026). AWS's own regional-availability table shows for ca-central-1: Claude Haiku 5.5, Sonnet 5.5, Opus 5.5 and Fable 5.1 **In-Region: not supported; Geo: supported; Global: supported**. https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/models-region-compatibility.html (9 Oct 2026; first 100,000 characters read; Cohere, Meta and Mistral rows fall beyond that and were not read). A search-result snippet from the Opus 4.6 model card says a Canada source region's "Geo" inference can route to us-east-1, us-east-2 and us-west-2 (https://docs.aws.eu/bedrock/latest/userguide/model-card-anthropic-claude-opus-4-6.html, snippet only, 9 Oct 2026). In plain words: a request sent from Canada is processed in the United States.
- **Training and retention:** *Vendor claim.* "Amazon Bedrock uses a zero data retention (ZDR) data security model. This means that by default, Amazon Bedrock does not store model inputs or outputs." Exceptions: "For Anthropic Claude Fable 5 and Claude Fable 5.1, all traffic will be retained for up to 30 days for automated offline abuse detection." "Retained inputs and outputs are stored and processed by AWS and are not shared with third-party model providers." https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/abuse-detection.html (9 Oct 2026). Data-protection page: "model providers … don't have access to Amazon Bedrock logs or to customer prompts and completions." https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/data-protection.html (9 Oct 2026). Retention mode `none`: "Zero data retention. No request or response data is written to durable storage by AWS or shared with the model provider." https://docs.aws.amazon.com/bedrock/latest/userguide/data-retention.html (9 Oct 2026). AWS knowledge centre (search result, 9 Oct 2026): Bedrock "doesn't store customer input data and model output data, share the data with third-party model providers, or use the data to train models" (https://www.repost.aws/knowledge-center/amazon-bedrock-model-data-use).
- **Prices:** The Bedrock pricing page is built dynamically; the text fetched on 9 Oct 2026 showed only old example models (Claude Instant, Claude 2.1) and no current Claude, Cohere or Nova on-demand rates, so Bedrock list prices are **Unknown from the page** (https://aws.amazon.com/bedrock/pricing/). Anthropic's pricing page says "Partner-operated platforms (Bedrock and Google Cloud) have independent regional pricing" and regional endpoints add 10%.
- **Verdict:** fails (1) today (Canada routes to the US); meets (2) and (3) by default on non-Fable models.

### 2.4 OpenAI, direct API

- **Canada region for processing:** **No.** The data-controls page lists Canada (`ca.api.openai.com`) with regional **storage** available and regional **processing** not available; "If your selected Region does not support regional processing" OpenAI may process and temporarily store data outside the region. Non-US regions require approval for abuse-monitoring controls. https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/your-data (9 Oct 2026). OpenAI's own announcement confirms Canada was added to "at-rest data residency" (https://openai.com/index/expanding-data-residency-access-to-business-customers-worldwide/, 9 Oct 2026).
- **Training:** *Vendor claim.* "data sent to the OpenAI API is not used to train or improve OpenAI models" unless the customer opts in (same page).
- **Retention:** *Vendor claim.* "By default, abuse monitoring logs are generated for all API feature usage and retained for up to 30 days". Zero Data Retention: "Currently, these controls are subject to prior approval by OpenAI and acceptance of additional requirements." (same page).
- **Prices** (https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing, 9 Oct 2026): top-tier **gpt-6-astra: $10 / $50**; mid-tier **gpt-6-sol: $2 / $10** (gpt-6.1-sol also $2 / $10); cheapest gpt-6-luna $0.10 / $0.50. "Regional processing (data residency) endpoints are charged a 10% uplift for models released on or after March 5, 2026."
- **Verdict:** fails (1); meets (2); (3) only with approved ZDR.

## 4. Risks for the pricing decision

1. **The product's own gate may stay shut.** AG-11-93 locks the firm AI switch until a provider that "process[es] in Canada" is recorded. On 9 Oct 2026, by their own pages, none of Anthropic direct, Anthropic on Bedrock, OpenAI direct or Azure OpenAI offers that for current models; Google's Montréal region lists Gemini and perhaps Claude; Cohere offers a dedicated deployment on request. If the founders want a Claude-class model and Canada-only processing, the realistic routes are Google Montréal (to be confirmed by a test call) or a dedicated Cohere deployment, and both change the cost shape (regional premium, or per-instance pricing). **Decision for the founder and the co-founder (Proposal, logged in `../proposals.md`):** either keep "only in Canada" and choose from the routes that can meet it, or adopt SA-11 §3.6's wording ("Canadian-resident or explicitly approved with documented safeguards") and record a US-region provider with safeguards. Either way: *confirm with counsel*.

2. **If only a US region is available.** Then by AG-11-93 the switch stays LOCKED_OFF, every AI Worker in the catalogue is unavailable to every firm, and Release 1 ships the manual paths only (AG-11-71 guarantees they work). The commercial effect: no per-file revenue line, no AI differentiation in the founding-firm offer, and the investor story rests on a product fact that is false until a provider is recorded. If instead AG11-D15 is widened to allow a US region with safeguards, three things follow: the data-residency declaration (SA-8-31) shows "Non-Canadian with confirmed safeguard" for AI processing; SA-11-21's compliance warning logic must treat that as acceptable; and the client-notice wording the product must supply (AG-11-93) must say that AI processing happens in the United States. The US-only routes also cost 10% more than global routing (Anthropic, OpenAI).

3. **Quebec Law 25 points, each "confirm with counsel".** (a) Section 17 of Quebec's private-sector privacy act requires a privacy impact assessment before personal information is communicated outside Quebec, weighing "the sensitivity of the information", "the purposes for which it is to be used", "the protective measures, including the contractual measures" and "the legal framework applicable in the State in which the information will be communicated", and "a written contract with the entity receiving the information" (BLG, 7 May 2024, https://www.blg.com/en/insights/2022/12/cross-border-transfers-of-personal-information-outside-quebec, 9 Oct 2026). A practitioner summary says this applies "including to another Canadian province" (DataGuidance, https://www.dataguidance.com/opinion/quebec-overview-law-25-part-two, 9 Oct 2026). In plain words: for a Quebec firm, even processing in Toronto may need the assessment, and processing in the US certainly does. Whether an AI sub-processor counts as "communication" and what the assessment must contain: *confirm with counsel*. (b) Law 25 also carries duties on decisions made "exclusively" by automated processing; IMP-11 and FD-20 already hold that question; a human-approved AI draft is probably not such a decision, but that is *Unknown* and for counsel. (c) SA-11 v2.7 already routes "any provincial overlay (e.g., Quebec Law 25)" to counsel (§13 item 2, L623).

4. **PIPEDA points, each "confirm with counsel".** The federal regulator's guideline on transfers for processing says "PIPEDA does not distinguish between domestic and international transfers of data", that "The transferring organization is accountable for the information" and must use "contractual or other means to provide a comparable level of protection", and that individuals should be told their information may be "processed in a foreign country" and "may be accessible to law enforcement and national security authorities of that jurisdiction" (https://www.priv.gc.ca/en/privacy-topics/airports-and-borders/gl_dab_090127/, 9 Oct 2026). A 2019 consultation proposed requiring consent for such transfers and was withdrawn, leaving the 2009 position in force (Torys, https://www.torys.com/insights/publications/2019/09/opc-not-changing-guidelines-on-transfers-for-processing, 9 Oct 2026). Consequence for the product: the client-notice wording AG-11-93 promises is the PIPEDA transparency step and must name the country of processing; the contract with the provider is the accountability step (no-training, no-retention, region). Who is controller and who is processor between the firm and Expat Ready (AG-16 §3.9 says "ExpatReady is the data processor") is a counsel question already flagged in the reference index.

5. **A model price change.** Three kinds, with the arithmetic: (a) *List price.* Prices on 9 Oct 2026 range from $0.10 / $0.50 to $10 / $50 for a million tokens; a move from Sonnet 5.5 to Opus 5.5 doubles the typical file from CAD 2.24 to CAD 4.49; a move to the dearest list price takes it to CAD 11.22. (b) *Hidden price.* Anthropic's pricing page states that Claude 4.7 and later models use a tokenizer that "produces approximately 30% more tokens for the same text"; the same prompt therefore costs about 30% more without any price change. Google's Gemini 3.8 Flash price doubles on 1 January 2027 by the page's own words. (c) *Retirement.* Models are deprecated on a schedule (Anthropic lists "retired" models on its pricing page); each retirement forces a re-run of the evaluation set (IMP-29) and a prompt-version change (AG-11-72 logs the version). Mitigations already in the design: spend caps per firm and per person (IMP-36), a provider rail in SA-08 so a switch is configuration (IMP-6), and the manual fallback (AG-11-71). **Proposal:** price the File Builder so that model cost is under 15% of the file price at typical volume (CAD 2.24 against CAD 25 is 9%), leaving room for a doubling of model prices without a customer price change.

6. **Volume tail and the co-pilot.** The high case (CAD 14 mid-tier, CAD 29 on Opus) comes mostly from long scanned documents (480 page calls) and heavy co-pilot use (360,000 input tokens). Without caps a few firms could cost more than they pay. IMP-36's caps and a per-file allowance are the answer; this estimate gives the numbers to set them.

7. **Metering is not allowed yet.** SA-4-44 ("no add-on prices in Release 1") blocks a per-file charge; FD-18 permits "metered add-ons for consumption rails". IMP-18 and IMP-19 already propose the sequence (allowance inside plans for the pilot, one metered add-on at launch). Nothing new is added here; the cost numbers above are the evidence that the add-on is affordable to offer.

## 6. Five-line summary

1. The requirements demand, in exact words, AI processing "only in Canada", a recorded provider and region, a no-training and no-retention agreement, and one audited firm switch (AG11-D13, D15; AG-11-73, -93; AG-16-35).
2. On 9 Oct 2026, by their own pages, Anthropic direct, Anthropic on Bedrock, OpenAI direct and Azure OpenAI do not process in Canada; Gemini on Google's Montréal region is listed; Claude in Montréal is contradicted between Google's pages (Unknown); Cohere offers a Canadian deployment only on request.

4. Against a CAD 199 seat-month (website) or CAD 229 Practice firm-month (plan default), the typical model bill is 5–20% at 5–20 files a month; the tail (long scanned files, heavy co-pilot use) needs the caps and per-file unit already proposed (IMP-18, IMP-36).
5. The pricing risk is not the model bill but the gate: until a Canada-processing provider is recorded, the AI switch stays locked by the product's own rule; the founder and the co-founder must choose between keeping "only in Canada" and widening it to "with safeguards", and counsel must read Law 25 section 17 and the PIPEDA transfer guideline for the client notice.
