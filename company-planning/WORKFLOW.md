## Purpose

Help Ranjan and the founder agree how to win and retain initial customers, run the small commercial effort, and fund the next meaningful milestone. They must be able to explain the plan to the team and investors, change assumptions, and understand the effect without learning our process.

## Deliver only what serves that purpose

- A short business plan: first buyer, costly problem, reason to switch, offer, initial sales and marketing approach, practical referral test, onboarding/support, responsibilities, funding choices, and next actions with owners. Include the strongest objection to the recommendation and what would change it. Reuse verified partner/investor contact routes only where relevant to a next action.
- An editable monthly money model: customers, recurring revenue, collections, costs, cash and any funding gap. Start with available cash and product commitments; unknown facts stay visibly unknown. Label illustrative assumptions. Keep everyday inputs together. Add a schedule only where necessary for a real decision or retained offer.

The action list belongs in the plan. A deck, formal CRM, multiple trackers, detailed investor reporting and additional scenarios are created when there is a specific use. There is no arbitrary page, input, tab or formula quota. Simplicity must reduce work, not merely conceal it.

## Quality without unnecessary machinery

1. Challenge both models and the founder with evidence and alternatives. Agreement is not proof. Forecasts explore assumptions; they do not establish customer demand, staffing needs or fundability.
2. Keep one source for editable numbers. Show meaningful, traceable calculations. Distinguish recurring run rate, revenue and cash. Handle annual prepayments, refunds and referral rewards correctly when retained; do not create an accounting platform.
3. Compare what happens without new investment before assuming a raise. Explain what funding buys and when cash is needed. Hiring recommendations require an actual workload/skill/cost argument, not an automatic rule built on guessed conversion rates.
4. Reuse research and founder answers. Research only a gap that changes a current decision. Distinguish facts, proposals and unknowns. Keep sources close enough to verify, without making the founder decode IDs.
5. Preserve product readiness, customer-data protection, immigration-content upkeep, support and applicable legal checks. Link product-owned evidence. Simpler planning does not authorize weaker safeguards or unapproved product changes.
6. Founder updates must be straightforward. Do not require scripts for everyday spreadsheet changes. Avoid repeating forecasts in prose; date any fixed exhibit and explain its refresh honestly.
